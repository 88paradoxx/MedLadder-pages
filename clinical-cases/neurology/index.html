What these neurology cases test

Neurology vignettes are about timing and localisation. You decide whether a stroke patient is inside the thrombolysis window, bring the blood pressure below 185/110 before alteplase, reverse anticoagulation in an intracerebral bleed, and work through status epilepticus drug by drug. Other cases cover Guillain–Barré syndrome, myasthenic crisis, meningitis, cerebral venous thrombosis and brain death testing. Advanced cases add hyponatraemia after subarachnoid haemorrhage, demyelination and the neurocutaneous syndromes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Neurology clinical cases are there on MedLadder?

MedLadder has 35 Neurology clinical cases: 13 Core and 22 Advanced. 8 of them are free to play, and the remaining 27 need MedLadder Pro.

Are Neurology clinical cases free?

8 Neurology cases are free to play without a subscription. The other 27 cases, including all Advanced cases beyond the free set, need MedLadder Pro, which also unlocks the full QBank and past papers.

Which neurology topics are most tested in NEET PG?

Stroke and TIA, seizures, intracranial haemorrhage, meningitis, Guillain–Barré syndrome, myasthenia gravis and raised intracranial pressure. Questions usually test what to do first, which is how these cases are written.

Do the cases cover the thrombolysis window and contraindications?

Yes. Several stroke cases ask whether to give alteplase, based on time from onset, blood pressure, anticoagulation and imaging, and the explanations set out the cut-offs you need for the exam.

Are there advanced neurology cases for INI-CET and NEET SS?

Yes. The Advanced cases cover cerebral salt wasting versus SIADH after a bleed, demyelinating disease, motor neuron disease and the less common emergencies that INI-CET likes to test.

Which exams are these Neurology clinical cases designed for?

NEET PG, INI-CET and FMGE first of all - and they work just as well for NEET SS and USMLE-style practice. Every one of these exams now tests you with patient vignettes instead of one-line facts, so each case here is built the same way: a patient story that unfolds step by step, and you commit to a diagnosis and a plan before the next clue appears.

Are these cases based on the new exam pattern?

Yes. Look at recent NEET PG, INI-CET, FMGE and NEET SS papers - and USMLE-style questions - and you will see the shift: fewer one-liners, more real patients. The question gives you a story and asks what you would do next. That is exactly how every case on this page works: history, examination, investigations and management, revealed one step at a time.

How do I use these cases to prepare for NEET PG?

Take one case at a time and decide your answer before you reveal the next step - that is how the exam will test you. Start with the Core cases for the presentations you must know, then move to Advanced when you want the harder differentials. The 8 free cases let you try the format before you decide on Pro.

Do the cases explain the reasoning, or just give the answer?

They show the reasoning. You get the history, examination, investigations and management step by step, with the key learning point at the end - so you understand why each decision was made, not just what the answer was.
