What these psychiatry cases test

Psychiatry vignettes ask you to name the disorder and choose what to do next. You treat delirium tremens with benzodiazepines, manage delirium without drugs first, recognise neuroleptic malignant syndrome and lithium toxicity, decide when a suicidal patient needs admission, and choose ECT for psychotic depression with refusal to eat or drink. A large set covers mental health law, capacity and confidentiality, along with opioid overdose and stimulant intoxication. Advanced cases include shared psychotic disorder and treatment-resistant illness.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Psychiatry clinical cases are there on MedLadder?

MedLadder has 57 Psychiatry clinical cases: 34 Core and 23 Advanced. 8 of them are free to play, and the remaining 49 need MedLadder Pro.

Are Psychiatry clinical cases free?

8 Psychiatry cases are free to play without a subscription. The other 49 cases, including all Advanced cases beyond the free set, need MedLadder Pro, which also unlocks the full QBank and past papers.

Which psychiatry topics are most tested in NEET PG?

Schizophrenia, mood disorders, substance use and withdrawal, anxiety disorders, delirium and dementia, and the side effects of psychiatric drugs. These cases focus on telling apart disorders that look alike, and on management.

Do the cases cover the Mental Healthcare Act and legal psychiatry?

Yes. Several cases cover capacity, involuntary admission, confidentiality and forensic questions, which come up often and are easy marks once the rules are clear.

Are psychiatric drug side effects included?

Yes. Cases cover neuroleptic malignant syndrome, serotonin syndrome, lithium toxicity and the metabolic effects of antipsychotics, with what to do in each.

Are these psychiatry cases useful for MBBS exams, NEET SS and USMLE?

Yes. MBBS final-year and university exams use the same case-based format, so the Core cases double as viva and long-case practice. For NEET SS (Psychiatry group, including DM Child & Adolescent and DM Addiction Psychiatry), the Advanced cases go to super-specialty depth. For USMLE Step 2 CK, every case is a next-best-step vignette in the same style. Where Indian guidelines differ from US practice, the explanation says so.

Which exams are these Psychiatry clinical cases designed for?

NEET PG, INI-CET and FMGE first of all - and they work just as well for NEET SS and USMLE-style practice. Every one of these exams now tests you with patient vignettes instead of one-line facts, so each case here is built the same way: a patient story that unfolds step by step, and you commit to a diagnosis and a plan before the next clue appears.

Are these cases based on the new exam pattern?

Yes. Look at recent NEET PG, INI-CET, FMGE and NEET SS papers - and USMLE-style questions - and you will see the shift: fewer one-liners, more real patients. The question gives you a story and asks what you would do next. That is exactly how every case on this page works: history, examination, investigations and management, revealed one step at a time.

How do I use these cases to prepare for NEET PG?

Take one case at a time and decide your answer before you reveal the next step - that is how the exam will test you. Start with the Core cases for the presentations you must know, then move to Advanced when you want the harder differentials. The 8 free cases let you try the format before you decide on Pro.

Do the cases explain the reasoning, or just give the answer?

They show the reasoning. You get the history, examination, investigations and management step by step, with the key learning point at the end - so you understand why each decision was made, not just what the answer was.
