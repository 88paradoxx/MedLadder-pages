What these renal and electrolyte cases test

Renal cases are built around numbers you have to act on. You give calcium gluconate first when hyperkalaemia changes the ECG, correct sodium without causing osmotic demyelination, work out an anion gap and decide whether the kidney injury is pre-renal, renal or obstructive. Further cases cover nephrotic and nephritic syndromes, stones, urinary infection and dialysis. Advanced cases include contrast nephropathy, calciphylaxis, renal vein thrombosis, transplant rejection and anti-GBM disease.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Renal & Electrolytes clinical cases are there on MedLadder?

MedLadder has 30 Renal & Electrolytes clinical cases: 11 Core and 19 Advanced. 8 of them are free to play, and the remaining 22 need MedLadder Pro.

Are Renal & Electrolytes clinical cases free?

8 Renal & Electrolytes cases are free to play without a subscription. The other 22 cases, including all Advanced cases beyond the free set, need MedLadder Pro, which also unlocks the full QBank and past papers.

Which renal topics are most tested in NEET PG?

Acute kidney injury, potassium and sodium disorders, acid–base problems, nephrotic and nephritic syndromes, and urinary tract infection. Electrolyte emergencies are especially common because they test management as well as diagnosis.

Do the cases teach acid–base interpretation?

Yes. Cases give you the gas and the electrolytes, and the explanation goes through the anion gap and compensation step by step, the same approach the exam expects.

Are there Advanced cases for INI-CET nephrology?

Yes. They cover transplant medicine, glomerular disease with pulmonary haemorrhage, renal tubular acidosis and the drug-related kidney injuries that INI-CET favours.

Are these nephrology cases useful for MBBS exams, NEET SS and USMLE?

Yes. MBBS final-year and university exams use the same case-based format, so the Core cases double as viva and long-case practice. For NEET SS (Medicine group, including DM Nephrology), the Advanced cases go to super-specialty depth. For USMLE Step 2 CK, every case is a next-best-step vignette in the same style. Where Indian guidelines differ from US practice, the explanation says so.

Which exams are these Renal & Electrolytes clinical cases designed for?

NEET PG, INI-CET and FMGE first of all - and they work just as well for NEET SS and USMLE-style practice. Every one of these exams now tests you with patient vignettes instead of one-line facts, so each case here is built the same way: a patient story that unfolds step by step, and you commit to a diagnosis and a plan before the next clue appears.

Are these cases based on the new exam pattern?

Yes. Look at recent NEET PG, INI-CET, FMGE and NEET SS papers - and USMLE-style questions - and you will see the shift: fewer one-liners, more real patients. The question gives you a story and asks what you would do next. That is exactly how every case on this page works: history, examination, investigations and management, revealed one step at a time.

How do I use these cases to prepare for NEET PG?

Take one case at a time and decide your answer before you reveal the next step - that is how the exam will test you. Start with the Core cases for the presentations you must know, then move to Advanced when you want the harder differentials. The 8 free cases let you try the format before you decide on Pro.

Do the cases explain the reasoning, or just give the answer?

They show the reasoning. You get the history, examination, investigations and management step by step, with the key learning point at the end - so you understand why each decision was made, not just what the answer was.
