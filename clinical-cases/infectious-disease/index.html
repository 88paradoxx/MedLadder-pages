What these infectious disease cases test

These cases are written for Indian practice. You manage septic shock past the first litre of fluid, treat severe falciparum and vivax malaria (including in pregnancy), manage the critical phase of dengue, recognise the eschar of scrub typhus and the Jarisch–Herxheimer reaction in leptospirosis, and spot typhoid perforation in the third week of fever. HIV cases cover cryptococcal meningitis and Pneumocystis pneumonia. Other cases cover tetanus, leprosy, zoonoses and the national immunisation schedule.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Infectious Disease clinical cases are there on MedLadder?

MedLadder has 31 Infectious Disease clinical cases: 8 Core and 23 Advanced. 8 of them are free to play, and the remaining 23 need MedLadder Pro.

Are Infectious Disease clinical cases free?

8 Infectious Disease cases are free to play without a subscription. The other 23 cases, including all Advanced cases beyond the free set, need MedLadder Pro, which also unlocks the full QBank and past papers.

Which infectious diseases are most tested in NEET PG and FMGE?

Malaria, dengue, typhoid, tuberculosis, HIV and its opportunistic infections, sepsis and meningitis. FMGE in particular tests tropical infections, so these cases follow Indian treatment guidelines.

Do the cases follow Indian guidelines?

Yes, where it matters. Malaria, dengue fluid management and the immunisation schedule follow the national programmes, and the explanations say so when international guidance differs.

Are there cases on antibiotic choice and resistance?

Yes. They cover empirical antibiotics for sepsis and meningitis, hospital-acquired and resistant infections, and the antimicrobial facts the exam keeps coming back to.

Are these infectious diseases cases useful for MBBS exams, NEET SS and USMLE?

Yes. MBBS final-year and university exams use the same case-based format, so the Core cases double as viva and long-case practice. For NEET SS (Medicine group, including DM Infectious Diseases), the Advanced cases go to super-specialty depth. For USMLE Step 2 CK, every case is a next-best-step vignette in the same style. Where Indian guidelines differ from US practice, the explanation says so.

Which exams are these Infectious Disease clinical cases designed for?

NEET PG, INI-CET and FMGE first of all - and they work just as well for NEET SS and USMLE-style practice. Every one of these exams now tests you with patient vignettes instead of one-line facts, so each case here is built the same way: a patient story that unfolds step by step, and you commit to a diagnosis and a plan before the next clue appears.

Are these cases based on the new exam pattern?

Yes. Look at recent NEET PG, INI-CET, FMGE and NEET SS papers - and USMLE-style questions - and you will see the shift: fewer one-liners, more real patients. The question gives you a story and asks what you would do next. That is exactly how every case on this page works: history, examination, investigations and management, revealed one step at a time.

How do I use these cases to prepare for NEET PG?

Take one case at a time and decide your answer before you reveal the next step - that is how the exam will test you. Start with the Core cases for the presentations you must know, then move to Advanced when you want the harder differentials. The 8 free cases let you try the format before you decide on Pro.

Do the cases explain the reasoning, or just give the answer?

They show the reasoning. You get the history, examination, investigations and management step by step, with the key learning point at the end - so you understand why each decision was made, not just what the answer was.
