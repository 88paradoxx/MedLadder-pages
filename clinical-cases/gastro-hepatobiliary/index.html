What these GI and liver cases test

GI and liver cases mostly test the order of priorities. In an upper GI bleed you protect the airway, resuscitate, start octreotide and antibiotics in cirrhosis, and only then scope. You give aggressive fluids early in pancreatitis, tap the ascites of a febrile cirrhotic patient to exclude SBP, and pick conservative treatment or the operating theatre in bowel obstruction. Advanced cases cover Barrett surveillance, false-negative H. pylori tests, drug-induced liver injury and hepatobiliary cancers.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Gastro & Hepatobiliary clinical cases are there on MedLadder?

MedLadder has 50 Gastro & Hepatobiliary clinical cases: 27 Core and 23 Advanced. 8 of them are free to play, and the remaining 42 need MedLadder Pro.

Are Gastro & Hepatobiliary clinical cases free?

8 Gastro & Hepatobiliary cases are free to play without a subscription. The other 42 cases, including all Advanced cases beyond the free set, need MedLadder Pro, which also unlocks the full QBank and past papers.

Which GI topics are most important for NEET PG?

Upper GI bleeding, acute pancreatitis, cirrhosis and its complications, the acute abdomen, inflammatory bowel disease and viral hepatitis. These cases cover both the medicine and the surgical decisions.

Do the cases include surgical gastroenterology?

Yes. Bowel obstruction, mesenteric ischaemia, perforation, cholecystitis and cholangitis appear alongside the medical topics, so you practise deciding when a patient needs theatre.

How do these cases help with liver disease questions?

They follow a cirrhotic patient through the complications the exam tests most: variceal bleeding, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, hepatorenal syndrome and encephalopathy, each with the first-line management explained.

Are these gastroenterology cases useful for MBBS exams, NEET SS and USMLE?

Yes. MBBS final-year and university exams use the same case-based format, so the Core cases double as viva and long-case practice. For NEET SS (Medicine and Surgery group, including DM Gastroenterology and DM Hepatology), the Advanced cases go to super-specialty depth. For USMLE Step 2 CK, every case is a next-best-step vignette in the same style. Where Indian guidelines differ from US practice, the explanation says so.

Which exams are these Gastro & Hepatobiliary clinical cases designed for?

NEET PG, INI-CET and FMGE first of all - and they work just as well for NEET SS and USMLE-style practice. Every one of these exams now tests you with patient vignettes instead of one-line facts, so each case here is built the same way: a patient story that unfolds step by step, and you commit to a diagnosis and a plan before the next clue appears.

Are these cases based on the new exam pattern?

Yes. Look at recent NEET PG, INI-CET, FMGE and NEET SS papers - and USMLE-style questions - and you will see the shift: fewer one-liners, more real patients. The question gives you a story and asks what you would do next. That is exactly how every case on this page works: history, examination, investigations and management, revealed one step at a time.

How do I use these cases to prepare for NEET PG?

Take one case at a time and decide your answer before you reveal the next step - that is how the exam will test you. Start with the Core cases for the presentations you must know, then move to Advanced when you want the harder differentials. The 8 free cases let you try the format before you decide on Pro.

Do the cases explain the reasoning, or just give the answer?

They show the reasoning. You get the history, examination, investigations and management step by step, with the key learning point at the end - so you understand why each decision was made, not just what the answer was.
