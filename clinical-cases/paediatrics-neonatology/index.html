What these paediatric cases test

Paediatric cases are about doses, thresholds and age. You resuscitate a newborn who is not breathing, start ampicillin and gentamicin for neonatal sepsis, work out which jaundiced baby needs urgent investigation, give a 20 mL/kg bolus to a shocked infant, and remove an inhaled foreign body by rigid bronchoscopy. Others cover milestones, immunisation, severe acute malnutrition, Kawasaki disease and intussusception. Advanced cases include hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy, congenital diaphragmatic hernia and ROP screening criteria.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Paediatrics & Neonatology clinical cases are there on MedLadder?

MedLadder has 59 Paediatrics & Neonatology clinical cases: 30 Core and 29 Advanced. 8 of them are free to play, and the remaining 51 need MedLadder Pro.

Are Paediatrics & Neonatology clinical cases free?

8 Paediatrics & Neonatology cases are free to play without a subscription. The other 51 cases, including all Advanced cases beyond the free set, need MedLadder Pro, which also unlocks the full QBank and past papers.

Which paediatric topics are most tested in NEET PG?

Neonatology (resuscitation, jaundice and sepsis), dehydration, respiratory infections, developmental milestones, immunisation, malnutrition and paediatric emergencies. The cases follow the IAP and national programme guidance the exam uses.

Is there a separate set of neonatology cases?

Neonatology is built in. Resuscitation, prematurity, respiratory distress, jaundice, hypoglycaemia and sepsis in the newborn each have their own cases, and many of the Advanced cases are neonatal.

Do the cases cover paediatric surgery?

Yes. Intussusception, pyloric stenosis, foreign-body aspiration and the other common surgical emergencies in children are covered.

Which exams are these Paediatrics & Neonatology clinical cases designed for?

NEET PG, INI-CET and FMGE first of all - and they work just as well for NEET SS and USMLE-style practice. Every one of these exams now tests you with patient vignettes instead of one-line facts, so each case here is built the same way: a patient story that unfolds step by step, and you commit to a diagnosis and a plan before the next clue appears.

Are these cases based on the new exam pattern?

Yes. Look at recent NEET PG, INI-CET, FMGE and NEET SS papers - and USMLE-style questions - and you will see the shift: fewer one-liners, more real patients. The question gives you a story and asks what you would do next. That is exactly how every case on this page works: history, examination, investigations and management, revealed one step at a time.

How do I use these cases to prepare for NEET PG?

Take one case at a time and decide your answer before you reveal the next step - that is how the exam will test you. Start with the Core cases for the presentations you must know, then move to Advanced when you want the harder differentials. The 8 free cases let you try the format before you decide on Pro.

Do the cases explain the reasoning, or just give the answer?

They show the reasoning. You get the history, examination, investigations and management step by step, with the key learning point at the end - so you understand why each decision was made, not just what the answer was.
