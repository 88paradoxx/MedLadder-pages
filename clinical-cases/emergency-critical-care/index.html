What these emergency and ICU cases test

Emergency cases test the primary survey and the decisions made in the first ten minutes. You send an unstable patient with a positive FAST scan to theatre, relieve tamponade, treat organophosphate poisoning and snake envenomation, give intramuscular adrenaline for anaphylaxis, and intubate early when a burn has damaged the airway. ICU cases cover sedation, fluid responsiveness and ventilator alarms. Advanced cases include cardiac arrest in pregnancy, choosing a paralytic in crush injury, and raised intracranial pressure after head injury.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many Emergency & Critical Care clinical cases are there on MedLadder?

MedLadder has 35 Emergency & Critical Care clinical cases: 13 Core and 22 Advanced. 8 of them are free to play, and the remaining 27 need MedLadder Pro.

Are Emergency & Critical Care clinical cases free?

8 Emergency & Critical Care cases are free to play without a subscription. The other 27 cases, including all Advanced cases beyond the free set, need MedLadder Pro, which also unlocks the full QBank and past papers.

Which emergency medicine topics are most tested?

Trauma (ATLS), shock, poisoning, envenomation, anaphylaxis, burns, cardiac arrest and airway management. Most questions ask what to do first, and these cases are built the same way.

Do the cases cover Indian toxicology such as organophosphate and snake bite?

Yes. Organophosphate poisoning, snake envenomation and drug overdose are covered, with antidotes and supportive care laid out as Indian exams test them.

Are ICU and ventilator questions included?

Yes. Cases cover mechanical ventilation, including reading peak and plateau pressures, as well as sepsis resuscitation, sedation and organ support. That makes them useful for INI-CET and NEET SS anaesthesia and critical care.

Are these emergency medicine and critical care cases useful for MBBS exams, NEET SS and USMLE?

Yes. MBBS final-year and university exams use the same case-based format, so the Core cases double as viva and long-case practice. For NEET SS (Anaesthesiology and Medicine group, including DM Critical Care Medicine), the Advanced cases go to super-specialty depth. For USMLE Step 2 CK, every case is a next-best-step vignette in the same style. Where Indian guidelines differ from US practice, the explanation says so.

Which exams are these Emergency & Critical Care clinical cases designed for?

NEET PG, INI-CET and FMGE first of all - and they work just as well for NEET SS and USMLE-style practice. Every one of these exams now tests you with patient vignettes instead of one-line facts, so each case here is built the same way: a patient story that unfolds step by step, and you commit to a diagnosis and a plan before the next clue appears.

Are these cases based on the new exam pattern?

Yes. Look at recent NEET PG, INI-CET, FMGE and NEET SS papers - and USMLE-style questions - and you will see the shift: fewer one-liners, more real patients. The question gives you a story and asks what you would do next. That is exactly how every case on this page works: history, examination, investigations and management, revealed one step at a time.

How do I use these cases to prepare for NEET PG?

Take one case at a time and decide your answer before you reveal the next step - that is how the exam will test you. Start with the Core cases for the presentations you must know, then move to Advanced when you want the harder differentials. The 8 free cases let you try the format before you decide on Pro.

Do the cases explain the reasoning, or just give the answer?

They show the reasoning. You get the history, examination, investigations and management step by step, with the key learning point at the end - so you understand why each decision was made, not just what the answer was.
