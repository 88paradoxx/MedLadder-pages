Question 1

A child receives prophylactic irradiation as part of conditioning before bone marrow transplantation for acute lymphoblastic leukemia. Which type of cell is least susceptible to damage from radiation in this setting?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The sensitivity of cells to ionizing radiation is described by the Law of Bergonié and Tribondeau, which states that radiosensitivity is directly proportional to the rate of cell division and inversely proportional to the degree of cell differentiation. Cells that are rapidly dividing and poorly differentiated are the most sensitive. Tissues like bone marrow, intestinal epithelium, and spermatogonia are composed of rapidly proliferating cells and are therefore highly radiosensitive. In contrast, neurons are highly specialized, fully differentiated cells that are post-mitotic (they do not divide in adulthood). This makes them one of the most radioresistant cell types in the body. While very high doses of radiation can cause neuronal damage, they are significantly less affected by conventional radiation doses compared to the rapidly renewing tissues listed in the other options.

Question 10

In which type of radiation is the Bragg peak effect most prominent?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The Bragg peak is a characteristic physical phenomenon describing the energy loss of heavy charged particles as they travel through matter. Unlike photons (X-rays) or electrons, which deposit their energy gradually from the surface inwards, a heavy charged particle like a proton travels through tissue and deposits a relatively small amount of energy along its initial path. As the particle slows down, its rate of energy loss increases dramatically, reaching a sharp maximum at a specific depth. This sharp peak in energy deposition is the Bragg peak. Beyond this peak, the energy deposition falls off to almost zero very rapidly. This physical property is exploited in proton beam therapy for cancer. It allows for the delivery of a very high, conformal dose of radiation precisely to the tumor volume, while sparing the healthy tissues located both in front of and, most importantly, behind the tumor.

Question 11

The salt and pepper appearance of the skull is characteristic of which condition?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The salt-and-pepper appearance of the skull on a lateral radiograph is a classic sign of bone disease caused by hyperparathyroidism, particularly primary hyperparathyroidism. Excess parathyroid hormone (PTH) leads to increased osteoclastic activity and bone resorption. In the skull, this process results in the loss of trabeculae in the diploic space (the spongy bone between the inner and outer tables of the calvarium). This resorption creates numerous tiny, punctate lucencies (the pepper) interspersed with areas of more normal or slightly sclerotic bone (the salt). This fine, granular pattern of demineralization is highly characteristic. In severe, long-standing cases, it can be associated with other skeletal findings of hyperparathyroidism, such as subperiosteal bone resorption in the hands and the presence of brown tumors. Multiple myeloma typically causes larger, well-defined, punched-out lytic lesions, not a fine granular pattern.

Question 14

This scenario is a classic example of a delusional perception, which is one of Schneider's first-rank symptoms of schizophrenia. A delusional perception is a two-stage process. First, there is a normal, real perception of an object or event (the patient sees birds flying, which is a true perception). Question 20

Which of the following contrast agents is not water-soluble?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Radiological contrast agents are used to enhance the visibility of internal structures. They are broadly classified based on their properties. Iodinated contrast agents, such as iohexol and iopamidol (which are non-ionic, low-osmolar agents), and Gastrografin (which is a high-osmolar, ionic agent containing diatrizoate meglumine and diatrizoate sodium), are all water-soluble compounds. They are used for a wide range of applications, including intravenous urography, angiography, CT scans, and gastrointestinal studies. In contrast, barium sulfate is an inert, metallic salt that is completely insoluble in water. It is used exclusively for imaging the gastrointestinal tract. It is administered orally or rectally as a suspension. Because it is not absorbed, it provides excellent opacification of the GI lumen. Its insolubility means it is contraindicated if a GI perforation is suspected, as leakage into the peritoneum can cause severe peritonitis.

Question 44

Which test relies on the idea that a suspect’s behavior changes when they recognize or react to an event they have witnessed?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The technique described is Brain Electrical Oscillation Signature Profiling (BEOS), commonly referred to as brain mapping or brain fingerprinting. This is a neuro-investigative technique that is based on the principle of detecting experiential knowledge. The underlying theory is that when an individual witnesses a significant event, their brain creates a memory trace of that event. When the suspect is later presented with stimuli (words, pictures) that are relevant to the crime (probes), their brain will automatically recognize these stimuli if they have prior knowledge of the event. This recognition elicits a specific, characteristic brainwave response known as a P300-MERMER (Memory and Encoding Related Multifaceted Electroencephalographic Response). An EEG is used to detect this brainwave, which is considered an involuntary physiological reaction, thus revealing whether the suspect possesses knowledge of the crime that only the perpetrator would have.
