The first step in the pathogenesis of streptococcal pharyngitis is the adhesion of Streptococcus pyogenes (Group A Streptococcus) to the epithelial cells of the pharynx. Question 14

How does colchicine act in the treatment of acute gout?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Colchicine is an anti-inflammatory agent used specifically for the treatment of acute gout flares. Its mechanism of action is unique and targeted at the inflammatory cascade triggered by uric acid crystals. Colchicine binds to tubulin, the protein subunit of microtubules, and inhibits its polymerization. This disruption of the microtubular network within inflammatory cells, particularly neutrophils, has several downstream effects. It impairs the motility and migration of leukocytes (neutrophils) into the affected joint, thus reducing the inflammatory infiltrate. It also inhibits the formation of the NLRP3 inflammasome and the subsequent release of pro-inflammatory cytokines like IL-1β from neutrophils and macrophages that have phagocytosed the crystals. Colchicine does not affect purine metabolism or uric acid levels directly; its action is purely anti-inflammatory. Therefore, its function as a microtubule inhibitor that subsequently blocks leukocyte migration and activation is the correct mechanism.

Question 19

A patient with recurrent Neisseria gonorrhoeae infections is being investigated. Which Click Here to Buy book on Amazon immunological test would best identify a defect in the complement pathway?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The complement system is a critical part of the innate immune defense against bacterial infections. The final step of the complement cascade is the formation of the Membrane Attack Complex (MAC), which is composed of the terminal complement components C5b through C9. The MAC creates pores in the outer membrane of bacteria, particularly Gram-negative bacteria, leading to their lysis. Individuals with an inherited deficiency in one of the terminal complement components (C5, C6, C7, C8, or C9) are unable to form a functional MAC. This makes them profoundly susceptible to recurrent and disseminated infections with Neisseria species, including Neisseria meningitidis and Neisseria gonorrhoeae. Sacubitril is a novel cardiovascular drug used in the treatment of heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. It is the first in its class, acting as a neprilysin inhibitor. Neprilysin (also known as neutral endopeptidase) is an enzyme responsible for the breakdown of several endogenous vasoactive peptides, including natriuretic peptides (such as ANP and BNP), bradykinin, and adrenomedullin. Question 81

The disc diffusion method is also known as :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The disc diffusion method is a standardized and widely used technique in clinical microbiology laboratories for performing antibiotic susceptibility testing. This method is also known as the Kirby-Bauer test. The procedure involves inoculating the surface of a Mueller-Hinton agar plate with a standardized suspension of the bacterial isolate to be tested. Paper discs, each impregnated with a specific concentration of a different antibiotic, are then placed on the agar surface. During incubation, the antibiotics diffuse out from the discs into the agar, creating a concentration gradient. If the bacteria are susceptible to an antibiotic, their growth will be inhibited in a circular zone around the disc. The diameter of this zone of inhibition is measured and compared to standardized interpretive charts to classify the organism as susceptible, intermediate, or resistant to that particular antibiotic.

Question 93

A patient presents with guttate lesions in one eye and bullous keratopathy in the other. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

This is a classic description of the clinical spectrum of Fuchs' endothelial dystrophy. This is a common, bilateral, slowly progressive genetic disorder of the corneal endothelium. Question 95

Endothelin exerts its effects through which receptors?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Endothelins are a family of potent vasoconstrictor peptides produced primarily by endothelial cells. They exert their wide range of physiological effects by binding to specific endothelin receptors on the surface of target cells. There are two main subtypes of endothelin receptors, ET-A and ET-B. Both of these receptors belong to the large superfamily of G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs). These are transmembrane receptors that, upon ligand binding, activate intracellular G proteins, which in turn trigger various downstream signaling cascades. For example, activation of the ET-A receptor on vascular smooth muscle cells couples to Gq protein, leading to an increase in intracellular calcium and subsequent vasoconstriction. Activation of ET-B receptors on endothelial cells can lead to the release of vasodilators like nitric oxide. The fundamental mechanism involves signaling through a GPCR. cGMP is a second messenger, not a receptor.

Question 97

Apixaban is:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Apixaban is one of the direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs), also known as non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOACs). Its mechanism of action is the direct, selective, and reversible inhibition of Factor Xa.
