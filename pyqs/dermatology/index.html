Question 2

In a patient with low ovarian reserve, the anti- Müllerian hormone (AMH) level will be?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH) is a glycoprotein hormone that is secreted exclusively by the granulosa cells of small, growing, preantral and small antral follicles in the ovary. Because it is produced by this specific cohort of developing follicles, the serum AMH level serves as a direct and reliable biomarker for the size of the remaining oocyte pool, or the ovarian reserve. A higher AMH level reflects a larger pool of growing follicles and a robust ovarian reserve. Conversely, as a woman ages and her ovarian reserve declines, the number of primordial follicles decreases, leading to a fall in the serum AMH level. A low ovarian reserve is therefore indicated by a low AMH level. While the exact reference ranges can vary slightly between assays, an AMH level less than 1.0 ng/mL is generally considered to be indicative of a diminished or low ovarian reserve.

Question 4

Which nerve is most frequently compressed during pregnancy and the puerperium?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

During pregnancy, hormonal changes and significant fluid retention are common. This generalized edema can lead to compression of peripheral nerves as they pass through anatomical tunnels or canals. The most common peripheral nerve entrapment syndrome in pregnancy is carpal tunnel syndrome, which is caused by the compression of the median nerve as it passes through the carpal tunnel at the wrist. The increased fluid volume during pregnancy increases the pressure within this confined space, leading to symptoms of pain, numbness, and tingling in the distribution of the median nerve (the thumb, index, middle, and radial half of the ring finger). Symptoms are often worse at night and can be bilateral. While other nerve palsies can occur (e.g., from positioning during labor), carpal tunnel syndrome due to median nerve compression is by far the most frequent peripheral neuropathy associated with pregnancy.

Question 5

Acute fatty liver of pregnancy most commonly occurs during which stage of pregnancy?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Acute Fatty Liver of Pregnancy (AFLP) is a rare but life-threatening obstetric emergency characterized by microvesicular fatty infiltration of hepatocytes, leading to acute liver failure. The onset of the disease is almost exclusively in the third trimester of pregnancy, with the majority of cases presenting between 30 and 38 weeks of gestation. It can also occur in the immediate postpartum period, but the classic presentation is late in pregnancy. The symptoms are often non-specific initially, including nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, and malaise, but can rapidly progress to jaundice, hypoglycemia, coagulopathy, encephalopathy, and multi-organ failure. The exact cause is unknown but is linked to a genetic defect in fetal fatty acid oxidation.

Question 6

At which stage is fetoplacental circulation established?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The development of the circulatory system between the fetus and the placenta is a critical early event in embryogenesis. The process begins with the formation of blood islands in the mesoderm of the yolk sac, connecting stalk, and chorion around day 17. These blood islands coalesce to form primitive blood vessels. By the end of the third week of gestation, these fetal vessels have developed and connected with the vessels forming in the chorionic villi of the placenta. At the same time, the primitive heart tube has formed and begins to beat and pump blood around day 21 or 22. This establishes a rudimentary but functional fetoplacental circulation, allowing for the exchange of gases, nutrients, and waste products between the maternal and fetal bloodstreams within the placenta. Therefore, the period of 20 to 22 days post-conception is the correct timeframe for the initial establishment of this vital circulatory loop.

Question 8

Which of the following is not a cause of secondary postpartum hemorrhage?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) is classified as primary or secondary. Primary PPH occurs within the first 24 hours after delivery. Secondary PPH is defined as excessive vaginal bleeding that occurs between 24 hours and 12 weeks postpartum. The most common cause of secondary PPH is subinvolution of the placental site, which is often associated with retained placental fragments or membranes. The second most common cause is uterine infection, such as endometritis. Other less common causes include the breakdown of uterine incision scars, placental site polyps, or underlying congenital bleeding disorders. Placenta previa, a condition where the placenta implants over or near the cervical os, is a major cause of antepartum hemorrhage (bleeding before delivery) and is a significant risk factor for primary PPH due to poor uterine contractility at the lower segment after placental separation. It is not a direct cause of secondary PPH.

Question 11

Which drug is administered to enhance fetal lung maturity?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Antenatal corticosteroids are a critical intervention administered to pregnant women who are at risk of preterm delivery (typically between 24 and 34 weeks of gestation). The purpose of this therapy is to accelerate the maturation of the fetal lungs and reduce the risk and severity of neonatal respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), which is caused by a deficiency of surfactant in the premature lungs. The corticosteroids cross the placenta and stimulate the fetal lung pneumocytes to produce and release surfactant. The two most commonly used and well-studied corticosteroids for this purpose are dexamethasone and betamethasone. A standard course consists of either two doses of betamethasone given 24 hours apart or four doses of dexamethasone given 12 hours apart. Beclomethasone is an inhaled corticosteroid used for asthma and is not used for this indication. Folic acid is a vitamin used to prevent neural tube defects.

Question 13

Which of the following situations is not classified as a high-risk pregnancy?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A high-risk pregnancy is one in which the mother or the fetus has an increased risk of morbidity or mortality before, during, or after delivery. Conditions like pre-existing or gestational diabetes (C), severe anemia (B), and significant maternal obesity (D) are all well-established risk factors that classify a pregnancy as high-risk. These conditions can lead to numerous complications, including pre-eclampsia, fetal growth abnormalities, preterm birth, and delivery complications. Question 14

Which of the following is not used in the management of preeclampsia?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The management of hypertension in pre-eclampsia involves the use of antihypertensive agents that are safe and effective during pregnancy. The commonly used and recommended first-line oral agents include the central alpha-2 agonist methyldopa (A) and the combined alpha- and beta-blocker labetalol (C). The calcium channel blocker nifedipine is also a first-line option. For hypertensive emergencies, intravenous labetalol and the direct vasodilator hydralazine (D) are used. However, atenolol (B), which is a beta-1 selective blocker, is generally avoided during pregnancy. Its use, particularly in the second and third trimesters, has been associated with an increased risk of fetal growth restriction (intrauterine growth restriction - IUGR) and low birth weight. Because of this potential adverse effect on fetal growth, other beta-blockers like labetalol are preferred, and atenolol is not a recommended choice for treating hypertension in pre-eclampsia.

Question 16

What is the definition of superfecundation?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Superfecundation is the fertilization of two or more ova released during the same ovulatory cycle by sperm from separate acts of sexual intercourse. This can result in dizygotic (fraternal) twins who have different biological fathers, a phenomenon known as heteropaternal superfecundation. This occurs if a woman has intercourse with two different men within a short time frame around her ovulation. It is a rare event in humans but is well-documented. This is different from the fertilization of two ova from a single act of intercourse (A), which is the standard way dizygotic twins are conceived from one father. It is also distinct from superfetation, which is the fertilization and subsequent implantation of a second ovum when a pregnancy is already established, an extremely rare occurrence in humans.

Question 17

At what stage of development does the fetal heart begin to contract?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The development of the cardiovascular system is one of the earliest and most critical events in embryogenesis. The primitive heart begins to form as a simple tube from the fusion of cardiogenic cords in the mesoderm during the third week of gestation. This heart tube begins to beat and pump blood in a coordinated, peristaltic fashion at approximately 21 to 22 days post-conception. This corresponds to the beginning of the fourth week of gestation, or the 5th week of pregnancy as dated from the last menstrual period. Therefore, the fetal heart starts its rhythmic contractions very early, within the 3-5 week gestational window. By the end of the fourth week, a rudimentary but functional circulation is established. The heartbeat can typically be detected by transvaginal ultrasound around 5.5 to 6 weeks of gestation.

Question 18

What is the preferred anesthetic technique for performing a cesarean section in a patient with severe pre-eclampsia?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Spinal anesthesia is the preferred anesthetic technique for performing a cesarean section in a patient with severe pre-eclampsia because it provides rapid, reliable, and dense anesthesia while avoiding the risks associated with general anesthesia. In severe pre-eclampsia, patients often have hypertension, airway edema, and an increased risk of difficult intubation. General anesthesia can cause a marked rise in blood pressure during laryngoscopy and intubation, which may precipitate complications such as intracranial hemorrhage or cardiac failure.

Show answer and explanation

For its use as an oral emergency contraceptive, ulipristal acetate is administered as a single oral dose of 30 mg. Question 21

What is the maximum allowable dose of PGF₂α (Prostaglandin F2 alpha) for managing postpartum hemorrhage (PPH)?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Prostaglandin F2-alpha (PGF2α), in the form of its synthetic analogue carboprost tromethamine, is a potent uterotonic agent used as a second-line treatment for postpartum hemorrhage (PPH) due to uterine atony that is refractory to oxytocin and methylergometrine. Carboprost is administered via deep intramuscular (IM) injection. The standard single dose is 250 micrograms (µg). This dose can be repeated every 15 to 90 minutes if uterine tone does not improve and bleeding continues. However, there is a maximum cumulative dose that should not be exceeded due to the risk of significant side effects (such as bronchospasm, hypertension, and severe diarrhea). The total maximum dose of carboprost that can be administered is 2 milligrams (mg), which is equivalent to a total of eight 250 µg injections.

In a patient with uterine prolapse who has been fitted with a vaginal ring pessary, how can you confirm that the pessary is correctly positioned?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A vaginal ring pessary is a commonly used conservative treatment for uterine prolapse. After insertion, proper placement must be assessed to ensure it provides adequate support to the uterus and vaginal walls. One of the simplest and most practical ways to confirm correct positioning is to ask the patient to perform activities that increase intra-abdominal pressure, such as coughing, straining, or bearing down (Valsalva maneuver).If the pessary remains in place and is not expelled during these actions, it indicates that it fits appropriately and is providing sufficient support. A correctly fitted pessary should also be comfortable, allowing the patient to walk, sit, and void urine without difficulty.Bleeding or discomfort are not reliable indicators of proper placement. Therefore, the best method to determine correct positioning is ensuring the pessary is retained during increased abdominal pressure.

Question 24

For which condition is hormone therapy (HT) recommended in postmenopausal women?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Hormone therapy (HT) is primarily indicated for the management of menopausal symptoms, especially vasomotor symptoms such as hot flushes and night sweats. Are these the official NEET PG papers?

No. NBEMS does not release official papers, so these are memory-based recall questions collected from the exams, each with an answer and, where available, an explanation.

How many Dermatology previous-year questions are here?

This page has 42 text-based recall questions tagged Dermatology across the NEET PG papers in the bank. Image-based questions are excluded for now because the bank's images are currently unavailable.

Why might the same question appear in a year paper too?

Year papers group questions by exam year; this page groups them by subject. A question recalled in one year appears once here, under its subject.
