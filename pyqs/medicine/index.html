Question 1

A posthumous child is:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The term posthumous is derived from the Latin postumus, meaning last, and in this context, it refers to something that occurs or is published after someone's death. In legal and common language, a posthumous child is specifically defined as a child who is born after the death of their biological father. This situation has significant legal implications, particularly concerning matters of inheritance, succession, and legitimacy. Most legal systems have provisions to ensure that a posthumous child is granted the same rights as a child born during the father's lifetime. For example, the child is generally considered an heir to the deceased father's estate. The term does not apply to a child delivered after the mother's death (which would typically involve a postmortem cesarean section) or after both parents' deaths, although the word could be used more broadly in a non-legal sense. Abandonment by parents is a separate social and legal issue.

Brimonidine is a topical alpha-2 adrenergic agonist used to lower intraocular pressure in the treatment of glaucoma. Chronic poisoning with the heavy metal cadmium has characteristic effects on the musculoskeletal and renal systems. Question 15

A patient shows proximal tubule proteinuria. Which metal is most likely responsible for this finding?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Heavy metals can be nephrotoxic, but they often affect different parts of the nephron. Cadmium is classically associated with chronic toxicity that primarily targets the proximal convoluted tubules of the kidney. The proximal tubules are responsible for reabsorbing low-molecular-weight (LMW) proteins from the glomerular filtrate. Cadmium damages these tubular cells, impairing their reabsorptive capacity. This leads to a specific type of proteinuria known as tubular proteinuria, which is characterized by the excretion of LMW proteins like β2-microglobulin and retinol-binding protein. This is a key feature of cadmium-induced nephropathy and can be an early sign of toxicity. In contrast, mercury and gold are more associated with glomerular damage, leading to albuminuria (glomerular proteinuria). Lead can also cause a chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis, but cadmium's effect on the proximal tubule is a more specific and classic association.

Question 18

Locard is famous for:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Dr. Edmond Locard was a French criminologist and a pioneer in the field of forensic science. He is most famous for formulating the basic principle of forensic science known as Locard's Exchange Principle. This principle states that every contact leaves a trace. It posits that whenever two objects come into contact, there is a mutual exchange of matter between them. This means a perpetrator of a crime will bring something into the crime scene and leave with something from it, and both can be used as forensic evidence. For example, fibers from a suspect's clothing might be found on a victim, and hairs from the victim might be found on the suspect. This theory of exchange is the fundamental concept behind the collection and analysis of trace evidence in criminal investigations. Fingerprint study was advanced by Sir Francis Galton , stature estimation by Karl Pearson , and identification by body measurements (anthropometry) by Alphonse Bertillon.

Question 20

What characteristic odor is associated with a mummified body?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Mummification is a modification of putrefaction that occurs in dry environments, either hot or cold, with good air circulation. In these conditions, the body tissues undergo rapid dehydration before decompositional bacteria can proliferate. The skin becomes dry, leathery, and brown-black, and it adheres closely to the underlying bones. Because the putrefactive process is arrested due to the lack of moisture, the characteristic foul, offensive, and putrid odors associated with decomposition do not develop. Instead, a mummified body is essentially odourless or may have a faint, dry, or dusty smell. This lack of odor is a key feature that distinguishes mummification from typical putrefaction. The internal organs also dry out and shrivel, and the body can be preserved for a very long time in this state. The smell is not pungent, which would imply a sharp, strong smell.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The Sabin-Feldman dye test is the gold standard serological test for the diagnosis of toxoplasmosis, an infection caused by the protozoan parasite Toxoplasma gondii. The principle of the test is that live, laboratory-maintained Toxoplasma tachyzoites are incubated with the patient's serum. In contrast, if no antibodies are present, the parasites will remain viable and will stain blue. A high titer of antibodies indicates infection.

Question 169

Who is recognized as the Father of Microbiology?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Antonie van Leeuwenhoek is widely regarded as the Father of Microbiology because he was the first person to observe and accurately describe microorganisms using handcrafted microscopes of exceptional quality. Question 171

Which organism is most likely to cause infection following a kidney transplant?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Patients who undergo solid organ transplantation, such as a kidney transplant, require long-term immunosuppressive therapy to prevent graft rejection. This chronic state of immunosuppression makes them highly susceptible to a wide range of opportunistic infections. Among viral pathogens, Cytomegalovirus (CMV) is one of the most significant and common causes of morbidity and mortality in these patients. Infection can occur as a primary infection (if a CMV-negative recipient receives an organ from a CMV-positive donor), a reactivation of the recipient's latent CMV, or a superinfection with a new strain. Because it is so common and can have severe consequences, routine monitoring for CMV viremia and prophylactic or pre-emptive antiviral therapy (e.g., with valganciclovir) is a standard part of post-transplant care. While bacterial infections also occur, CMV is the most characteristic viral pathogen in this setting.
