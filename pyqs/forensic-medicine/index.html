Question 1

Reed–Sternberg cells are characteristically seen in which of the following diseases?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Reed–Sternberg cells are the diagnostic hallmark of Hodgkin’s disease (Hodgkin lymphoma), a malignant disorder of the lymphatic system. These cells are large, abnormal B-lymphocyte–derived cells that typically contain two mirror-image nuclei with prominent nucleoli, producing the classic “owl’s eye” appearance under the microscope. They are usually found within an inflammatory background composed of lymphocytes, eosinophils, plasma cells, and macrophages. The presence of Reed–Sternberg cells in lymph node biopsy is essential for confirming the diagnosis of Hodgkin lymphoma. Clinically, patients often present with painless enlargement of lymph nodes, especially in the cervical region, along with systemic “B symptoms” such as fever, night sweats, and unexplained weight loss. Reed–Sternberg cells are not seen in sickle cell anemia, thalassemia, or chronic myeloid leukemia, which are disorders of red blood cells or myeloid lineage rather than lymphoid malignancy.

Question 3

Struvite urinary stones are primarily composed of which metal?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

(A) & (b)Struvite stones are a type of kidney stone composed mainly of magnesium ammonium phosphate. They typically form in alkaline urine during urinary tract infections caused by urease-producing bacteria such as Proteus, Klebsiella, or Pseudomonas. These bacteria break down urea into ammonia, which raises urinary pH and promotes precipitation of magnesium, ammonium, and phosphate ions to form struvite crystals. Over time, these stones can grow rapidly and may develop into large “staghorn calculi” that occupy much of the renal pelvis and calyces. Unlike calcium stones, which are the most common type of kidney stones overall, struvite stones are specifically associated with infection rather than metabolic abnormalities. They can lead to recurrent infections, obstruction, kidney damage, and impaired renal function if untreated. Question 6

Which of the following statements about Alzheimer’s disease is false?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Alzheimer’s disease is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder and the most common cause of dementia in older adults. It is characterized by gradual loss of memory, cognitive decline, impaired reasoning, language difficulties, and behavioral changes due to degeneration of neurons in the brain, particularly in the hippocampus and cerebral cortex. Although its exact cause is not fully understood, hallmark pathological features include extracellular amyloid plaques and intracellular neurofibrillary tangles composed of tau protein. Currently, there is no cure for Alzheimer’s disease. Available treatments, such as cholinesterase inhibitors and NMDA receptor antagonists, may temporarily improve symptoms or slow progression but cannot stop or reverse the disease process. The statement that about one in ten people aged 65 years and older has Alzheimer’s disease is broadly accurate, and dementia is a defining clinical outcome of the disorder. Therefore, the claim that Alzheimer’s disease is curable is false.

Question 22

What is the typical order in which rigor mortis develops and progresses through the muscles of the human body?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Rigor mortis is the postmortem stiffening of muscles due to the depletion of ATP, which prevents the detachment of myosin heads from actin filaments. The onset and progression of rigor mortis follow a predictable pattern, described by Nysten's Law. It typically begins in the smaller muscle groups and progresses to the larger ones. The sequence starts in the muscles of the face and jaw (eyelids, jaw muscles), then spreads downwards to the muscles of the neck, upper limbs, trunk, and finally to the lower limbs. This classic cephalocaudal or head-to-foot progression is due to the different metabolic rates and glycogen stores in various muscle groups. The process usually begins 2-4 hours after death, is fully established in about 12 hours, persists for another 12 hours, and then resolves in the same head-to-foot sequence as decomposition begins.

Question 40

While recording evidence in a court of law, a lawyer asked the witness, Were you present when A killed B?, and the witness answered yes. This type of leading questioning is permitted in :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

A leading question is one that suggests the desired answer or contains the information the questioner is looking to have confirmed (e.g., You were at the scene, werent you?). According to the rules of evidence, leading questions are generally prohibited during direct examination (or examination-in-chief) (A, B) and re-direct examination (C), where the lawyer is questioning their own witness. However, leading questions are permitted during cross-examination, where a lawyer is questioning a witness called by the opposing party. The goal of cross-examination is to challenge the witnesss testimony and test their credibility.

This process, known as lymphatic drainage, is a normal physiological response. In a deceased person, especially if the skin is decomposed, burned, or obscured, the tattoo itself may become invisible. However, the pigment will still be present within the regional draining lymph nodes. Therefore, during a postmortem examination, the forensic pathologist can dissect and examine the regional lymph nodes corresponding to the alleged tattoo location. Finding the characteristic ink pigment within these nodes can serve as a definitive method of identification, confirming the presence of a tattoo even when it is no longer visible on the skin surface.

Question 51

Perjury is punishable under which section of the Indian Penal Code (IPC)?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

In the Indian Penal Code, the act of perjury is defined in Section 191 as giving false evidence and in Section 192 as fabricating false evidence. However, the punishment for committing these acts is explicitly detailed in Section 193 of the IPC. This section serves as the primary penal provision for perjury. It stipulates that anyone who intentionally gives false evidence or fabricates false evidence for use in any stage of a judicial proceeding shall be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to seven years, and shall also be liable to a fine. For false evidence given in any other case, the punishment is imprisonment for up to three years, and a fine. Therefore, while Section 191 defines the crime, Section 193 is the legal provision that prescribes the punishment for it, making it the correct answer for the punishment of perjury.

Question 52

Viscera in toxicology is stored in?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

When viscera (internal organs) are collected during a postmortem examination for toxicological analysis, it is crucial to preserve them in a solution that prevents decomposition without interfering with the chemical tests for poisons. The standard preservative used for this purpose in India and many other regions is rectified spirit, which is a highly concentrated ethanol solution (approximately 95% alcohol by volume). Ethanol is an effective preservative that prevents bacterial putrefaction of the tissues. Importantly, it is chemically neutral and does not interfere with the standard extraction and detection methods for most common poisons. In contrast, formalin (C) is an excellent preservative for histopathology but is absolutely contraindicated for toxicology, as it denatures proteins and can interfere with the chemical analysis of many toxins. A saturated salt solution can be used if rectified spirit is unavailable, but the latter is the standard.
