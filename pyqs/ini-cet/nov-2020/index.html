Question 1

Reticular fibers are absent in :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Thymus Reticular fibers, which are composed of type III collagen, form a delicate, intricate meshwork that provides structural support to the parenchymal cells of most lymphoid and hematopoietic organs. This reticular stroma is a characteristic feature of the bone marrow (A), the spleen (B) (in the white and red pulp), and the lymph nodes (D), creating a scaffold for lymphocytes, macrophages, and other immune cells. The thymus (C) is the distinct exception among these organs. The structural framework of the thymus is not made of reticular fibers but is instead formed by a unique cytoreticulum composed of specialized cells called thymic epithelial cells (also known as epithelioreticular cells). These cells provide the necessary support and create the microenvironment essential for the maturation and selection of T-lymphocytes (thymocytes). This cellular, rather than fibrous, stroma is a key histological feature that distinguishes the thymus from other lymphoid tissues.

Question 2

A patient is unable to abduct the upper limb. Which nerve root is affected?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

C5 The abduction of the upper limb is a complex movement primarily performed by the deltoid and supraspinatus muscles. The initiation of abduction (the first 0-15 degrees) is by the supraspinatus muscle, which is innervated by the suprascapular nerve (with root values C5 and C6). The main power for abduction from 15 to 90 degrees comes from the deltoid muscle, which is innervated by the axillary nerve. The axillary nerve is derived from the posterior cord of the brachial plexus and receives its fibers from the C5 and C6 nerve roots. The C5 root is considered the primary contributor to both the axillary and suprascapular nerves and is therefore the most critical nerve root for shoulder abduction. A lesion affecting the C5 root, such as in an upper brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy), characteristically results in a profound inability to abduct the arm. The C6 root also contributes but is more associated with elbow flexion.

Question 3

Which of the following muscles does not take part in abducting the arm overhead?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Pectoralis major Abduction of the arm to an overhead position (180 degrees) is a coordinated movement involving both the glenohumeral joint and the scapulothoracic articulation, a process known as scapulohumeral rhythm. The deltoid muscle (B) is the prime mover of abduction at the glenohumeral joint up to 90 degrees. To achieve abduction beyond 90 degrees, the scapula must upwardly rotate on the thoracic wall. This crucial upward rotation is accomplished by a force couple created by the upper and lower fibers of the trapezius muscle (C) and the powerful serratus anterior muscle (A). The serratus anterior protracts the scapula and is essential for its upward rotation. In contrast, the primary actions of the pectoralis major muscle (D) are adduction and medial rotation of the humerus. It acts as an antagonist to abduction and therefore is not involved in, and in fact opposes, the action of overhead abduction.

Question 4

The slow waves (basic electrical rhythm or BER frequency) are lowest in which of the following?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Stomach Slow waves, also known as the basic electrical rhythm (BER), are spontaneous, rhythmic fluctuations in the membrane potential of gastrointestinal smooth muscle cells. They are generated by specialized pacemaker cells called the interstitial cells of Cajal. While these waves do not cause contractions themselves, they determine the maximum frequency at which contractions can occur. The frequency of the BER follows a gradient along the GI tract. It is highest in the proximal small intestine, with the duodenum (B) having a rate of about 12 cycles per minute. The frequency then decreases distally through the jejunum (C) and ileum (D) (to about 8-9 cycles per minute). The stomach (A), however, has a much lower intrinsic BER frequency, with only about 3 slow waves per minute. Therefore, among the options provided, the frequency of slow waves is least in the stomach.

Question 5

All of the following substances show an increase in concentration within the Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT), except which one? :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Amino acid In the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT), a large proportion of filtered substances are reabsorbed back into the bloodstream. However, the concentration of a substance within the tubular fluid depends on whether it is reabsorbed more rapidly than water. Amino acids and glucose are almost completely reabsorbed early in the PCT by active transport (via sodium-dependent cotransporters). Because they are reabsorbed rapidly and nearly completely, their concentration in tubular fluid decreases, not increases. Bicarbonate is also significantly reabsorbed in the PCT (about 80–90%). Its concentration generally falls as it is reclaimed efficiently. In contrast, chloride initially shows a relative increase in concentration in the early PCT. This occurs because water and bicarbonate are reabsorbed faster than chloride, leaving chloride behind and increasing its tubular concentration temporarily before it is later reabsorbed. Thus, the substance that does not show an increase in concentration in the PCT is amino acid, making option (A) correct.

Question 6

What happens when a person moves from a lying (supine) position to standing?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Decrease firing in baroreceptor afferents When a person stands up from a supine position, gravity causes blood to pool in the veins of the lower extremities. This leads to a transient decrease in venous return to the heart, a decrease in cardiac output, and consequently, a drop in arterial blood pressure. This fall in blood pressure is sensed by the high-pressure baroreceptors located in the carotid sinus and the aortic arch. These receptors are stretch receptors, and the reduced stretch due to the lower blood pressure causes a decrease in the firing rate of the baroreceptor afferent nerves that travel to the brainstem. This decreased afferent signal triggers a compensatory reflex : sympathetic output increases (causing vasoconstriction and increased heart rate), and vagal (parasympathetic) output to the heart decreases (C is incorrect), all aimed at restoring the blood pressure to its normal level.

Question 7

What is the sequence of events after the "P" wave? A. "a" wave B. 1st heart sound C. Rapid filling of ventricles D. "t" wave

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A > C > B > D. Cardiac Cycle Sequence To understand the correct order, let's trace the events that follow the P wave, which represents the electrical depolarization of the atria. 1. P wave (Atrial Depolarization) → Atrial Contraction i. A. "a" wave : The contraction of the atria increases atrial pressure, causing a corresponding pressure wave in the jugular veins known as the "a" wave. This happens immediately after the P wave. ii. C. Rapid filling of ventricles : The contracting atria push the final volume of blood into the ventricles. This phase is more accurately called "late ventricular filling" or the "atrial kick," but it follows the "a" wave as the pressure from the contraction drives the blood flow. 2. QRS Complex (Ventricular Depolarization) → Ventricular Contraction B. 1st heart sound (S1) : As the ventricles begin to contract (systole), the pressure inside them quickly rises above the pressure in the atria. This forces the atrioventricular (mitral and tricuspid) valves to snap shut, producing the first heart sound ("lub"). This event occurs after the QRS complex, which follows the P wave. 3. T wave (Ventricular Repolarization) D. "t" wave : This wave on the ECG represents the repolarization (electrical reset) of the ventricles. It occurs during the latter part of ventricular contraction, after the first heart sound but before the second heart sound. Therefore, the correct chronological sequence of these events after the P wave is : "a" wave → filling of ventricles → 1st heart sound → "t" wave.

Question 9

In membrane receptors, the transmembrane region is made up of which type of amino acids?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Hydrophobic Transmembrane proteins, including membrane receptors, must span the lipid bilayer of the cell membrane. The core of the lipid bilayer is composed of the fatty acid tails of phospholipids, creating a highly nonpolar, oily environment. To be stably embedded within this environment, the segment of the protein that passes through the membrane must be composed of amino acids with nonpolar, hydrophobic side chains. These amino acids (such as leucine, isoleucine, valine, and phenylalanine) interact favorably with the lipid tails, anchoring the protein in the membrane. Typically, this transmembrane domain is arranged in an alpha-helical structure. In contrast, the extracellular and intracellular domains of the receptor, which are exposed to the aqueous environments outside and inside the cell, are primarily composed of hydrophilic (B) and charged (C) amino acids.

Question 10

High anion gap acidosis is seen in all except :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Respiratory disorders A high anion gap metabolic acidosis is caused by the accumulation of unmeasured organic acids in the blood. In diabetic ketoacidosis (A), the acids are acetoacetate and beta-hydroxybutyrate. In lactic acidosis (B), the acid is lactate. Certain types of renal failure (uremia) can also lead to the accumulation of acids like sulfates and phosphates, causing a high anion gap. The term "renal aciduria" (C) is ambiguous but likely refers to this or a renal tubular acidosis that can sometimes be associated with a high anion gap. In contrast, respiratory disorders (D) cause respiratory acidosis or alkalosis, not metabolic acidosis. Respiratory acidosis is caused by the retention of CO 2 . While this lowers the pH, it does not involve the addition of an unmeasured anion and therefore does not cause a high anion gap. It is a distinct acid-base disturbance with a different underlying pathophysiology.

Question 11

The macula densa is located in :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) The macula densa is a specialized plaque of densely packed epithelial cells that is a key component of the juxtaglomerular apparatus (JGA). The JGA is a structure that regulates renal blood flow and glomerular filtration rate. Anatomically, the macula densa is located at the very beginning of the distal convoluted tubule (DCT), at the precise point where the tubule passes between the afferent and efferent arterioles of its own glomerulus. Some texts describe its location as the terminal part of the thick ascending limb of the loop of Henle, right at the transition to the DCT. These cells function as chemoreceptors, sensing the concentration of sodium chloride in the tubular fluid. This information is then used to signal the afferent arteriole to constrict or dilate, a process known as tubuloglomerular feedback, which helps to autoregulate the filtration rate of the individual nephron.

Question 13

Which of the following statements about skeletal muscle contraction are correct?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

A & C both The process of excitation-contraction coupling in skeletal muscle has specific characteristics. The action potential travels down the T-tubules, where it activates voltage-sensitive DHP receptors. These receptors are mechanically linked to ryanodine receptors on the membrane of the sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR). This mechanical coupling triggers the opening of the ryanodine receptors, causing a massive release of stored calcium ions (Ca ++ ) from the SR into the cytoplasm (C is true). This rise in intracellular calcium initiates the cross-bridge cycle. Unlike cardiac muscle, skeletal muscle contraction is not directly dependent on the influx of extracellular calcium for triggering this release; the process is purely mechanical. Therefore, extracellular Ca ++ is not important for initiating the contraction (A is true). Calcium is released from the SR, not the mitochondria (B is incorrect). Since both statements A and C are true regarding skeletal muscle, option D is the correct answer. BIOCHEMISTRY

Question 14

DNA methylation is not associated with :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

RNA splicing DNA methylation is a fundamental epigenetic mechanism that plays a role in several key cellular processes. It is a primary mechanism of gene silencing (C), as the methylation of CpG islands in a gene's promoter region typically prevents the binding of transcription factors and recruits repressive proteins. This process is intimately linked with chromatin remodeling (A), as methylated DNA promotes the formation of condensed, transcriptionally silent heterochromatin. In prokaryotes, it is also crucial for methyl-directed mismatch repair (D), a system that uses the methylation status of the parental DNA strand to identify and correct errors made during DNA replication on the newly synthesized strand. However, RNA splicing (B) is a post-transcriptional process. It involves the removal of introns from a pre-mRNA molecule to form mature mRNA. This process is carried out by the spliceosome and is regulated by splicing factors, but it is not directly controlled by the methylation status of the DNA from which the RNA was transcribed.

Question 17

Which of the following statements about telomerase is incorrect?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

It maintains high fidelity during DNA replication Telomerase is a unique enzyme responsible for maintaining the length of chromosomes (D) by adding repetitive nucleotide sequences to the ends, or telomeres. It is a ribonucleoprotein that contains its own RNA template and synthesizes DNA from this RNA template. This enzymatic activity makes it a specialized type of reverse transcriptase (A). Telomerase is a feature of eukaryotic chromosomes; prokaryotes have circular chromosomes and do not require it (B). The statement that telomerase maintains high fidelity during DNA replication (C) is incorrect. The primary enzyme responsible for the high-fidelity replication of the main chromosomal DNA is DNA polymerase, which has proofreading capabilities. Telomerase's function is specifically to solve the "end-replication problem" by adding telomeric repeats, a process that is separate from the high-fidelity replication of the coding regions of the genome.

Question 18

The following values are given below: A. Km of an enzyme = 10 µM; kcat = 20s ⁻ ¹ B. Km of an enzyme = 200 nM; kcat = 50s ⁻ ¹ C. Km of an enzyme = 2 µM; kcat = 200s ⁻ ¹ D. Km of an enzyme = 4 µM; kcat = 200s ⁻ ¹ The highest catalytic efficiency is seen in :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

B Catalytic efficiency is a measure of how efficiently an enzyme converts a substrate into a product. It is represented by the ratio of two kinetic parameters : kcat / Km. A higher kcat (turnover number) or a lower Km (substrate affinity) will result in a higher catalytic efficiency. We need to calculate this ratio for each option, ensuring the units are consistent. Let's convert all Km values to µM. A : Efficiency = 20 / 10 = 2 B : Km = 200 nM = 0.2 µM. Efficiency = 50 / 0.2 = 250 C : Efficiency = 200 / 2 = 100 D : Efficiency = 200 / 4 = 50 Comparing the calculated efficiency values (A=2, B=250, C=100, D=50), enzyme B clearly has the highest catalytic efficiency. This is because it has both a very high affinity for its substrate (very low Km) and a reasonably high turnover rate (kcat).

Question 19

Which of the following enzymes shows a decrease in Vitamin B12 deficiency? A. Methionine synthase B. Methyl malonyl CoA isomerase C. Homocysteine synthase D. Folate reductase Choose the correct options :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A and B Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is an essential cofactor for only two enzymes in the human body. A deficiency of Vitamin B12 will lead to decreased activity of these two specific enzymes. 1. Methionine synthase : This enzyme catalyzes the conversion of homocysteine to methionine. It requires methylcobalamin (a form of Vitamin B12) as a cofactor to transfer a methyl group from N5-methyltetrahydrofolate. Its deficiency leads to the accumulation of homocysteine. 2. Question 20

The steps of PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) are :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Denaturation → Annealing → Elongation The Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) is a technique used to amplify a specific segment of DNA exponentially. It consists of a series of repeated cycles, with each cycle comprising three distinct temperature-controlled steps : 1. Denaturation : The reaction mixture is heated to a high temperature (typically 94-98°C). This breaks the hydrogen bonds holding the double-stranded DNA template together, separating it into two single strands. 2. Annealing : The temperature is lowered (typically 50-65°C). This allows short, single-stranded DNA primers, which are complementary to the sequences flanking the target region, to bind (anneal) to their respective sites on the single-stranded DNA templates. 3. Elongation (or Extension) : The temperature is raised again (typically to 72°C), which is the optimal temperature for the thermostable DNA polymerase (like Taq polymerase). The polymerase then synthesizes a new complementary DNA strand, starting from the primers and extending along the template strand. This three-step cycle is repeated 25-35 times, leading to an exponential amplification of the target DNA sequence.

Question 21

Compare the normality of 2 mM HCl and 1 mM H 2 SO 4 .

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Same Normality (N) is a measure of concentration defined as the molarity (M) of a solution multiplied by its equivalence factor (or n-factor). For an acid, the n-factor is the number of moles of hydrogen ions (H + ) that one mole of the acid can donate. For hydrochloric acid (HCl), it is a monoprotic acid, meaning it donates one H + ion per molecule. Therefore, its n-factor is 1. 1. Normality of 2 mM HCl = 2 mM × 1 = 2 mN (millinormal). 2. For sulfuric acid (H 2 SO 4 ), it is a diprotic acid, meaning it can donate two H + ions per molecule. Therefore, its n-factor is 2. 3. Normality of 1 mM H 2 SO 4 = 1 mM × 2 = 2 mN. Since both solutions have a normality of 2 mN, their normalities are the same.

Question 22

The thermogenic effect of food is highest for :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Proteins This is a duplicate of a previous question. The thermic effect of food (TEF), also called specific dynamic action, is the energy expenditure required for the digestion, absorption, and metabolism of nutrients after a meal. This value varies significantly between the different macronutrients. Proteins have the highest TEF, with about 20-30% of their ingested energy being used for their processing and assimilation. This is due to the energy-intensive processes of deamination and urea synthesis. Carbohydrates (B) have an intermediate TEF, typically in the range of 5-10%. Lipids (A), or fats, have the lowest TEF, at only about 0-3%, meaning they are the most metabolically efficient macronutrient to store. Therefore, protein consumption leads to the greatest increase in metabolic rate compared to the other macronutrients, a principle often utilized in diets for weight management. PATHOLOGY

Question 23

In which of the following conditions is the JAK2 mutation found?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Polycythemia vera The JAK2 (Janus kinase 2) gene encodes a tyrosine kinase that is a critical component of the signaling pathway for several hematopoietic growth factor receptors, including the erythropoietin receptor. A specific acquired somatic mutation in the JAK2 gene, known as JAK2 V617F, is the pathognomonic molecular marker for the classic BCR-ABL negative myeloproliferative neoplasms (MPNs). This gain-of-function mutation leads to constitutive, growth factor-independent activation of the signaling pathway, causing uncontrolled proliferation of hematopoietic cells. The JAK2 V617F mutation is found in over 95% of patients with Polycythemia Vera, which is characterized by an overproduction of red blood cells. It is also found in about 50-60% of patients with essential thrombocythemia and primary myelofibrosis. The other anemias listed are not associated with this mutation.

Question 24

Which of the following increases in COPD? 1 FRC (Functional Residual Capacity) 2 TLC (Total Lung Capacity) 3 VC (Vital Capacity) 4 FEV1/FVC (Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 second / Forced Vital Capacity)

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

1 & 2 Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD) is characterized by persistent airflow limitation. This obstruction leads to air trapping, where air gets trapped in the lungs at the end of expiration. This has several effects on lung volumes measured by spirometry and plethysmography. The trapped air leads to an increase in the volume of air remaining in the lungs after a normal expiration, which is the Functional Residual Capacity (FRC) (1). It also leads to an increase in the volume of air remaining after a maximal expiration (Residual Volume, RV). As a consequence of the increased RV, the Total Lung Capacity (TLC) (2), which is the sum of RV and Vital Capacity, is also typically increased or normal. In contrast, the Vital Capacity (VC) (3) (the maximum volume of air that can be exhaled) is often decreased. The hallmark of COPD is a reduced FEV1/FVC ratio (4), which is decreased, not increased.

Question 25

Which of the following is not a distinguishing feature between Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma (HL) and Nodular Lymphocyte Predominant Hodgkin Lymphoma (NLPHL)?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Nodular lymphocyte predominant has poor prognosis This statement is the exception because it is false. Nodular Lymphocyte-Predominant Hodgkin Lymphoma (NLPHL) is an indolent lymphoma with a very favorable prognosis, which is generally better than that of most subtypes of Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma (CHL). Question 26

Which of the following is found in t(11;14)? A. SOX 11 B. Cyclin D1 C. CD10 D. CD200

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

B and D only. The chromosomal translocation t(11;14) is the genetic signature of Mantle Cell Lymphoma (MCL). This genetic event is pivotal because it leads directly to the over expression of a key protein. B. Cyclin D1 : The t(11;14) translocation places the CCND1 gene (which codes for Cyclin D1) next to a powerful gene enhancer. This results in the massive overexpression of Cyclin D1, a protein that pushes the cell cycle forward. It's the defining molecular feature of MCL. D. CD200 : This is a cell surface glycoprotein. While not as fundamental as Cyclin D1, research shows that CD200 is expressed in a notable subset of MCL cases (around 25-40%). Question 27

Which of the following conditions is least likely to result in a dry tap during bone marrow aspiration?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Follicular Lymphoma A "dry tap" on bone marrow aspiration occurs when no marrow particles can be aspirated, despite the needle being correctly positioned. This is most commonly due to marrow fibrosis (myelofibrosis) or packing of the marrow space by a dense infiltrate of cells. Conditions notorious for causing significant marrow fibrosis and a dry tap include hairy cell leukemia (D), chronic myeloproliferative disorders like primary myelofibrosis (A), and acute megakaryoblastic leukemia (M7 AML) (C). In these conditions, a bone marrow biopsy is essential for diagnosis. In contrast, while follicular lymphoma (B) can infiltrate the bone marrow, the infiltration is typically interstitial or paratrabecular and not usually associated with the extensive fibrosis that would lead to a dry tap. Therefore, among the choices provided, follicular lymphoma is the least likely to result in an inaspirable bone marrow.

Question 28

Which of the following is not true about telomerase?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

DNA repair/synthesis This question is a near duplicate of a previous one. Telomerase is a unique ribonucleoprotein enzyme. It acts as a specialized reverse transcriptase (A), using its own internal RNA component as a template to synthesize DNA. Its specific function is to add repetitive DNA sequences (telomeres) to the ends of chromosomes, thereby maintaining chromosome length (B) and compensating for the "end-replication problem." This process is specific to linear chromosomes and is thus a feature of eukaryotes (C). The statement that it is involved in general DNA repair/synthesis (D) is not true. While it does synthesize DNA, its function is highly specialized and restricted to the telomeres. The major enzymes for general DNA synthesis (replication) and repair are the various DNA polymerases, not telomerase.

Question 29

Which of the following statements about Nodular Lymphocyte Predominant Hodgkin Lymphoma (NLPHL) is incorrect?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Poor prognosis compared to Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma (CHL) This is a duplicate of question 27. The incorrect statement is that NLPHL has a poor prognosis. Nodular Lymphocyte-Predominant Hodgkin Lymphoma (NLPHL) is an indolent (slow-growing) form of lymphoma that typically has a very favorable prognosis, which is generally considered better than that of most subtypes of Classical Hodgkin Lymphoma (CHL). The other statements accurately describe the key features of NLPHL that distinguish it from CHL. It is characteristically negative for Epstein-Barr Virus (EBV) (A). Question 30

Pancytopenia on blood examination is seen in :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Hairy cell leukemia. All four options are valid causes of pancytopenia. Megaloblastic anemia, Acute promyelocytic leukemia, and Myelodysplastic syndrome all disrupt the bone marrow's ability to produce sufficient healthy blood cells, leading to low counts. However, Hairy cell leukemia is uniquely characterized by this presentation. The disease involves malignant B-lymphocytes (the "hairy cells") infiltrating the bone marrow. This infiltration, combined with the significant marrow fibrosis (scarring) it causes, physically crowds out and suppresses the normal production of red cells, white cells, and platelets. Therefore, pancytopenia is a hallmark and nearly universal finding at the time of diagnosis for Hairy cell leukemia, often accompanied by a massively enlarged spleen. This makes it a quintessential example of a disease presenting with pancytopenia.

Question 31

Which clotting factor deficiency does not affect clotting in vivo?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Factor XII Factor XII (Hageman factor) is the protein that initiates the intrinsic pathway of the coagulation cascade in vitro, as measured by the activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT). A deficiency in Factor XII will therefore cause a markedly prolonged aPTT. However, extensive clinical observation has shown that individuals with Factor XII deficiency do not have a bleeding disorder and do not experience abnormal bleeding in vivo, even after major surgery or trauma. This indicates that the initiation of the intrinsic pathway via Factor XII is not essential for normal hemostasis in the body. In contrast, deficiencies of all other clotting factors, including Factor V (A), Factor VII (B), and Tissue Factor (Factor III) (C), lead to significant and often severe bleeding disorders, highlighting their crucial roles in the in vivo coagulation process. Question 32

Which is used for absence seizures? 1. Ethosuximide 2. Carbamazepine 3. Valproic acid 4. Gabapentin

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

1 & 3 Only Absence seizures are a type of generalized seizure characterized by brief episodes of staring and unresponsiveness, accompanied by a classic 3-Hz spike-and-wave pattern on EEG. The treatment for absence seizures is specific. 1. Ethosuximide : This is the first-line and most specific drug for treating uncomplicated childhood absence seizures. It works by blocking T-type calcium channels in thalamic neurons. 3. Valproic acid : This is a broad-spectrum antiepileptic drug that is also highly effective for absence seizures and is the drug of choice if the patient also has co-existing generalized tonic-clonic seizures. 2. Carbamazepine : This is a narrow-spectrum drug used for focal seizures. It is contraindicated in absence seizures as it can exacerbate them. 4. Gabapentin : This is used as an add-on for focal seizures and for neuropathic pain; it is not effective for absence seizures. Therefore, only ethosuximide and valproic acid are used for the treatment of absence seizures.

Question 33

If codeine is being introduced to the market in combination with dextromethorphan as an antitussive, what steps should be taken next?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Should not be used as it is irrational This question assesses the principles of rational pharmacotherapy. Codeine is an opioid that suppresses cough by acting on the cough center in the brainstem. Dextromethorphan is a non-opioid, synthetic derivative of morphine that also acts centrally to suppress the cough reflex, likely via sigma-1 receptor agonism and NMDA receptor antagonism. Since both drugs are centrally acting antitussives that work to achieve the same therapeutic goal (cough suppression), combining them in a fixed-dose combination offers no proven synergistic or additive benefit over using an adequate dose of a single agent. Such a combination increases the risk of side effects, potential for confusion, and cost without providing any additional efficacy. This violates the principles of rational drug use, and therefore, the combination should not be used as it is irrational.

Question 34

Which of the following antidepressants is linked to the fewest sexual side effects?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Mirtazapine Sexual dysfunction (e.g., decreased libido, anorgasmia) is a very common and distressing side effect of many antidepressant medications, particularly those that work primarily by increasing serotonin levels. Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) like fluoxetine (B) and Serotonin-Norepinephrine Reuptake Inhibitors (SNRIs) like venlafaxine (C) are associated with a high incidence of sexual side effects. In contrast, mirtazapine is an atypical antidepressant with a different mechanism of action (a noradrenergic and specific serotonergic antagonist, or NaSSA). It blocks α 2-adrenergic autoreceptors and certain serotonin receptors (5-HT2 and 5-HT3). This mechanism is associated with a significantly lower incidence of sexual dysfunction compared to SSRIs and SNRIs, and in some cases, it may even improve sexual function. Imipenem (D) is a carbapenem antibiotic, not an antidepressant.

Question 35

Which of the following is a bone-forming drug?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Teriparatide Osteoporosis medications are broadly classified as either anti-resorptive (slowing bone breakdown) or anabolic (promoting new bone formation). Most treatments fall into the first category. Bisphosphonates (D), raloxifene (A) (a SERM), and calcitonin (C) are all anti-resorptive agents that work by inhibiting the activity of osteoclasts, the cells that break down bone. In contrast, teriparatide is an anabolic agent. It is a recombinant form of the first 34 amino acids of human parathyroid hormone (PTH). When administered intermittently as a once-daily subcutaneous injection, PTH paradoxically stimulates the activity of osteoblasts, the cells responsible for forming new bone. This leads to a significant increase in bone mineral density and a substantial reduction in fracture risk. It is the primary anabolic or "bone-forming" drug used for the treatment of severe osteoporosis.

Question 36

Which of the following statements about disulfiram are correct?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Disulfiram action still persists for a few days after being stopped Disulfiram is a medication used as a deterrent therapy for alcohol dependence. It works by irreversibly inhibiting the enzyme aldehyde dehydrogenase. If a person taking disulfiram consumes alcohol, acetaldehyde accumulates, causing a highly unpleasant reaction (flushing, nausea, vomiting, tachycardia). This statement (A) is true : because the inhibition of the enzyme is irreversible, its action persists for several days (up to a week) after the last dose, until new enzyme is synthesized. It should only be given to highly motivated patients who are committed to abstinence, not those with doubtful motivation (B is false). Disulfiram is an aversive agent; it does not reduce the underlying desire or craving for alcohol. Naltrexone and acamprosate are anti-craving drugs (C is false). Therefore, only statement A is true.

Question 37

What is the preferred treatment for a condition characterized by subepidermal blisters and neutrophilic infiltration?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Dapsone The histopathological finding of subepidermal blisters (or microabscesses) filled with neutrophils in the dermal papillae is the classic feature of dermatitis herpetiformis (DH). DH is an intensely pruritic, autoimmune blistering skin disease that is a cutaneous manifestation of celiac disease. The treatment of choice for dermatitis herpetiformis is the drug dapsone. Dapsone provides rapid and dramatic relief from the intense itching and blistering, often within 24-48 hours. Its mechanism of action in DH is thought to be related to its ability to inhibit neutrophil chemotaxis and function. While a strict gluten-free diet is essential for long-term management of the underlying celiac disease, dapsone is the first-line pharmacological agent for controlling the acute skin manifestations. Rituximab, cyclosporine, and azathioprine are other immunosuppressants used for different autoimmune conditions.

Question 38

The Nigro regimen is used in the treatment of which condition?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Carcinoma of the anal canal The Nigro regimen is the standard, definitive treatment protocol for squamous cell carcinoma of the anal canal. It is a combined modality chemoradiation therapy that has transformed the management of this disease, allowing for sphincter preservation and avoiding the need for radical surgery (abdominoperineal resection) in the majority of cases. The original regimen, developed by Dr. Norman Nigro, consists of external beam radiation therapy delivered to the primary tumor and regional lymph nodes, given concurrently with chemotherapy. The classic chemotherapy agents used are mitomycin-C and 5-fluorouracil (5-FU). This combined approach is highly effective, achieving complete remission in over 80% of patients with localized disease. The Nigro regimen is specific to anal cancer and is not used for colon or bladder cancer.

Question 39

What is the mechanism through which anti- migraine drugs can produce angina-like symptoms?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

5-HT agonist The primary classes of drugs used for the acute treatment of migraine are the triptans (e.g., sumatriptan) and the ergot alkaloids (e.g., ergotamine). The mechanism of action of both classes involves agonism at serotonin (5-HT) receptors, specifically the 5-HT1B and 5-HT1D subtypes. Their therapeutic effect is mediated by constricting dilated intracranial blood vessels and inhibiting the release of pro-inflammatory neuropeptides in the trigeminal system. However, these drugs are not entirely selective, and their agonist activity at 5-HT1B receptors located on coronary arteries can cause vasoconstriction of the coronary arteries. In susceptible individuals, particularly those with underlying coronary artery disease, this vasoconstriction can be significant enough to induce myocardial ischemia, leading to angina-like symptoms such as chest pain, tightness, or pressure. This is a well-known and important potential side effect of these drugs.

Question 41

Lignocaine. Myasthenia Gravis (MG) is an autoimmune disorder where antibodies block or destroy acetylcholine receptors at the neuromuscular junction. Question 42

The HPV vaccine is based on which viral proteins?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

L1 The currently available Human papillomavirus vaccine is based on the L1 major capsid protein of the human papillomavirus (HPV). The L1 protein has the ability to self-assemble into virus-like particles (VLPs). These VLPs resemble the outer shell of the virus but do not contain viral DNA, making them non-infectious and safe. When administered, these L1-based VLPs stimulate a strong neutralizing antibody response. The antibodies prevent HPV from attaching to and infecting epithelial cells, thereby providing protection against cervical cancer and other HPV-related cancers. The early proteins E6 and E7 are oncogenic proteins responsible for malignant transformation (they inactivate tumor suppressor proteins p53 and Rb). However, they are not used in current prophylactic vaccines because they are expressed after infection and are associated with oncogenic activity. Therefore, HPV vaccines such as Gardasil and Cervarix are based on the L1 capsid protein, not E6 or E7.

Question 43

Which of the following methods can be used for therapeutic drug monitoring in a patient?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

HPLC Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM) is the clinical practice of measuring the plasma concentration of a specific drug to maintain it within a target therapeutic range. This is essential for drugs with a narrow therapeutic index, such as certain antiepileptics (e.g., phenytoin), antibiotics (e.g., vancomycin), and immunosuppressants (e.g., tacrolimus). Several analytical techniques are used for this purpose. High-Performance Liquid Chromatography (HPLC) is a powerful and widely used technique in clinical laboratories for TDM. It works by separating the components of a mixture (like plasma) based on their physicochemical properties, allowing for the precise and accurate quantification of the specific drug of interest. While immunoassays are also very common, HPLC is a gold-standard reference method for many drugs. Spectrophotometry (C) is less specific, and NMR (B) and flow cytometry (D) are not used for routine TDM.

Question 44

A psychotic patient presented with voluntary purposeless movements and was observed to stand still in the ward with negativism and waxy flexibility. What is the appropriate medical management?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Lorazepam The patient's symptoms—purposeless movements, immobility (stupor), negativism (resistance to instructions), and waxy flexibility (maintaining an imposed posture)—are the classic motor signs of catatonia. Catatonia is a psychomotor syndrome that can occur in the context of psychotic, mood, or medical disorders. The first-line medical treatment for catatonia is a benzodiazepine. A "lorazepam challenge" is often used both diagnostically and therapeutically. Administration of an intravenous or intramuscular dose of lorazepam (typically 1-2 mg) often produces a rapid and dramatic, albeit temporary, improvement in the catatonic symptoms, confirming the diagnosis and guiding further treatment. High doses of benzodiazepines are the mainstay of therapy. While antipsychotics like haloperidol (A) or risperidone (D) may be needed to treat the underlying psychosis, they can sometimes worsen catatonia and carry a risk of neuroleptic malignant syndrome.

Question 45

Which of the following is true regarding the treatment of gout?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Aspirin is not used because it increases uric acid levels. The management of acute gout focuses on rapidly suppressing the intense inflammation. Aspirin is specifically avoided in the treatment of gout. Question 46

A patient taking antiepileptic medication is diagnosed with neurocysticercosis. What should be the next steps in this patient’s management?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Steroid followed by antihelminthic drug Neurocysticercosis is a parasitic infection of the central nervous system caused by the larval cysts of Taenia solium. When antihelminthic drugs (like albendazole or praziquantel) are administered, they kill the cysticerci within the brain. The death of the parasite triggers a significant host inflammatory response around the degenerating cyst. This inflammation can lead to a dangerous increase in cerebral edema, worsening of neurological symptoms, and an increased risk of seizures. To mitigate this inflammatory response, it is standard practice to administer corticosteroids (e.g., dexamethasone) before or concurrently with the initiation of the antihelminthic drug. The antiepileptic medication should be continued throughout this period to control seizures. Therefore, the correct sequence is to start steroids first to suppress inflammation, and then begin the anti-parasitic treatment. FORENSIC MEDICINE

Question 47

You are an assistant surgeon working in a CHC and 13 year old rape victim is brought to you by her parents with history of sexual assault by 22- year old male. The police from the concerned is not accompanying the victim. The victim is refusing to give consent for the medical examination and any further tests. What should be done in the above given scenario? 1 Start treatment for STD and pregnancy 2 Inform the police 3 Record refusal of consent 4 Take vaginal samples 5 Start medical examination

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

1,2,3 This is a 13-year-old minor and therefore a child under the Protection of Children from Sexual Offences Act (POCSO Act). Sexual assault of a minor is a mandatory reportable offence, and healthcare providers are legally obligated to inform the police. Hence, informing the police (2) is compulsory, even if police have not accompanied the victim.
