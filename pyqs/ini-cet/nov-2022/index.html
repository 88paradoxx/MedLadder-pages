Question 1

Arrange the following structures in the correct order as they participate in the direct pathway of the basal ganglia : 1 Striatum 2 Globus pallidus 3 Substantia nigra 4 Thalamus 5 Cortex

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

5,1,2,3,4 The direct pathway of the basal ganglia is a crucial circuit that facilitates the initiation of voluntary movement. The sequence begins with excitatory glutamatergic signals from the cerebral cortex (5) to the striatum (1) (caudate and putamen). The striatum then sends inhibitory GABAergic projections directly to the internal segment of the globus pallidus (GPi) and the substantia nigra pars reticulata (SNr) (2 & 3). This inhibitory signal from the striatum suppresses the tonic inhibitory output of the GPi/SNr complex. The GPi/SNr normally inhibits the thalamus (4). By inhibiting the GPi/SNr, the striatum effectively disinhibits the thalamus, allowing it to send excitatory signals back to the motor cortex, thus promoting movement. Therefore, the correct sequence of information flow in the direct pathway is Cortex → Striatum → Globus Pallidus Internus/Substantia Nigra Pars Reticulata → Thalamus → Cortex. The provided option C reflects this pathway core sequence.

Question 11

In a pressure-volume curve, what does a reading of zero pressure imply?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Functional residual capacity The pressure-volume curve of the respiratory system plots lung volume against pressure. The pressure referred to is the transthoracic or trans-respiratory system pressure, which is the pressure difference between the airway opening (mouth) and the body surface. A reading of zero pressure signifies the point where the respiratory system is at rest, with no muscular effort being exerted. At this equilibrium point, the inward elastic recoil of the lungs is perfectly balanced by the outward elastic recoil of the chest wall. The volume of air remaining in the lungs at this specific point is the Functional Residual Capacity (FRC). It is the sum of the expiratory reserve volume (ERV) and the residual volume (RV). Residual volume (A) is the air left after maximal forced expiration. Tidal volume (B) is the air exchanged during normal breathing. Inspiratory reserve volume (D) is the extra air that can be inhaled after a normal inspiration.

Question 14

Where is the signaling sequence located in a type 2 membrane protein that has its N-terminal facing the cytoplasm?

Inside the membrane Type II transmembrane proteins are single-pass proteins oriented with their N-terminus in the cytoplasm and their C-terminus in the extracellular space or organelle lumen. Correct answer: B

6,4,2,3,1,5 Vomiting is a coordinated reflex controlled by the vomiting center in the medulla. First, reverse peristalsis in the small intestine (6) occurs, pushing intestinal contents back toward the stomach. Next, the pyloric sphincter relaxes (4), allowing duodenal contents to enter the stomach. Question 20

What is the product of deamination of methylated cytosine?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Thymine Deamination is the removal of an amine group from a molecule. In the context of DNA bases, spontaneous deamination is a common form of DNA damage. When an unmethylated cytosine undergoes deamination, it is converted into uracil (A). This is easily recognized by DNA repair enzymes (uracil DNA glycosylase) as an abnormal base in DNA and is efficiently removed. However, in many organisms, cytosine can be methylated at the 5th carbon position to form 5-methylcytosine, an important epigenetic mark. When 5-methylcytosine undergoes spontaneous deamination, it is converted into thymine. Since thymine is a normal DNA base, this C-to-T transition is not as easily recognized by repair systems, making methylated cytosine residues "hotspots" for mutation. This process is a significant source of single nucleotide polymorphisms in the genome.

Question 21

What is the cause of elevated H+ ions in the intermembrane space of mitochondria?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Increased ATP production During cellular respiration, the electron transport chain (ETC) located on the inner mitochondrial membrane actively pumps hydrogen ions (H ⁺ ) from the mitochondrial matrix into the intermembrane space. This process occurs as electrons pass through complexes I, III, and IV. The energy released from electron transfer is used to pump protons against their concentration gradient, resulting in an accumulation of H ⁺ ions in the intermembrane space. This creates a proton gradient and an electrochemical potential difference known as the proton motive force. The elevated concentration of H ⁺ ions in the intermembrane space is essential for ATP synthesis. When protons flow back into the matrix through ATP synthase, the energy released drives the conversion of ADP and inorganic phosphate into ATP. Thus, increased ATP production is associated with active electron transport and proton pumping, which leads to elevated H ⁺ concentration in the intermembrane space. Therefore, option (B) is correct.

Question 23

Rise in heart rate According to the Neonatal Resuscitation Program (NRP) guidelines, the single most important and reliable indicator that positive pressure ventilation (PPV) is effectively oxygenating the baby's vital organs is a prompt rise in the heart rate. Question 119

Arrange the following cells in the order they are found from the basal layer to the lumen within the seminiferous tubules : 1 Spermatogonia 2 Primary spermatocyte 3 Spermatid 4 Spermatozoa 5 Myoepithelial cell

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

5,1,2,3,4 The process of spermatogenesis occurs within the seminiferous tubules of the testes, with cells arranged in a highly organized fashion from the periphery towards the lumen. The outermost layer of the tubule is the basement membrane, which is surrounded by contractile peritubular myoid cells (often called myoepithelial cells in this context). Immediately inside the basement membrane, in the basal compartment, are the most immature germ cells, the spermatogonia. As these cells divide and differentiate, they move towards the adluminal compartment. Spermatogonia develop into primary spermatocytes, which undergo meiosis I to become secondary spermatocytes. These then undergo meiosis II to become spermatids. Finally, through the process of spermiogenesis, the round spermatids mature and elongate to form spermatozoa, which are then released into the lumen of the tubule. Therefore, the correct sequence from the outermost layer to the lumen is Myoid cell → Spermatogonia → Primary spermatocyte → Spermatid → Spermatozoa.

Question 125

Kassowitz rule is related to :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Congenital syphilis Kassowitz's Law is an old clinical observation related to the transmission and severity of congenital syphilis. The law states that the risk of transmission of syphilis from an untreated mother to her fetus, and the severity of the resulting congenital infection, are highest when the maternal infection is recent and decrease with the duration of the maternal infection. In other words, a mother who acquires primary or secondary syphilis just before or during pregnancy is much more likely to have a severely affected infant (e.g., stillbirth or early congenital syphilis) than a mother who has had untreated latent syphilis for many years. While not an absolute rule, it reflects the principle that the spirochetemia is highest during the early stages of syphilis, leading to a greater likelihood of placental invasion and fetal infection.

Question 126

What is the hormone secreted by the placenta?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

hCG The placenta is a temporary but highly active endocrine organ that secretes numerous hormones essential for maintaining pregnancy. The most well-known and earliest hormone produced by the placenta is human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG). hCG is a glycoprotein hormone that is secreted by the syncytiotrophoblast cells of the developing placenta shortly after implantation. What is the hormone secreted by the placenta? A 29-year-old woman with a past history of mania is currently experiencing hypomanic symptoms and is planning a pregnancy. Which medication should be avoided because of its significant risk of fetal malformations?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Valproate The management of bipolar disorder during pregnancy is complex, requiring a careful balance between controlling maternal mood symptoms and minimizing fetal teratogenic risk. Among the mood stabilizers, valproate (valproic acid) is associated with the highest risk of major congenital malformations. Exposure during the first trimester, particularly between days 17 and 30 of gestation, carries a significant risk (around 10%) of causing neural tube defects, such as spina bifida. It is also associated with an increased risk of cardiac defects, craniofacial abnormalities, and long-term neurodevelopmental problems, including lower IQ scores and an increased risk of autism spectrum disorder. Because of this high teratogenic potential, valproate is generally contraindicated for use in women of childbearing potential unless other treatments are ineffective. Lithium also carries a risk (Ebstein's anomaly) but it is lower than that of valproate.
