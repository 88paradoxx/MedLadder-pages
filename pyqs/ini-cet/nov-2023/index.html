Question 8

What contributes to the zero phase of the action potential in the SA node?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Calcium (Ca) only The action potential of the sinoatrial (SA) node, the heart's natural pacemaker, differs significantly from that of ventricular myocytes. The upstroke, or phase 0, of the SA node action potential is primarily mediated by the influx of calcium ions (Ca² ⁺ ) through L-type calcium channels. This is a slower process compared to the rapid sodium influx that characterizes phase 0 in ventricular and atrial muscle cells, which is why SA nodal action potentials have a less steep upstroke. While sodium influx through the "funny" current (If) is crucial for the pacemaker potential (phase 4) that brings the cell to threshold, the depolarization phase itself (phase 0) is calcium-dependent. Potassium efflux is responsible for repolarization (phase 3).

Question 9

With respect to glucose entry into cells, which organ or tissue does not rely on insulin for glucose uptake?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Adipose tissue Most tissues in the body require insulin for glucose uptake, but some have insulin-independent mechanisms. This question asks to identify the tissue that is insulin-dependent. Adipose tissue and skeletal muscle primarily use the GLUT4 transporter, which is insulin-sensitive. In the presence of insulin, GLUT4 translocates from intracellular vesicles to the cell membrane to facilitate glucose entry. In contrast, red blood cells (RBCs) use GLUT1, the brain uses GLUT1 and GLUT3, the pancreas (beta-cells) uses GLUT2, and hepatocytes (liver cells) use GLUT2. All of these transporters (GLUT1, GLUT2, GLUT3) are insulin-independent, meaning they can take up glucose from the blood without the need for insulin signaling. Therefore, adipose tissue is the exception as its glucose uptake is critically dependent on insulin.

Question 10

What is the main mechanism through which parathyroid hormone (PTH) increases blood calcium levels?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

All are true Parathyroid hormone (PTH) is the principal regulator of calcium homeostasis in the body, and it employs multiple mechanisms to increase serum calcium levels. Firstly, PTH acts directly on bone to stimulate osteoclast activity, which leads to the resorption of bone matrix and the release of calcium and phosphate into the bloodstream. Secondly, PTH acts on the kidneys to increase the reabsorption of calcium in the distal convoluted tubules, thereby reducing urinary calcium excretion. Thirdly, PTH stimulates the renal enzyme 1-alpha-hydroxylase, which is responsible for converting inactive 25-hydroxyvitamin D into its active form, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol). Calcitriol then acts on the intestines to significantly increase the absorption of dietary calcium. Since all three of these mechanisms are primary actions of PTH that contribute to elevating blood calcium, all the statements are true.

Question 11

Which component of the nerve fiber provides the motor innervation to the muscle spindle?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Gamma Muscle spindles are proprioceptive sensory organs within skeletal muscles that detect changes in muscle length and the rate of change in length. While they are primarily sensory, their intrafusal muscle fibers (specialized fibers within the spindle capsule) receive motor innervation to regulate their sensitivity. This motor supply is provided by gamma motor neurons. The axons of gamma motor neurons terminate on the polar ends of the intrafusal fibers, causing them to contract. This contraction stretches the central, non-contractile sensory region of the spindle, thereby increasing its sensitivity to stretch of the main muscle. In contrast, alpha motor neurons (A) innervate the extrafusal muscle fibers, which are the main force-generating fibers of the muscle. This co-activation of alpha and gamma motor neurons allows the spindle to remain sensitive to stretch across a wide range of muscle lengths.

Question 14

Hybridoma technology uses which of the following?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Purine Salvage pathway Hybridoma technology is a method for producing large numbers of identical antibodies (monoclonal antibodies). It involves fusing antibody-producing B cells with immortal myeloma (cancerous plasma) cells. A crucial step in this process is the selection of fused hybridoma cells from the unfused myeloma cells. This is accomplished using the HAT medium (Hypoxanthine-Aminopterin-Thymidine). Aminopterin blocks the de novo purine synthesis pathway. This forces the cells to rely on the purine salvage pathway to synthesize nucleotides and survive. The myeloma cells used are specifically chosen because they are deficient in an enzyme of the salvage pathway, hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT). Therefore, unfused myeloma cells cannot survive in the HAT medium. However, when fused with a B cell, the resulting hybridoma inherits a functional salvage pathway from the B cell and can thus survive and proliferate, allowing for the selection of the desired antibody-producing hybrid cells.

Question 15

Which of the following is not involved in the repair of DNA double-strand breaks?

Correct answer: A

ATP DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) are highly cytotoxic lesions that are primarily repaired by two major pathways: non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) and homologous recombination (HR). Question 16

The active site of an enzyme is mostly complementary to:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Substrate Although enzymes bind substrates, the active site is most complementary to the transition state, not the substrate itself. According to modern enzyme theory (transition state theory), enzymes catalyze reactions by stabilizing the transition state — the high-energy, unstable intermediate formed during conversion of substrate to product. The active site is shaped and chemically arranged to bind this transition state more tightly than either the substrate or the product. If the active site were perfectly complementary to the substrate, the enzyme would bind the substrate too tightly and would not efficiently convert it into product. Instead, by preferentially stabilizing the transition state, the enzyme lowers the activation energy and speeds up the reaction. Allosteric activators bind at a different regulatory site, not the active site. The product binds less strongly because it must be released after the reaction. Therefore, the active site is most complementary to the transition state, making option (D) correct.

Question 20

Following class switching, a specific antibody class is generated, yet IgM and IgD continue to be co-expressed. Which mechanism accounts for this?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Alternate RNA splicing Naive mature B cells, before encountering an antigen, co-express both IgM and IgD on their surface. This is not a result of class switching, which is a later, irreversible DNA recombination event that changes the constant region of the heavy chain. Instead, the co-expression of IgM and IgD is achieved through alternate RNA splicing. A single long primary RNA transcript is synthesized, which contains the variable region (VDJ) along with the constant regions for both the mu (μ for IgM) and delta ( δ for IgD) heavy chains. This primary transcript is then processed in two different ways. Depending on which polyadenylation sites are used and how the RNA is spliced, either the Cμ or the C δ segment is joined to the VDJ segment, resulting in mature mRNAs that code for either IgM or IgD heavy chains, respectively. Allelic exclusion, somatic hypermutation, and affinity maturation are other important processes in B cell development but are not responsible for this specific co-expression.

Question 21

A patient shows reduced serum transferrin saturation. What is the most probable underlying condition?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Iron Deficiency Anemia Transferrin saturation is a key laboratory parameter used to assess iron status. It is calculated as (Serum Iron / Total Iron-Binding Capacity) × 100%. In Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA), the body's iron stores are depleted. This leads to a low serum iron level. Simultaneously, the liver increases the production of transferrin (the protein that transports iron), leading to a high Total Iron-Binding Capacity (TIBC). The combination of low serum iron and high TIBC results in a significantly low transferrin saturation percentage, often below 15%. In contrast, conditions of iron overload like hemochromatosis would show a high transferrin saturation. Pernicious anemia is a type of vitamin B12 deficiency anemia and does not directly affect iron saturation levels in this manner.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Supracondylar fracture The "three bony point relationship" of the elbow refers to the alignment of the medial epicondyle, the lateral epicondyle, and the tip of the olecranon. In a normally extended elbow, these three points form a straight line. When the elbow is flexed to 90 degrees, they form an isosceles triangle. This relationship is disrupted in an elbow dislocation, as the olecranon (part of the ulna) moves out of its normal articulation with the humerus. However, in a supracondylar fracture, the fracture line is located on the distal humerus above the epicondyles. Therefore, the relationship between the two epicondyles and the olecranon remains intact because the distal fragment (containing the epicondyles) moves along with the ulna and radius. Fractures of the epicondyles themselves (options A and B) can cause local swelling that obscures the landmarks, but the fundamental geometric relationship of the three points is preserved relative to the olecranon. Supracondylar fracture is the classic example where this relationship is maintained despite significant deformity.

Question 137

How do sweat chloride levels and pancreatic chloride levels typically appear in individuals with cystic fibrosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Chlorine transport decrease in sweat Cystic Fibrosis (CF) is caused by mutations in the CFTR (Cystic Fibrosis Transmembrane Conductance Regulator) gene, which codes for a chloride ion channel. The function of this channel differs by organ. In the ducts of sweat glands, the CFTR channel's primary role is to reabsorb chloride ions from the sweat back into the body. In CF, this channel is defective, leading to decreased reabsorption (transport) of chloride. This failure to reabsorb chloride results in a high concentration of chloride in the sweat, which is the basis of the sweat chloride test for diagnosing CF. Therefore, statement B is correct. In contrast, in the pancreas, the CFTR channel normally secretes chloride into the pancreatic ducts, which helps draw water into the secretions. In CF, defective chloride secretion leads to thick, dehydrated pancreatic secretions, which obstruct the ducts. This means chloride concentration in pancreatic secretions is decreased, not increased or normal.

In a patient with uterine inversion in whom the placenta is still attached, what is the most appropriate immediate management step?

Reposition the uterus inside without removing the placenta Acute uterine inversion is a rare but life-threatening obstetric emergency. Question 143

Causes of non-immune hydrops fetalis are all except:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

ABO incompatibility Hydrops fetalis is the excessive accumulation of fluid in fetal extravascular compartments and body cavities. It is broadly divided into immune and non-immune types. Non-immune hydrops accounts for about 90% of cases and has numerous causes, including cardiac abnormalities (structural or arrhythmic), chromosomal anomalies (e.g., Turner syndrome, trisomies), and infections (e.g., Parvovirus B19, CMV). Question 151

Two doctors were asked to interpret 100 X-rays, and the resulting Kappa value is 0.71. How should this be interpreted?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Good The Cohen's Kappa coefficient ( κ ) is a statistical measure of inter-rater reliability or agreement for categorical items. It accounts for the possibility of agreement occurring by chance. The interpretation of the Kappa value can vary slightly, but the Landis and Koch scale is widely accepted: 1. κ ≤ 0: Poor agreement 2. κ = 0.01–0.20: Slight agreement 3. κ = 0.21–0.40: Fair agreement 4. κ = 0.41–0.60: Moderate agreement 5. κ = 0.61–0.80: Substantial agreement 6. κ = 0.81–1.00: Almost perfect agreement A Kappa value of 1.0 indicates perfect agreement, while a value of -1.0 indicates perfect disagreement. A value of 0.71 falls squarely within the "Substantial agreement" range, which is often colloquially referred to as "Good" agreement. It indicates that the two doctors have a high level of consistency in their X-ray interpretations, well beyond what would be expected by chance.
