Show answer and explanation

a-2,b-4, c-3,d-1 The pectoralis major is a powerful muscle of the upper chest, primarily responsible for flexion, adduction, and medial rotation of the humerus at the shoulder joint. Question 3

Which of the following drains directly into the inferior vena cava (IVC)?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Left renal vein The venous drainage pattern on the left and right sides of the body is asymmetrical. The left renal vein is a large vein that travels from the left kidney to drain directly into the inferior vena cava (IVC). It is notable for receiving blood from several other veins on the left side, including the left gonadal (testicular or ovarian) vein and the left adrenal (suprarenal) vein. Therefore, the left gonadal vein and left adrenal vein do not drain directly into the IVC but into the left renal vein first. In contrast, the right gonadal and right adrenal veins typically drain directly into the IVC. The right ascending lumbar vein usually drains into the azygos vein system.

Question 5

In which region do the fibers of the internal oblique and transversus abdominis form part of the inguinal canal wall?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Roof The inguinal canal is an oblique passage through the lower part of the anterior abdominal wall. Its walls are formed by different layers of the abdominal musculature. The roof (superior wall) of the inguinal canal is formed by the arching lower fibers of the internal oblique and transversus abdominis muscles as they pass superiorly and medially to insert into the pubic crest and pectineal line, forming the conjoint tendon. The anterior wall is mainly formed by the aponeurosis of the external oblique muscle. The posterior wall is formed by the transversalis fascia and the conjoint tendon. The floor is formed by the inguinal ligament, reinforced medially by the lacunar ligament. Therefore, the internal oblique and transversus abdominis muscles form the roof of the canal.

Question 8

What contributes to the zero phase of the action potential in the SA node?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Calcium (Ca) only The action potential of the sinoatrial (SA) node, the heart's natural pacemaker, differs significantly from that of ventricular myocytes. The upstroke, or phase 0, of the SA node action potential is primarily mediated by the influx of calcium ions (Ca² ⁺ ) through L-type calcium channels. This is a slower process compared to the rapid sodium influx that characterizes phase 0 in ventricular and atrial muscle cells, which is why SA nodal action potentials have a less steep upstroke. While sodium influx through the "funny" current (If) is crucial for the pacemaker potential (phase 4) that brings the cell to threshold, the depolarization phase itself (phase 0) is calcium-dependent. Potassium efflux is responsible for repolarization (phase 3).

Question 9

With respect to glucose entry into cells, which organ or tissue does not rely on insulin for glucose uptake?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Adipose tissue Most tissues in the body require insulin for glucose uptake, but some have insulin-independent mechanisms. This question asks to identify the tissue that is insulin-dependent. Adipose tissue and skeletal muscle primarily use the GLUT4 transporter, which is insulin-sensitive. In the presence of insulin, GLUT4 translocates from intracellular vesicles to the cell membrane to facilitate glucose entry. In contrast, red blood cells (RBCs) use GLUT1, the brain uses GLUT1 and GLUT3, the pancreas (beta-cells) uses GLUT2, and hepatocytes (liver cells) use GLUT2. All of these transporters (GLUT1, GLUT2, GLUT3) are insulin-independent, meaning they can take up glucose from the blood without the need for insulin signaling. Therefore, adipose tissue is the exception as its glucose uptake is critically dependent on insulin.

Question 10

What is the main mechanism through which parathyroid hormone (PTH) increases blood calcium levels?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

All are true Parathyroid hormone (PTH) is the principal regulator of calcium homeostasis in the body, and it employs multiple mechanisms to increase serum calcium levels. Firstly, PTH acts directly on bone to stimulate osteoclast activity, which leads to the resorption of bone matrix and the release of calcium and phosphate into the bloodstream. Secondly, PTH acts on the kidneys to increase the reabsorption of calcium in the distal convoluted tubules, thereby reducing urinary calcium excretion. Thirdly, PTH stimulates the renal enzyme 1-alpha-hydroxylase, which is responsible for converting inactive 25-hydroxyvitamin D into its active form, 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D (calcitriol). Calcitriol then acts on the intestines to significantly increase the absorption of dietary calcium. Since all three of these mechanisms are primary actions of PTH that contribute to elevating blood calcium, all the statements are true.

Question 11

Which component of the nerve fiber provides the motor innervation to the muscle spindle?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Gamma Muscle spindles are proprioceptive sensory organs within skeletal muscles that detect changes in muscle length and the rate of change in length. While they are primarily sensory, their intrafusal muscle fibers (specialized fibers within the spindle capsule) receive motor innervation to regulate their sensitivity. This motor supply is provided by gamma motor neurons. The axons of gamma motor neurons terminate on the polar ends of the intrafusal fibers, causing them to contract. This contraction stretches the central, non-contractile sensory region of the spindle, thereby increasing its sensitivity to stretch of the main muscle. In contrast, alpha motor neurons (A) innervate the extrafusal muscle fibers, which are the main force-generating fibers of the muscle. This co-activation of alpha and gamma motor neurons allows the spindle to remain sensitive to stretch across a wide range of muscle lengths.

1,4 A cough is a protective reflex that helps clear the airways of irritants, mucus, and foreign particles. The process begins with a deep inspiration, followed by the closure of the glottis. Question 14

Hybridoma technology uses which of the following?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Purine Salvage pathway Hybridoma technology is a method for producing large numbers of identical antibodies (monoclonal antibodies). It involves fusing antibody-producing B cells with immortal myeloma (cancerous plasma) cells. A crucial step in this process is the selection of fused hybridoma cells from the unfused myeloma cells. This is accomplished using the HAT medium (Hypoxanthine-Aminopterin-Thymidine). Aminopterin blocks the de novo purine synthesis pathway. This forces the cells to rely on the purine salvage pathway to synthesize nucleotides and survive. The myeloma cells used are specifically chosen because they are deficient in an enzyme of the salvage pathway, hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT). Therefore, unfused myeloma cells cannot survive in the HAT medium. However, when fused with a B cell, the resulting hybridoma inherits a functional salvage pathway from the B cell and can thus survive and proliferate, allowing for the selection of the desired antibody-producing hybrid cells.

Question 15

Which of the following is not involved in the repair of DNA double-strand breaks?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

ATP DNA double-strand breaks (DSBs) are highly cytotoxic lesions that are primarily repaired by two major pathways: non-homologous end joining (NHEJ) and homologous recombination (HR). The proteins listed are key players in these pathways. ATM (Ataxia-Telangiectasia Mutated) is a sensor kinase that detects DSBs and initiates the signaling cascade for repair. Ku70 (along with Ku80) is a critical component of the NHEJ pathway; it binds directly to the broken DNA ends and recruits other repair factors. RAD51 is a central protein in the HR pathway, where it forms a filament on the single-stranded DNA and mediates the search for and invasion of a homologous template sequence. While ATP is required as an energy source for the enzymatic activities of many DNA repair proteins (e.g., kinases, ligases, helicases), it is not a structural or signaling component of the repair machinery itself in the same way as ATM, Ku70, and RAD51. Therefore, it does not directly "contribute" to the repair in a mechanistic sense but rather fuels the process.

Question 16

The active site of an enzyme is mostly complementary to:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Substrate Although enzymes bind substrates, the active site is most complementary to the transition state, not the substrate itself. According to modern enzyme theory (transition state theory), enzymes catalyze reactions by stabilizing the transition state — the high-energy, unstable intermediate formed during conversion of substrate to product. The active site is shaped and chemically arranged to bind this transition state more tightly than either the substrate or the product. If the active site were perfectly complementary to the substrate, the enzyme would bind the substrate too tightly and would not efficiently convert it into product. Instead, by preferentially stabilizing the transition state, the enzyme lowers the activation energy and speeds up the reaction. Allosteric activators bind at a different regulatory site, not the active site. The product binds less strongly because it must be released after the reaction. Therefore, the active site is most complementary to the transition state, making option (D) correct.

Question 18

2,3-BPG binds to which part of hemoglobin?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Central part of hemoglobin 2,3-Bisphosphoglycerate (2,3-BPG) binds in the central cavity of deoxyhemoglobin, between the two β -chains of the hemoglobin tetramer. This central pocket is present in the T (tense) state of hemoglobin. 2,3-BPG is negatively charged and interacts with positively charged amino acid residues (such as lysine and histidine) located in the central region of the globin chains. By binding in this cavity, 2,3-BPG stabilizes the T-state of hemoglobin, thereby reducing its affinity for oxygen and promoting oxygen release to tissues. It does not bind to the heme portion directly. Instead, it binds to the globin chains within the central cavity, not on the outer surface. Thus, the binding site of 2,3-BPG is the central part of hemoglobin, making option (A) correct. PATHOLOGY

Question 20

Following class switching, a specific antibody class is generated, yet IgM and IgD continue to be co-expressed. Which mechanism accounts for this?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Alternate RNA splicing Naive mature B cells, before encountering an antigen, co-express both IgM and IgD on their surface. This is not a result of class switching, which is a later, irreversible DNA recombination event that changes the constant region of the heavy chain. Instead, the co-expression of IgM and IgD is achieved through alternate RNA splicing. A single long primary RNA transcript is synthesized, which contains the variable region (VDJ) along with the constant regions for both the mu (μ for IgM) and delta ( δ for IgD) heavy chains. This primary transcript is then processed in two different ways. Depending on which polyadenylation sites are used and how the RNA is spliced, either the Cμ or the C δ segment is joined to the VDJ segment, resulting in mature mRNAs that code for either IgM or IgD heavy chains, respectively. Allelic exclusion, somatic hypermutation, and affinity maturation are other important processes in B cell development but are not responsible for this specific co-expression.

Question 21

A patient shows reduced serum transferrin saturation. What is the most probable underlying condition?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Iron Deficiency Anemia Transferrin saturation is a key laboratory parameter used to assess iron status. It is calculated as (Serum Iron / Total Iron-Binding Capacity) × 100%. In Iron Deficiency Anemia (IDA), the body's iron stores are depleted. This leads to a low serum iron level. Simultaneously, the liver increases the production of transferrin (the protein that transports iron), leading to a high Total Iron-Binding Capacity (TIBC). The combination of low serum iron and high TIBC results in a significantly low transferrin saturation percentage, often below 15%. In contrast, conditions of iron overload like hemochromatosis would show a high transferrin saturation. Sideroblastic anemia can have normal or high saturation because iron is available but cannot be properly incorporated into heme. Pernicious anemia is a type of vitamin B12 deficiency anemia and does not directly affect iron saturation levels in this manner.

Question 26

Following an orchidectomy, the specimen reveals the presence of Reinke’s crystals. What is the most probable diagnosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Leydig Cell Tumor Reinke's crystals are distinctive, eosinophilic, rod-shaped or rhomboid cytoplasmic inclusions. Their presence is a pathognomonic histological feature of a Leydig cell tumor. Leydig cells are the testosterone-producing cells of the testes, and tumors arising from them are a type of sex cord-stromal tumor. While not present in all Leydig cell tumors, when Reinke's crystals are identified, they are diagnostic. They are not found in germ cell tumors like seminoma or yolk sac tumor, nor are they a feature of metastatic tumors to the testis.

Simultaneously, the liver increases the production of transferrin (the protein that transports iron), leading to a high Total Iron-Binding Capacity (TIBC). The combination of low serum iron and high TIBC results in a significantly low transferrin saturation percentage, often below 15%. In contrast, conditions of iron overload like hemochromatosis and hemosiderosis would show a high transferrin saturation. Which recently approved anti-diabetic medication is indicated for patients who have heart failure with reduced ejection fraction and decreased urine output?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Empagliflozin Empagliflozin is a sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor. Question 29

Which antigen is present in the HPV vaccine?

Correct answer: C

Question 30

Which drug exerts its effect by inhibiting ATP- binding cassette (ABC) transporters?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Verapamil ATP-binding cassette (ABC) transporters are a family of transmembrane proteins that transport various substrates across cell membranes. One of the most well-known is P-glycoprotein (P-gp), encoded by the ABCB1 gene, which functions as an efflux pump that can extrude drugs from cells, contributing to multidrug resistance in cancer. Certain drugs can inhibit the function of these transporters. Verapamil, a calcium channel blocker, is a well-known inhibitor of P-glycoprotein. By blocking the efflux pump, verapamil can increase the intracellular concentration of other drugs that are P-gp substrates, which has been studied as a strategy to overcome chemotherapy resistance. While nifedipine and diltiazem are also calcium channel blockers, verapamil is the most classic and potent inhibitor of P-gp among the options. Tacrolimus is an immunosuppressant and is a substrate, not an inhibitor, of P-gp.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Captopril The patient's symptoms are classic for congestive heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. Similarly, digoxin can improve symptoms and reduce hospitalizations but does not have a proven mortality benefit. Question 32

Which of the following is the disadvantage of oxcarbazepine over carbamazepine?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Dilutional hyponatremia Oxcarbazepine is a structural analog of carbamazepine, another antiepileptic drug. While oxcarbazepine is generally better tolerated, with a lower incidence of severe side effects like hepatotoxicity and skin reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome), it has one notable disadvantage. This is thought to be due to an increased sensitivity of the renal tubules to antidiuretic hormone (ADH), leading to water retention and dilution of serum sodium. This side effect is particularly important to monitor in elderly patients or those on other medications that can affect sodium balance, such as diuretics. Thrombocytopenia is a potential side effect of both drugs, but not a specific disadvantage of oxcarbazepine.

Question 33

All of the following drugs are used in thyroid storm except?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Aspirin Thyroid storm is a life-threatening hypermetabolic state caused by excessive release of thyroid hormones. Management involves a multi-pronged approach. Beta-blockers like propranolol are used to control the adrenergic symptoms (tachycardia, tremor). Thionamides like propylthiouracil (PTU) or methimazole are used to block the synthesis of new thyroid hormones. Iodine solutions, such as Lugol's iodine or potassium iodide, are given at least one hour after the thionamide to block the release of pre-formed hormone from the thyroid gland (Wolff-Chaikoff effect). Corticosteroids are also used to reduce peripheral conversion of T4 to T3 and treat potential adrenal insufficiency. Aspirin, however, is contraindicated. Aspirin can displace thyroid hormone from its binding proteins, which can paradoxically increase the levels of free, active thyroid hormone in the circulation and worsen the storm. Acetaminophen is the preferred antipyretic.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Fostamatinib The question asks for a tyrosine kinase inhibitor used for isolated thrombocytopenia, such as Immune Thrombocytopenia (ITP). Fostamatinib is an oral spleen tyrosine kinase (Syk) inhibitor. In ITP, antibody-coated platelets are destroyed by macrophages in the spleen. Question 41

Which of the following substances was the East India Company smuggling to China in the 18th century?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Opium In the 18th and 19th centuries, the British East India Company engaged in a large-scale trade of opium, which was cultivated in British-controlled India, and smuggled it into China. Question 50

Which of the following substances is not used as a chelating agent for treating heavy metal poisoning?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Oximes Chelating agents are molecules that can form multiple bonds with a single metal ion, sequestering it and facilitating its excretion from the body.
