Question 1

What is the causative agent of anogenital wart?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Anogenital warts, also known as condylomata acuminata, are benign epithelial growths caused by infection with the Human Papillomavirus (HPV). HPVs are classified into high-risk (oncogenic) types and low-risk types. The vast majority (over 90%) of anogenital warts are caused by low-risk HPV types 6 and 11. Of these two, HPV 11 (B) is a very common cause. High-risk HPV types, such as HPV 16 and 18 (D), are the primary cause of cervical, anal, and other anogenital cancers, but they do not typically cause visible warts. HPV 2 (A) is a common cause of cutaneous warts on the hands and feet (verruca vulgaris). HPV 5 (C) is a type associated with epidermodysplasia verruciformis.

Question 3

A 0-year-old woman presents with easily rupturable, flaccid bullae on the skin. Histopathological examination of a skin biopsy shows a suprabasal cleft. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The clinical and histological findings are classic for Pemphigus Vulgaris. This is a severe autoimmune blistering disease characterized by autoantibodies against desmoglein-3, a protein component of desmosomes that holds epidermal cells together. This antibody attack leads to a loss of cell-to-cell adhesion (acantholysis) within the epidermis. Histologically, this results in an intra epidermal split just above the basal cell layer, which is described as a supra basal split. Clinically, this superficial split results in the formation of thin-roofed, flaccid bullae that rupture easily, leaving painful erosions (a positive Nikolsky sign). Pemphigus foliaceus (C) involves a more superficial split within the granular layer. Pemphigus vegetans (A) is a variant of vulgaris. Erythema multi forme (D) is a hypersensitivity reaction with a different histology.

Question 5

An obese woman presented with facial acne that did not improve despite appropriate medical treatment. What is the most likely underlying cause of her persistent acne?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The combination of obesity and persistent, treatment-resistant acne in a female should raise suspicion for an underlying endocrine disorder, specifically Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS). PCOS is a common hormonal disorder characterized by hyperandrogenism (excess male hormones), ovulatory dysfunction, and polycystic ovarian morphology. The excess androgens are responsible for the clinical signs of hirsutism (excess hair growth) and persistent acne. The acne is often inflammatory and located on the lower face and jawline. Obesity is also a very common feature of PCOS and contributes to insulin resistance, which can exacerbate the hypermodernism. While diet and cosmetics can influence acne, a persistent case unresponsive to standard treatment warrants investigation for PCOS.

Question 7

A 3-year-old primigravida lives in the same household as her school-going nephew, who has developed varicella infection. She visits a medical center and is found to be negative for varicella antibodies. Which of the following statements regarding her condition and management are correct?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Varicella-zoster virus (VZV) causes chickenpox (varicella) as its primary infection. After this infection, the virus becomes latent in the dorsal root ganglia. Immunity is conferred by the presence of anti-VZV antibodies. A person who is immune can later develop zoster (shingles) due to reactivation of this latent virus. This patient has a negative varicella antibody test, which means she has never had a primary infection with VZV and therefore has no immunity. As a non-immune individual, she is susceptible to contracting the primary infection, which is chickenpox, from her infected nephew. This is a high-risk situation, as primary varicella infection during pregnancy can have serious consequences for both the mother and the fetus. She is a candidate for post-exposure prophylaxis with Varicella-Zoster Immune Globulin (VZIG).

Question 8

A newborn is delivered with the body covered by a thick, membranous layer. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

A newborn who is born encased in a tight, shiny, translucent membrane resembling a collodion or parchment is known as a collodion baby. This presentation is not a final diagnosis but is the initial manifestation of several underlying forms of congenital ichthyosis. This membrane is the stratum corneum, which is shed over the first few weeks of life to reveal the underlying skin condition. The most common disorder that presents as a collodion baby is autosomal recessive congenital ichthyosis, of which lamellar ichthyosis is a major subtype. In lamellar ichthyosis, after the collodion membrane sheds, the infant is left with large, dark, plate-like scales covering the entire body, often with associated ectropion (outward turning of the eyelids). X-linked ichthyosis and ichthyosis vulgaris present later and do not have a collodion membrane at birth.

Question 9

Which of the following statements about the skin is NOT true?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The statement that is not true is that both the dermis and epidermis are derived from the ectoderm. The skin is composed of two main layers with distinct embryological origins. The superficial layer, the epidermis, is a stratified squamous epithelium that is derived from the surface ectoderm. This is true, and its primary cell type is the keratinocyte (C is a true statement). However, the deeper layer, the dermis, which is the connective tissue layer of the skin, is derived from the mesoderm. This mesodermal origin gives rise to the fibroblasts that produce the collagen (primarily Type I and Type III) and elastin fibers that make up the dermis (D is a true statement). The skin is the body's largest organ and accounts for approximately 15% of the total body weight (B is a true statement). Therefore, the incorrect statement is the one claiming a common ectodermal origin for both layers.

Question 10

What is the primary function of Positive End-Expiratory Pressure (PEEP) in mechanical ventilation?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Positive End-Expiratory Pressure (PEEP) is a setting in mechanical ventilation where pressure in the lungs is maintained above atmospheric pressure at the end of expiration. The primary purpose of PEEP is to prevent alveolar collapse (atelectasis). By keeping the alveoli open throughout the respiratory cycle, PEEP increases the functional residual capacity (FRC), improves ventilation-perfusion (V/Q) matching by recruiting collapsed lung units, and increases the surface area for gas exchange.
