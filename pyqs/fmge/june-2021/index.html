The correct answer is (D) External capsule, as it is incorrectly marked in the diagram. The external capsule is a thin white matter tract located lateral to the putamen and medial to the claustrum. In the figure, label D does not point to this thin white band; instead, it appears to indicate a broader cortical or subcortical region, making the labeling incorrect. Other options are correctly marked: (A) Caudate nucleus is correctly identified. It lies adjacent to the lateral ventricle and appears as a curved gray matter structure along its margin, which matches the diagram. (B) Globus pallidus is also correctly labeled. It is situated medial to the putamen and forms the medial part of the lentiform nucleus, usually shown as a lighter shaded region. (C) Putamen is correctly marked as well. It lies lateral to the globus pallidus and is a major component of the basal ganglia, forming the outer part of the lentiform nucleus. Among the listed laryngeal cartilages, which is least prone to calcification?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The laryngeal cartilages are composed of either hyaline or elastic cartilage, and this histological difference determines their tendency to calcify with age. The thyroid (Option A), cricoid (Option B), and the majority of the arytenoid (Option C) cartilages consist of hyaline cartilage. Hyaline cartilage characteristically undergoes calcification and even ossification as a normal part of the aging process, which can be visible on radiographic imaging. In contrast, the epiglottis, corniculate, and cuneiform cartilages are composed of elastic cartilage. Elastic cartilage contains a high density of elastic fibers and does not typically undergo calcification regardless of age. This flexibility is essential for the epiglottis to function as a lid during swallowing. Clinically, understanding this pattern is important because a calcified thyroid cartilage on an X-ray can be mistaken for a foreign body by an inexperienced clinician, whereas the epiglottis remains radiolucent throughout life due to its elastic nature. The basal ganglia are organized into functional and phylogenetic groups. The "neostriatum" (or simply the striatum) consists of the caudate nucleus and the putamen. Question 17

Which of the following substances does not usually show a reduction in concentration as the filtrate passes through the proximal convoluted tubule (PCT)?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The proximal convoluted tubule (PCT) is responsible for the bulk reabsorption of filtered solutes. Substances like glucose (Option B) and amino acids (Option A) are almost 100% reabsorbed in the early PCT via sodium-linked cotransporters, causing their concentration in the tubular fluid to drop precipitously. Bicarbonate (Option D) is also preferentially reabsorbed in the early PCT, leading to a decrease in its concentration. In contrast, chloride (Option C) is not actively reabsorbed in the very first segment of the PCT; instead, as water and other solutes are removed, the concentration of chloride actually "increases" slightly in the tubular fluid. It is only later in the PCT that chloride is reabsorbed down its concentration gradient through paracellular and transcellular pathways. Therefore, on a "TF/P" (Tubular Fluid/Plasma concentration) graph, glucose and amino acids show the sharpest decline, while chloride is the only substance among the choices that remains above a ratio of 1.0 throughout the PCT. Question 18

Does Von Gierke's disease involve a deficiency of which enzyme?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Von Gierke's disease, also known as Glycogen Storage Disease Type I (GSD I), is caused by a deficiency of the enzyme glucose 6-phosphatase. This enzyme is essential for the final step of both glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis, as it converts glucose 6-phosphate into free glucose in the liver and kidneys. Without it, patients experience severe fasting hypoglycemia, hepatomegaly (due to glycogen accumulation), hyperuricemia, and lactic acidosis. Liver phosphorylase deficiency (Option A) causes GSD Type VI (Hers disease), which is generally milder. Lysosomal alpha-glucosidase deficiency (Option B) causes GSD Type II (Pompe disease), characterized by cardiomegaly and muscle weakness. Hepatic glycogen synthase deficiency (Option C) is known as GSD Type 0, which results in decreased glycogen stores rather than accumulation. Understanding these enzymatic defects is fundamental for metabolic biochemistry and pediatrics. Von Gierke's requires frequent feedings with uncooked cornstarch to maintain stable blood glucose levels and prevent life-threatening hypoglycemic episodes and metabolic derangements.

Question 19

Deficiency of which enzyme leads to the development of Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD)?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Maple Syrup Urine Disease (MSUD) is an autosomal recessive metabolic disorder caused by a deficiency in the branched-chain alpha-ketoacid dehydrogenase (BCKAD) complex. This enzyme complex is responsible for the oxidative decarboxylation of the ketoacids derived from the three branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs): Leucine, Isoleucine, and Valine. A defect here leads to the accumulation of these BCAAs and their toxic alpha-ketoacid byproducts, which impart a characteristic sweet, maple syrup odor to the patient's urine and earwax. Isovaleryl CoA dehydrogenase deficiency (Option B) causes Isovaleric Acidemia, which presents with a "sweaty feet" odor. Pyruvate carboxylase (Option C) and Acetyl CoA carboxylase (Option D) are involved in gluconeogenesis and fatty acid synthesis, respectively, and are not related to BCAA metabolism. MSUD is a pediatric emergency; if untreated, the leucine accumulation causes severe cerebral edema and neurological damage. Treatment involves a strict life-long diet restricted in BCAAs and specialized medical formulas.

Question 22

Which of the following factors are involved in maintaining and regulating calcium levels in the blood?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Blood calcium homeostasis is a tightly regulated process involving three primary hormones. Parathormone (PTH), secreted by the parathyroid glands, is the most important regulator for "increasing" blood calcium; it stimulates bone resorption, renal calcium reabsorption, and the activation of Vitamin D. Vitamin D (Option C), specifically its active form calcitriol, increases calcium levels by promoting its absorption from the small intestine. Calcitonin (Option A), secreted by the parafollicular C-cells of the thyroid, acts as a physiological antagonist to PTH by "decreasing" blood calcium through the inhibition of bone-resorbing osteoclasts. While calcitonin plays a smaller role in daily human calcium regulation compared to PTH, it is still a recognized component of the regulatory system. Together, these substances ensure that ionized calcium remains within a narrow physiological range, which is critical for muscle contraction, nerve conduction, and blood coagulation. Therefore, the combination of all three factors (Option D) constitutes the complete regulatory machinery for serum calcium.

Therefore, IPR is the primary surveillance tool for assessing the immediate success of malaria control programs.

Question 50

A 40-year-old patient is brought to the emergency department in an unconscious state. Examination reveals needle track marks on the upper limbs along with hypertension, tachycardia, and hyperthermia. Based on these findings, which type of poisoning should be suspected?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The patient’s symptoms represent a "sympathomimetic toxidrome," characterized by hypertension (elevated BP), tachycardia (increased HR), and hyperthermia (high body temperature). Cocaine is a potent CNS stimulant that causes these exact signs by blocking the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine. Heroin (Option A) is an opioid; a patient would typically present with a "triad" of pinpoint pupils, respiratory depression, and bradycardia, which is the opposite of this presentation. Cannabis (Option C) poisoning generally does not cause severe hypertension or high fever. LSD (Option D) is a hallucinogen that can cause some sympathetic arousal but is rarely associated with unconsciousness and severe systemic instability compared to cocaine. The presence of "needle track marks" indicates intravenous drug abuse, which is common for both cocaine and heroin addicts. In a comatose patient with a racing heart and high fever, cocaine toxicity must be suspected and managed with supportive care and benzodiazepines to control the sympathetic surge.
