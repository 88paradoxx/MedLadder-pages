Question 2

Which type of epithelium lines the trachea?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The trachea is lined by pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium, often referred to as respiratory epithelium. Although it appears layered (stratified) because cell nuclei are at different heights, every cell is actually in contact with the basement membrane, making it a "pseudo" stratified tissue. This epithelium contains goblet cells that secrete mucus to trap inhaled particles and cilia that move the mucus upward toward the pharynx (the mucociliary escalator). Simple squamous epithelium (Option A) lines the alveoli for gas exchange. Transitional epithelium (Option B) is characteristic of the urinary tract, such as the bladder, allowing for distension. Stratified squamous epithelium (Option C) is found in areas prone to abrasion, like the esophagus and skin. In chronic smokers, the tracheal epithelium may undergo metaplasia, changing to stratified squamous epithelium to survive the irritation, which unfortunately destroys the protective ciliary mechanism, leading to a persistent "smoker's cough."

Gastric lavage is contraindicated in which of the following poisonings?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Gastric lavage ("pumping the stomach") is a common decontamination procedure, but it is strictly contraindicated in hydrocarbon poisoning (e.g., kerosene, gasoline, or thinner). These substances have low viscosity and high volatility. Lavage is also contraindicated in corrosive (acid/alkali) poisoning due to the risk of esophageal perforation. Strychnine (Option A) and Paraldehyde (Option B) poisonings can be managed with lavage if done early.
