Which of the following regulate body temperature?

The Hypothalamus serves as the body's master thermostat and primary center for thermoregulation. It contains specialized thermosensitive neurons that monitor both the core blood temperature and signals from peripheral skin receptors. The anterior (preoptic) hypothalamus primarily coordinates heat loss mechanisms, such as sweating and vasodilation, while the posterior hypothalamus coordinates heat conservation and production mechanisms, such as shivering and vasoconstriction. Question 12

In patients who are susceptible to refeeding syndrome, several electrolyte disturbances are commonly observed. Which of the following electrolyte abnormalities is NOT typically associated with refeeding syndrome?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Refeeding syndrome occurs when nutrition is reintroduced too rapidly to severely malnourished patients. The physiological hallmark is a massive release of insulin in response to carbohydrates, which causes a rapid shift of electrolytes from the extracellular space into the cells. This leads to profound Hypophosphatemia (Option D, the most common and dangerous sign), Hypokalemia (Option A), and Hypomagnesemia. Hypocalcemia (Option C) can also occur secondary to magnesium depletion. Hyperkalemia (Option B) is not associated with refeeding syndrome; instead, the problem is a life-threatening deficiency of serum potassium. These electrolyte drops can cause cardiac arrhythmias, respiratory failure, and seizures. Clinicians must identify high-risk patients (e.g., those with anorexia nervosa, chronic alcoholism, or marasmus) and "start low and go slow" with caloric intake while vigilantly monitoring and replacing electrolytes. Hyperkalemia is more typical of conditions like acute renal failure or cell lysis syndromes, which are pathophysiologically distinct from the anabolic shift seen in refeeding.

The Erlanger-Gasser classification categorizes nerve fibers based on their diameter and conduction velocity. Histidine is the primary amino acid responsible for the buffering capacity of proteins at physiological pH (around 7.4). Question 18

Ammonia is a toxic byproduct of nitrogen metabolism, particularly dangerous to the central nervous system. In the brain, excess ammonia is detoxified by being combined with glutamate to form Glutamine, a reaction catalyzed by the enzyme glutamine synthetase. Glutamine is a non-toxic, neutral amino acid that can safely circulate in the blood to be transported to the liver (and kidneys). Question 28

Apoptotic bodies comprise of?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Apoptosis is "programmed cell death," a tidy process designed to remove unwanted cells without triggering inflammation. During apoptosis, the cell shrinks and the DNA fragments. The cell then breaks apart into several membrane-bound fragments called apoptotic bodies. These bodies comprise cytoplasm, tightly packed organelles, and fragments of the nucleus (intranuclear material). Apoptosis is a vital physiological process during embryogenesis, immune system regulation, and the removal of DNA-damaged cells. The "empty delta sign" is the pathognomonic radiological finding for Cerebral Venous (Sinus) Thrombosis on a contrast-enhanced CT scan. Question 194

A patient reports the belief that his thoughts and feelings are being controlled or influenced by an external agency. What is this psychopathological phenomenon called?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The belief that one's feelings, thoughts, or movements are being controlled or influenced by an external agency is known as a Passivity phenomenon (or delusion of control). This is one of Schneider’s First-Rank Symptoms of schizophrenia. Delusion of nihilism (Option A, Cotard’s syndrome) involves the belief that one is dead or that the world doesn't exist. Delusion of reference (Option B) is the belief that neutral events (like news broadcasts) have a special, personal meaning meant only for the patient. Othello syndrome (Option C) is a delusion of morbid jealousy, where the patient is convinced of a partner's infidelity without cause. Passivity phenomena are highly diagnostic for psychotic disorders. The patient may feel like a "puppet," with their will being overridden by outside forces. Identifying these core psychotic features is essential for differentiating schizophrenia from other mental health conditions and starting appropriate antipsychotic management.

Atypical antipsychotic with least metabolic side effects?

Second-generation (atypical) antipsychotics are generally associated with metabolic side effects like weight gain and diabetes. Question 196

The patient is presenting with acute psychological distress following a life-threatening traumatic event (being held at gunpoint). Question 197

A patient receiving the antipsychotic haloperidol develops acute dystonia. What is the next best step in management?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Acute dystonia (involuntary muscle contractions, such as torticollis or oculogyric crisis) is a common and distressing extrapyramidal side effect of high-potency first-generation antipsychotics like Haloperidol. The next best step and standard acute treatment is the administration of an anticholinergic agent, such as Benztropine or Procyclidine, which rapidly reverses the muscle spasm. Fluphenazine (Option C) is another high-potency antipsychotic that would likely worsen the dystonia. Increasing the dose of haloperidol (Option D) is strictly contraindicated and would exacerbate the condition. Changing to clozapine (Option B) is a long-term strategy for refractory patients but is not the immediate treatment for an acute dystonic episode. After reversing the dystonia, the overall treatment plan should be reviewed, often by switching the patient to an atypical antipsychotic with lower extrapyramidal risk. Bedside recognition and rapid pharmacological reversal are essential for patient comfort and safety.

A patient with schizophrenia, currently on antipsychotic medication, develops acute dystonia. What is the appropriate management for this patient?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Acute dystonia is a common extrapyramidal side effect (EPS) of antipsychotic therapy. The appropriate management involves using an anticholinergic medication. Benzhexol (Trihexyphenidyl) is a standard anticholinergic drug used to treat and prevent dystonia and parkinsonian symptoms induced by antipsychotics. Fluphenazine (Option B) would likely worsen the EPS. Increasing the dose of haloperidol (Option C) is incorrect as it would increase the dopamine blockade responsible for the dystonia.
