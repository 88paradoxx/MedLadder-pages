Question 2

A 35-year-old carpenter presents with sudden anterior shoulder pain after lifting a heavy object overhead. He reports hearing a “pop” at the time of injury, followed by immediate swelling and bruising. Examination reveals a bulge in the mid–upper arm, and he has marked weakness in elbow flexion and forearm supination. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The clinical presentation described is a classic case of a long head of the biceps tendon rupture. The patient’s sudden "pop" sound during heavy overhead lifting and the subsequent formation of a visible bulge in the mid-upper arm—commonly referred to as the "Popeye deformity"—are pathognomonic. This muscle bulge occurs because the detached muscle belly retracts distally toward the elbow. The long head of the biceps is essential for elbow flexion and forearm supination, which explains the significant weakness the patient experiences. Triceps muscle rupture (Option B) would present with a bulge on the posterior aspect of the arm and weakness in elbow extension. A humerus fracture (Option C) would cause severe localized bone pain, instability, and usually a different radiographic appearance rather than an isolated muscle bulge. Tennis elbow (Option D), or lateral epicondylitis, involves chronic overuse pain at the lateral epicondyle rather than acute muscle deformity or flexion weakness.

Question 5

During intrauterine development, the talus, a tarsal bone of the foot, begins ossification at a specific gestational age. At what time during fetal life does ossification of the talus occur?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The ossification of the tarsal bones follows a specific chronological sequence during intrauterine development. The talus typically begins to ossify at 7 months of intrauterine life. It is the second tarsal bone to ossify, following the calcaneus, which begins ossification at approximately 5 to 6 months of gestation. The cuboid bone is usually the third, ossifying shortly before birth, often at 9 months. Options A (3 months), C (9 months), and D (2 months) are incorrect for the talus. While the primary centers for long bones appear much earlier (around the 8th week or 2nd month), the tarsal bones are mostly cartilaginous at birth, except for the calcaneus, talus, and sometimes the cuboid. Identifying the presence of these ossification centers on fetal radiographs or ultrasounds can be a useful clinical indicator for assessing gestational age and skeletal maturity in a developing fetus.

Question 14

A patient undergoing evaluation for suspected mitochondrial dysfunction has various mitochondrial structures assessed. Cardiolipin is a characteristic phospholipid of which mitochondrial location?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Cardiolipin is a unique phospholipid that is found almost exclusively in the inner mitochondrial membrane. It accounts for approximately 20% of the total lipid composition in this membrane. Cardiolipin is essential for the structural integrity and optimal function of various mitochondrial proteins, including those involved in the electron transport chain (such as Complex IV) and the ATP synthase complex. It is not a significant component of the outer mitochondrial membrane (Option A), the cytosol (Option B), or the mitochondrial matrix (Option D). Because of its specific location and role in bioenergetics, cardiolipin is often a target of investigation in cases of mitochondrial dysfunction. For example, abnormalities in cardiolipin metabolism are associated with Barth syndrome, a rare X-linked disorder characterized by cardiomyopathy and skeletal muscle weakness. Its unique structure, consisting of four fatty acid tails, allows the inner membrane to maintain the tight curvature necessary for forming cristae. BIOCHEMISTRY

Question 15

A 62-year-old man with a long history of chronic alcohol use presents with confusion, visual disturbances, gait instability, and impaired short- term memory. These findings are suggestive of a nutritional deficiency. Deficiency of which vitamin is most likely responsible for this clinical picture?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The clinical triad of confusion, vision disturbances (ophthalmoplegia/nystagmus), and unsteady gait (ataxia) in a chronic alcoholic is diagnostic for Wernicke encephalopathy. When accompanied by short-term memory loss and confabulation, it is termed Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome. This condition is caused by a severe deficiency of Thiamine (Vitamin B1). Thiamine is a vital cofactor for key enzymes in glucose metabolism, particularly in the brain. Ascorbic acid (Option A, Vitamin C) deficiency causes scurvy. Retinol (Option C, Vitamin A) deficiency leads to night blindness and xerophthalmia. Folate (Option D, Vitamin B9) deficiency causes megaloblastic anemia and can contribute to cognitive issues, but it does not produce the specific oculomotor and gait disturbances seen in Wernicke’s. Chronic alcohol use impairs thiamine absorption and storage, making these patients highly susceptible. It is a medical emergency; administering glucose before thiamine can worsen the condition by further depleting the remaining thiamine stores.

The symptoms of alopecia, dermatitis (often perioral and acral), diarrhea, and impaired wound healing are the classic presentation of Zinc deficiency. Question 18

Production of oxaloacetate is from which of the following amino acid?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

In amino acid metabolism, certain amino acids are classified as glucogenic because they can be converted into intermediates of the TCA cycle or gluconeogenesis. Aspartate is directly converted into oxaloacetate via a transamination reaction. In this reaction, catalyzed by aspartate aminotransferase (AST), the amino group of aspartate is transferred to alpha-ketoglutarate, producing oxaloacetate and glutamate. Alanine (Option A) is transaminated to produce pyruvate. Lysine (Option B) and Leucine (Option C) are strictly ketogenic amino acids; they are broken down into acetyl-CoA or acetoacetate and cannot be used for the net production of glucose or TCA cycle intermediates like oxaloacetate. Oxaloacetate is a critical four-carbon intermediate that condenses with acetyl-CoA to initiate the TCA cycle and is also the starting material for gluconeogenesis in the liver. Thus, aspartate serves as a vital nitrogen and carbon source for these central metabolic pathways.

Zinc is the mineral most closely associated with the stabilization and storage of insulin within the pancreatic beta cells. Question 20

All of the following can activate Hormone- sensitive lipase. Except?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) is a key enzyme in adipocytes responsible for the breakdown of stored triacylglycerols into free fatty acids and glycerol (lipolysis). It is activated by phosphorylation in response to hormones that signal a need for energy. Glucagon (Option A), Epinephrine (Option B), and ACTH (Option C) all bind to G-protein coupled receptors that increase intracellular cAMP, thereby activating HSL. Insulin, conversely, is the primary inhibitor of HSL. When blood glucose is high, insulin is secreted to promote fat storage (lipogenesis) and inhibit the breakdown of fat. It acts by activating a phosphatase that dephosphorylates and inactivates HSL, effectively shutting down lipolysis. This regulatory mechanism ensures that fat stores are mobilized only when other energy sources are low. Therefore, insulin is the exception among the hormones listed, as its role is to suppress rather than activate this enzyme.

Question 21

Which enzyme serves as the rate-limiting step in the heme synthesis pathway?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The synthesis of heme is a complex pathway occurring partly in the mitochondria and partly in the cytosol. The rate-limiting and primary regulatory step of the entire pathway is the first step: the condensation of succinyl-CoA and glycine to form delta-aminolevulinic acid. This reaction is catalyzed by the enzyme delta-aminolevulinic acid (ALA) synthase. This enzyme requires pyridoxal phosphate (Vitamin B6) as a cofactor and is inhibited by the end-product, heme (feedback inhibition). HMB synthase (Option A) is the enzyme deficient in acute intermittent porphyria. ALA dehydratase (Option B) and Ferrochelatase (Option D) are enzymes later in the pathway that are highly sensitive to lead inhibition but are not the primary rate-limiting steps under normal physiological conditions. Proper regulation of ALA synthase is essential to ensure that heme production matches the body's requirements for hemoglobin and other hemoproteins while preventing the accumulation of toxic porphyrin intermediates.

Question 23

Galactokinesis is governed by:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Galactokinesis, which refers to the ejection of milk from the mammary glands, is primarily governed by the hormone oxytocin. This occurs through the "milk-ejection reflex" or "let-down reflex." When an infant suckles, sensory signals are sent to the hypothalamus, triggering the posterior pituitary to release oxytocin into the bloodstream. Oxytocin then causes the contraction of myoepithelial cells surrounding the alveoli, forcing milk into the ducts. Prolactin (Option A) is responsible for lactogenesis (milk production) and galactopoiesis (maintenance of secretion) but not the actual ejection. Estrogen (Option B) and Progesterone (Option D) are involved in mamogenesis (development of the breast tissue) during pregnancy but actually inhibit the stimulatory effects of prolactin until their levels drop after delivery. Understanding these specific roles is critical for managing lactation issues; oxytocin's role in ejection means that emotional stress or pain can inhibit the let-down reflex by interfering with oxytocin release.

Question 24

In the management of celiac sprue, patients are advised to adhere strictly to a gluten-free diet to prevent symptom exacerbation and intestinal injury. Which of the following food items should be avoided by individuals with celiac disease?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Celiac disease is an autoimmune-mediated enteropathy triggered by the ingestion of gluten, a protein complex found in certain grains. Wheat (Option B) is the most common source of gluten and must be strictly avoided by individuals with this condition. Other grains to avoid include barley and rye (often remembered by the acronym "BROW": Barley, Rye, Oats, Wheat). Ingestion of even small amounts of gluten leads to villous atrophy in the small intestine, resulting in malabsorption and symptoms like diarrhea and weight loss. Rice (Option A), Dhal (Option C, lentils), and Ragi (Option D, finger millet) are naturally gluten-free and are safe, recommended alternatives for a celiac-friendly diet. Patients must be educated about hidden sources of gluten in processed foods and medications. Adhering to a lifelong gluten-free diet is the only effective treatment to prevent long-term complications such as osteoporosis and intestinal lymphoma.

Question 30

A patient develops immune-mediated hemolytic anemia following administration of penicillin, indicating an adverse drug reaction. This immune response represents which type of hypersensitivity reaction?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Drug-induced immune hemolytic anemia caused by penicillin is a classic example of a Type 2 (cytotoxic) hypersensitivity reaction. In this mechanism, penicillin acts as a hapten and binds to the surface of red blood cells. The immune system then produces antibodies (usually IgG or IgM) that specifically target the penicillin-coated RBCs. This leads to the destruction of the cells via the complement system or phagocytosis by splenic macrophages. Type 1 (Option A) is IgE-mediated and results in immediate anaphylaxis or hives. Type 3 (Option C) involves the deposition of circulating immune complexes in tissues, leading to conditions like serum sickness. Type 4 (Option D) is a cell-mediated (delayed) response, such as contact dermatitis. While penicillin can cause any of these reactions, the specific destruction of blood cells mediated by surface-bound antibodies is uniquely categorized as Type 2. Clinically, this is confirmed by a positive Direct Antiglobulin (Coombs) test.

Bedaquiline is a novel, breakthrough diarylquinoline antibiotic used specifically for the treatment of multidrug-resistant tuberculosis (MDR-TB). Pheochromocytoma is a catecholamine-secreting tumor of the adrenal medulla that causes severe paroxysmal hypertension. The gold-standard medication for preoperative management is Phenoxybenzamine.
