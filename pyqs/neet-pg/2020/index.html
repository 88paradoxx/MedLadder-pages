Question 2

The elongated path of the left recurrent laryngeal nerve is a result of the persistence of which embryonic aortic arch?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The asymmetrical course of the right and left recurrent laryngeal nerves is a direct consequence of the embryological development of the aortic arches. Both nerves initially hook around the 6th aortic arch on their respective sides. On the right side, the distal portion of the 6th arch and the entire 5th arch artery degenerate. This allows the right recurrent laryngeal nerve to ascend and hook underneath the next persistent structure, which is the 4th aortic arch artery (forming the right subclavian artery). On the left side, however, the distal part of the 6th aortic arch persists to form the ductus arteriosus (which later becomes the ligamentum arteriosum). Because this structure remains, the left recurrent laryngeal nerve is trapped and cannot ascend. It is held down in the thorax, where it loops under the arch of the aorta (formed by the left 4th arch) adjacent to the ligamentum arteriosum. This explains its longer, recurrent course from the chest back up to the larynx.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The posture described is a classic claw hand deformity. This deformity arises from an imbalance between the extrinsic and intrinsic muscles of the hand. The intrinsic hand muscles, specifically the lumbricals and interossei, are responsible for flexing the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joints and extending the interphalangeal (IP) joints. When these intrinsic muscles are paralyzed (typically due to a combined ulnar and median nerve injury), their actions are lost. This allows the unopposed action of the extrinsic muscles to dominate. This combination of MCP hyperextension and IP flexion creates the characteristic claw-like appearance. Therefore, paralysis of the interossei and lumbricals is the direct cause of this deformity.

Question 11

In fetal circulation, which location has the highest oxygen concentration?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

In fetal circulation, oxygenation occurs at the placenta, not the lungs. Highly oxygenated and nutrient-rich blood travels from the placenta to the fetus via the single umbilical vein. The umbilical vein enters the abdomen and sends a major portion of its blood through a shunt called the ductus venosus, which bypasses the liver and drains directly into the inferior vena cava (IVC). This freshly oxygenated blood from the placenta mixes with the deoxygenated blood returning from the lower body of the fetus within the IVC. Despite this mixing, the segment of the IVC just before it enters the right atrium contains the most highly oxygenated blood found anywhere in the fetal body. This stream is preferentially shunted across the foramen ovale into the left atrium to supply the fetal brain and heart. The superior vena cava (A) carries only deoxygenated blood from the upper body.

Question 13

Which proteoglycan is found in the glomerular basement membrane?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The glomerular basement membrane (GBM) is a crucial component of the renal filtration barrier. In addition to its structural components like type IV collagen and laminin, it contains proteoglycans that play a vital role in its function. The most important proteoglycan in the GBM is heparan sulfate. The glycosaminoglycan side chains of heparan sulfate are rich in negatively charged carboxyl and sulfate groups. This imparts a strong overall negative charge to the GBM. This negative charge creates an electrostatic barrier that repels other negatively charged molecules, such as albumin. This charge-selective filtration is essential for preventing the loss of plasma proteins into the urine. Loss of heparan sulfate, as seen in some glomerular diseases, leads to increased permeability to albumin and results in proteinuria.

Question 15

What is the name of the reflex that inhibits gastric emptying when acidic or hypertonic contents enter the duodenum?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The enterogastric reflex is a key physiological mechanism that regulates the rate of gastric emptying. This is an inhibitory reflex that is initiated by stimuli in the duodenum (entero-) and acts on the stomach (-gastric). When chyme enters the duodenum from the stomach, chemoreceptors and stretch receptors in the duodenal wall are activated. The most potent stimuli for this reflex are high acidity, high osmolarity (hypertonic solutions), the presence of fats and proteins, and duodenal distension. Activation of these receptors triggers both neural (short and long reflexes) and hormonal (release of cholecystokinin and secretin) pathways that act to inhibit gastric motility and slow down the rate of gastric emptying. This ensures that the duodenum is not overwhelmed and has adequate time to neutralize the acid and digest the nutrients before receiving more chyme from the stomach.

Question 18

Why is blood stored in citrate-phosphate-dextrose (CPD) preferred over acidic-citrate-dextrose (ACD) for transfusion in hypoxic patients?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The ability of hemoglobin to release oxygen to the tissues is modulated by several factors, most importantly the concentration of 2,3-diphosphoglycerate (2,3-DPG) within the red blood cells. A higher level of 2,3-DPG facilitates oxygen release (a rightward shift of the oxygen-hemoglobin dissociation curve), which is crucial for hypoxic patients. During the storage of blood, red blood cells continue to metabolize glucose, and the level of 2,3-DPG progressively declines (the storage lesion). Citrate-phosphate-dextrose (CPD) and its successor, CPDA-1 (which adds adenine), are superior to the older Acid-Citrate-Dextrose (ACD) solution because their higher pH and phosphate content help to better preserve the levels of 2,3-DPG during storage. This means that blood stored in CPD maintains a better oxygen-releasing capacity, making it more effective for transfusion into patients who are hypoxic and in critical need of tissue oxygenation.

Hepcidin is the master regulatory hormone of iron homeostasis. It is produced by the liver in response to high iron levels or inflammation. Hepcidin's primary function is to decrease the amount of iron available in the circulation. Which technique is employed to measure current flow through a single ion channel?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The patch-clamp technique is a revolutionary electrophysiological method that allows for the study of ionic currents flowing through individual ion channels. The technique involves using a micropipette with a very fine tip (around 1 micrometer in diameter) to form a high-resistance seal (a giga-seal) with a small patch of the cell membrane. This electrically isolates the patch of membrane. In the cell-attached configuration, this setup allows the recording of the picoampere-level currents that flow when a single ion channel within that patch opens and closes. Further variations of the technique (whole-cell, inside-out, outside-out) allow for the detailed characterization of channel properties, such as their gating, conductance, and pharmacology. The voltage clamp (B) technique is used to measure ionic currents across the entire membrane of a cell, not a single channel.

Question 27

Bilirubin in serum can be measured by-

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The classic laboratory method for measuring serum bilirubin levels is the Van den Bergh reaction. This reaction is based on the principle of the diazo reaction, first described by Ehrlich. It uses a diazo reagent (diazotized sulfanilic acid) which reacts with bilirubin to form a colored product called azobilirubin, which can be measured spectrophotometrically. A key aspect of the Van den Bergh test is its ability to differentiate between conjugated (direct) and unconjugated (indirect) bilirubin. Conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and reacts directly with the diazo reagent, giving the direct bilirubin value. Unconjugated bilirubin, which is bound to albumin, requires the addition of an accelerator like alcohol or caffeine to react. The total bilirubin is measured after adding the accelerator, and the indirect bilirubin is calculated by subtracting the direct from the total value.

Question 32

The richest source of Vitamin B12 is

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Vitamin B12 (cobalamin) is unique among vitamins in that it is synthesized exclusively by microorganisms, such as bacteria and archaea. It is not produced by plants or animals. Animals obtain Vitamin B12 by consuming these microorganisms directly or by absorbing it from their own gut microflora. The vitamin then accumulates in their tissues. Consequently, the only natural dietary sources of Vitamin B12 for humans are animal-derived products. The richest sources include organ meats (especially liver and kidney), shellfish (like clams and oysters), muscle meat (beef, lamb), fish, poultry, eggs, and dairy products. Plant-based foods, such as green leafy vegetables, and plant-derived oils, like corn oil and sunflower oil, contain no naturally occurring Vitamin B12. This is why individuals following a strict vegan or vegetarian diet are at high risk for developing Vitamin B12 deficiency unless they consume fortified foods or take supplements.

Question 34

True about non-competitive antagonist :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The question uses Michaelis-Menten kinetics terms (Km, Vmax) to describe a non-competitive antagonist, but these terms are for enzyme inhibitors. Assuming the question means a non-competitive inhibitor : a non-competitive inhibitor binds to the enzyme at a site distinct from the active site (an allosteric site). This binding can occur whether the substrate is bound or not. The inhibitor's binding changes the conformation of the enzyme, rendering it catalytically inactive. Because the inhibitor does not compete with the substrate for the active site, it does not affect the enzyme's affinity for the substrate, and therefore, the Km (Michaelis constant) remains the same. However, by inactivating a fraction of the enzyme molecules, the inhibitor effectively reduces the total concentration of functional enzyme available. This leads to a decrease in the Vmax (maximum reaction velocity), as the reaction can never reach the maximum velocity it would have in the absence of the inhibitor, no matter how much substrate is added.

Question 213

Which of the following pubertal changes does not depend on estrogen?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Puberty in females is driven by hormones from both the ovaries (estrogen) and the adrenal glands (androgens). Estrogen is responsible for the development of most female secondary sexual characteristics. It stimulates breast development (thelarche), growth of the uterus, cornification of the vaginal epithelium, and the production of thin, watery cervical mucus. The cyclical withdrawal of estrogen and progesterone is what leads to menstruation (menarche). In contrast, the growth of pubic and axillary hair (pubarche or adrenarche) is an androgen-dependent process. These androgens are primarily produced by the adrenal glands. Therefore, hair growth is not directly dependent on estrogen and is mediated by a different hormonal pathway during puberty.

Question 214

What is the most common site for fertilization?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The fallopian tube (or oviduct) is divided into four parts : the fimbriae, the infundibulum, the ampulla, and the isthmus. After ovulation, the oocyte is captured by the fimbriae and transported into the fallopian tube. Sperm travel from the vagina, through the uterus, and into the fallopian tubes. Fertilization, the fusion of the sperm and the oocyte, most commonly occurs in the ampulla of the fallopian tube. The ampulla is the widest and longest portion of the tube, providing an ideal environment for fertilization to take place. After fertilization, the resulting zygote begins to divide and travels down the rest of the fallopian tube over the next several days, eventually reaching the uterine cavity where it will implant. The isthmus is the narrow portion of the tube adjacent to the uterus, and the fimbriae are the finger-like projections at the ovarian end.

What is the most suitable uterine distension medium to be used during hysteroscopy when bipolar cautery is employed?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Hysteroscopy requires the use of a distension medium to expand the uterine cavity for visualization. The choice of medium depends on the type of electrosurgical energy being used. When using mono polar cautery, a non-electrolyte, non-conductive medium (such as glycine, sorbitol, or mannitol) must be used to prevent the dispersal of the electrical current. Question 225

Which is the most common drug causing dependence?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

When considering the most common drug causing dependence, it is important to look at the overall prevalence of use and the number of people who meet the criteria for a substance use disorder (dependence). While opioids like heroin and stimulants like cocaine and amphetamines have a very high potential for causing severe physiological and psychological dependence in their users, their overall prevalence of use in the general population is much lower than that of cannabis. Cannabis is one of the most widely used illicit substances globally. Because of its widespread use, even though its dependence potential per user might be lower than that of heroin, the sheer number of users means that a larger absolute number of people develop a cannabis use disorder (dependence). Therefore, in terms of the total number of individuals affected by dependence worldwide, cannabis is often cited as the most common illicit drug.
