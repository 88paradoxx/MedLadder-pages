Question 1

Thrombosis of the posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA) is associated with which syndrome?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Wallenberg syndrome, also known as the lateral medullary syndrome, is a neurological condition caused by an ischemic stroke in the lateral part of the medulla oblongata in the brainstem. The most common vascular cause for this infarct is an occlusion of the intracranial portion of the vertebral artery, followed by its largest branch, the posterior inferior cerebellar artery (PICA). This stroke leads to a characteristic pattern of neurological deficits due to damage to specific tracts and nuclei. Key features include vertigo, nystagmus, and ataxia (damage to the inferior cerebellar peduncle and vestibular nuclei); dysphagia and hoarseness (damage to the nucleus ambiguus); and a distinctive crossed sensory loss. Question 2

In which organ is the Space of Disse located?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The Space of Disse, or the perisinusoidal space, is a unique anatomical location found within the liver. It is the microscopic space situated between the basolateral surface of the hepatocytes (liver cells) and the fenestrated endothelial lining of the liver sinusoids (specialized capillaries). This space is filled with blood plasma that filters through the fenestrations of the sinusoidal endothelium. The Space of Disse is a critical site for the metabolic exchange between the blood and the hepatocytes; plasma solutes can directly contact the microvilli of the hepatocytes, allowing for efficient uptake of nutrients and secretion of proteins like albumin. It also contains the hepatic stellate cells (also known as Ito cells), which are responsible for storing Vitamin A and are involved in the pathogenesis of liver fibrosis. This perisinusoidal space is a feature unique to the liver's microarchitecture.

The sensations of pain and temperature are transmitted from the periphery to the brain via the spinothalamic tract, which is a key component of the anterolateral system. Question 9

The ducts of Bellini are found in which organ?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The ducts of Bellini, also known as the papillary ducts, are anatomical structures found within the kidney. They represent the most distal part of the collecting duct system. The collecting ducts from multiple nephrons progressively merge in the renal medulla to form larger and larger ducts. The final, large-caliber ducts that are formed just before the collecting system opens into the minor calyx at the tip of the renal papilla are called the ducts of Bellini. These ducts are lined by tall columnar epithelium and are responsible for channeling the final, concentrated urine from the renal medulla into the renal pelvis. They are the terminal conduits of the nephron and play a final role in concentrating the urine under the influence of antidiuretic hormone (ADH).

The rotational movement of the head, such as shaking the head no from left to right, occurs primarily at the atlanto-axial joint. Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The secretomotor innervation of the submandibular and sublingual salivary glands follows a complex parasympathetic pathway. The preganglionic parasympathetic fibers originate from the superior salivatory nucleus in the brainstem and travel with the facial nerve (CN VII). Question 14

The coronary arteries, which supply blood to the heart muscle itself, are the first branches of the aorta. Question 15

Bainbridge reflex causes which of the following?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The Bainbridge reflex, also known as the atrial reflex, is a physiological mechanism that results in an increase in heart rate in response to an increase in venous return and right atrial pressure. When venous return to the heart increases, the walls of the right atrium are stretched. This stretching stimulates low-pressure baroreceptors (stretch receptors) located in the atrial walls. The afferent signals from these receptors travel via the vagus nerve to the cardiovascular center in the medulla oblongata. The medullary centers then respond by decreasing parasympathetic (vagal) tone and increasing sympathetic tone to the sinoatrial (SA) node. This results in a reflexive tachycardia, or an increased heart rate. This reflex helps to prevent the pooling of blood in the venous system by accelerating the heart's pumping action to match the increased venous return.

Question 17

Which hormone is mainly released after day 14 of the menstrual cycle and influences the endometrium?

Correct answer: A

The menstrual cycle is divided into the follicular and luteal phases. Ovulation, triggered by the Luteinizing Hormone (LH) surge, occurs around day 14. Question 19

Functional residual capacity (FRC) is the volume of air left in the lungs after which respiratory event?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Functional Residual Capacity (FRC) is a standard lung volume that represents the amount of air remaining in the lungs at the end of a normal, quiet expiration. It is the equilibrium point of the respiratory system, where the outward elastic recoil of the chest wall is exactly balanced by the inward elastic recoil of the lungs. The FRC is not a single volume but is the sum of two other lung volumes : the Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV) and the Residual Volume (RV). FRC is a physiologically important volume because it acts as a buffer, ensuring that gas exchange in the alveoli can continue at a relatively constant rate throughout the respiratory cycle, even during expiration. The volume of air remaining after a forceful expiration (C) is the Residual Volume.

Question 25

C-peptide is present in which of the following situations?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Insulin is synthesized in the beta cells of the pancreas as a single polypeptide chain called proinsulin. The proinsulin molecule consists of the A chain and B chain of insulin, which are linked together by a connecting peptide, known as the C-peptide. For insulin to become active, this proinsulin molecule must be cleaved. Within the secretory granules of the beta cell, enzymes cleave off the C-peptide, leaving the A and B chains linked by disulfide bonds to form the mature insulin molecule. When the beta cell is stimulated to release insulin into the bloodstream, it co-secretes both mature insulin and the C-peptide in equimolar amounts (one molecule of C-peptide for every one molecule of insulin). Question 30

Which of the following statements is true regarding decorticate rigidity?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Decorticate rigidity (or posturing) is a stereotypical posture that occurs with specific types of severe brain injury. It is caused by a lesion in the cerebral hemispheres that is superior to the red nucleus in the midbrain. This lesion disconnects the cerebral cortex from the brainstem. The posture is characterized by flexion of the upper limbs (arms flexed and adducted, wrists and fingers flexed) and extension of the lower limbs. This occurs because the rubrospinal tract, which originates from the red nucleus and facilitates upper limb flexion, is left intact and unopposed by cortical input. The vestibulospinal tract causes extension in all limbs, but its effect on the upper limbs is overridden by the rubrospinal tract. In decerebrate rigidity, the lesion is below the red nucleus, so both upper and lower limbs are extended. Decerebrate rigidity is generally considered a more severe injury, and the associated extensor rigidity is more pronounced than in decorticate posturing.

Question 34

All are true regarding mitochondrial DNA, EXCEPT :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The statement that all respiratory proteins are synthesized within the mitochondria is false. While mitochondria possess their own small, circular, double-stranded genome (mtDNA) and the machinery to transcribe and translate it, this genome is very limited. Human mtDNA encodes only 13 proteins, all of which are essential subunits of the electron transport chain complexes (I, III, IV) and ATP synthase. However, the vast majority of mitochondrial proteins, including the other ~70 subunits of the respiratory chain complexes and all the proteins involved in mtDNA replication, transcription, translation, and metabolism, are encoded by genes in the nuclear DNA. These proteins are synthesized on cytoplasmic ribosomes and then imported into the mitochondria. Therefore, the assembly and function of the respiratory chain require a coordinated effort between both the mitochondrial and nuclear genomes. The other statements are true : mtDNA is inherited almost exclusively from the mother, and it has a high mutation rate compared to nuclear DNA.

Question 36

Hyperammonaemia inhibits the TCA cycle by depleting :

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Hyperammonemia, an excess of ammonia in the blood, is highly toxic to the central nervous system and is the primary cause of hepatic encephalopathy. One of the main mechanisms of ammonia's neurotoxicity is its interference with the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle in brain astrocytes. In the brain, excess ammonia is detoxified by being combined with glutamate to form glutamine, a reaction catalyzed by glutamine synthetase. To replenish the glutamate used in this reaction, another enzyme, glutamate dehydrogenase, combines ammonia with the TCA cycle intermediate α-ketoglutarate. In a state of severe hyperammonemia, this reaction is driven strongly in the direction of glutamate synthesis, which effectively depletes the brain's pool of α-ketoglutarate. The loss of this critical TCA cycle intermediate impairs the cycle's function, leading to a deficit in cellular energy (ATP) production and contributing significantly to the neurological dysfunction seen in hepatic encephalopathy.

The most common type of gallstones found in Western populations are cholesterol gallstones. These form when the bile in the gallbladder becomes supersaturated with cholesterol. Normally, cholesterol is kept soluble in bile through the formation of micelles with bile salts and phospholipids. When the concentration of cholesterol exceeds the solubilizing capacity of the bile, it can no longer remain in solution and begins to precipitate out. Which of the following anesthetic drugs is NOT cardiodepressive?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Most intravenous anesthetic induction agents cause some degree of cardiovascular depression, typically manifesting as a decrease in blood pressure, heart rate, and myocardial contractility. Propofol and thiopentone are both significant cardiodepressants, causing vasodilation and direct myocardial depression that can lead to hypotension. Etomidate is known for its remarkable cardiovascular stability, causing minimal changes in hemodynamics. It stimulates the sympathetic nervous system, leading to an increase in heart rate, blood pressure, and cardiac output. This makes ketamine a particularly useful induction agent for hemodynamically unstable patients, such as those in shock from trauma or sepsis.

Which method is most commonly used to assess the depth of anesthesia during surgery?

Which of the following contrast agents is not water-soluble?

Radiological contrast agents are used to enhance the visibility of internal structures. They are broadly classified based on their properties. Iodinated contrast agents, such as iohexol and iopamidol (which are non-ionic, low-osmolar agents), and Gastrografin (which is a high-osmolar, ionic agent containing diatrizoate meglumine and diatrizoate sodium), are all water-soluble compounds. Question 227

Which test relies on the idea that a suspect’s behavior changes when they recognize or react to an event they have witnessed?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The technique described is Brain Electrical Oscillation Signature Profiling (BEOS), commonly referred to as brain mapping or brain fingerprinting. This is a neuro-investigative technique that is based on the principle of detecting experiential knowledge. The underlying theory is that when an individual witnesses a significant event, their brain creates a memory trace of that event. When the suspect is later presented with stimuli (words, pictures) that are relevant to the crime (probes), their brain will automatically recognize these stimuli if they have prior knowledge of the event. This recognition elicits a specific, characteristic brainwave response known as a P300-MERMER (Memory and Encoding Related Multifaceted Electroencephalographic Response).
