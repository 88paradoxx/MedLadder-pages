In fetal circulation, the roles of the umbilical arteries and vein are reversed compared to systemic circulation in an adult. Question 4

Which nerve is responsible for mediating the gag reflex?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The gag reflex (or pharyngeal reflex) involves two key cranial nerves. The afferent (sensory) limb of the reflex is mediated by the 9th cranial nerve (glossopharyngeal nerve). When the posterior pharynx, tonsils, or base of the tongue is touched, sensory signals are transmitted via the glossopharyngeal nerve to the brainstem. The efferent (motor) limb, which causes the contraction of the pharyngeal muscles, is primarily mediated by the 10th cranial nerve (vagus nerve). Since the question asks which nerve is responsible for the reflex, and the 9th nerve is the primary sensory trigger, it is the correct answer among the choices. The 6th (abducens) and 11th (accessory) nerves are purely motor, and the 7th (facial) nerve has different sensory and motor functions.

Question 6

Through which secondary messenger system do β₂-adrenergic receptors exert their effects?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Beta-2 adrenergic receptors are a type of G-protein coupled receptor (GPCR). Specifically, they are coupled to a stimulatory G-protein (Gs). When a ligand like epinephrine binds to the beta-2 receptor, it activates the Gs protein. This activated protein, in turn, stimulates the membrane-bound enzyme adenylate cyclase. Adenylate cyclase then catalyzes the conversion of ATP into the second messenger, cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). The increased intracellular levels of cAMP activate Protein Kinase A (PKA), which then phosphorylates various downstream targets to produce the final physiological effect, such as bronchodilation or vasodilation. Tyrosine kinase receptors (B) are a different class of receptors used by insulin and growth factors.

Question 7

A child with dehydration is treated with oral rehydration solution (ORS). Which receptor or transporter in the gastrointestinal tract facilitates glucose absorption in this process?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The effectiveness of Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS) is based on the coupled transport of sodium and glucose in the small intestine. The absorption of glucose from the intestinal lumen into the enterocytes is primarily mediated by the Sodium-Glucose Linked Transporter 1 (SGLT1). This transporter is located on the apical membrane of intestinal epithelial cells. It functions by using the electrochemical gradient of sodium to co-transport one molecule of sodium along with one molecule of glucose into the cell. This process is a form of secondary active transport. The absorption of sodium and glucose creates an osmotic gradient that drives the passive absorption of water, effectively rehydrating the patient. SGLT2 (B) is found in the kidneys, while GLUT transporters (C, D) are involved in facilitated diffusion of glucose.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The bodys fluid compartments have distinct ionic compositions. The values provided (K+ 140 mEq/L, Na+ 10 mEq/L) are classic for the intracellular environment. The given ion profile is the inverse of the ECF and is definitively representative of the intracellular fluid compartment.

A young patient presents with muscle spasms, tingling in the hands and feet, seizures, and episodes of breathing difficulty due to laryngospasm, and laboratory investigations show an electrolyte abnormality. What is the most likely underlying cause of these symptoms?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The patients symptoms of muscle spasms (tetany), perioral and extremity numbness (paresthesias), seizures, and laryngospasm are all signs of acute hypocalcemia. Specifically, they are caused by a decrease in the level of ionized calcium in the blood. Acute respiratory alkalosis, often caused by hyperventilation, is a common cause of these symptoms. The increase in blood pH (alkalosis) causes more calcium to bind to albumin, which decreases the physiologically active ionized calcium fraction. This reduction in ionized calcium increases neuromuscular excitability, leading to tetany and the other neurological symptoms described. While other electrolyte imbalances can cause some of these symptoms, the full constellation in an acute setting is highly suggestive of symptomatic hypocalcemia secondary to respiratory alkalosis.

Question 16

Corneal transparency is primarily maintained by which glycosaminoglycan (GAG)?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The transparency of the cornea is a remarkable feature that depends on the highly organized and uniform arrangement of its collagen fibrils within the stroma. The specific glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) present in the corneal stroma play a crucial role in maintaining this precise architecture and hydration level. The predominant GAGs in the cornea are keratan sulfate and chondroitin sulfate. Defects in keratan sulfate metabolism, as seen in macular corneal dystrophy, lead to corneal clouding.

Question 140

A child receives prophylactic irradiation as part of conditioning before bone marrow transplantation for acute lymphoblastic leukemia. Which type of cell is least susceptible to damage from radiation in this setting?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The sensitivity of cells to ionizing radiation is described by the Law of Bergonié and Tribondeau, which states that radiosensitivity is directly proportional to the rate of cell division and inversely proportional to the degree of cell differentiation. Cells that are rapidly dividing and poorly differentiated are the most sensitive. Tissues like bone marrow, intestinal epithelium, and spermatogonia are composed of rapidly proliferating cells and are therefore highly radiosensitive. In contrast, neurons are highly specialized, fully differentiated cells that are post-mitotic (they do not divide in adulthood). This makes them one of the most radioresistant cell types in the body. While very high doses of radiation can cause neuronal damage, they are significantly less affected by conventional radiation doses compared to the rapidly renewing tissues listed in the other options.
