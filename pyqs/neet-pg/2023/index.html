Question 4

In a 35-year-old patient with intravascular hemolysis, the serum potassium level rises to 5.5 mEq/L. What effect will this have on the resting membrane potential in this patient?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The resting membrane potential (RMP) is primarily established by the potassium (K+) concentration gradient across the cell membrane, with a higher concentration inside the cell. The RMP is negative due to the constant efflux of positive K+ ions. Intravascular hemolysis releases K+ from red blood cells, causing hyperkalemia (increased extracellular K+). This reduces the concentration gradient between the intracellular and extracellular compartments. According to the Nernst and Goldman-Hodgkin-Katz equations, a smaller K+ gradient reduces the driving force for K+ to exit the cell. With less positive charge leaving the cell, the interior becomes less negative, meaning the RMP shifts to a more positive value (e.g., from -70mV to -60mV). This depolarization moves the RMP closer to the threshold for firing an action potential, initially increasing excitability.

Question 5

In cases of infant respiratory distress syndrome (IRDS), there is a deficiency of which specific lipid?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Infant respiratory distress syndrome (IRDS) is caused by a deficiency of pulmonary surfactant in the lungs of premature newborns. Surfactant is a complex that reduces alveolar surface tension, preventing the air sacs from collapsing at the end of expiration. The most crucial component of surfactant is a phospholipid called dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine (DPPC). DPPC is a specific type of lecithin, which is a general term for any phosphatidylcholine. In IRDS, the production of lecithin is inadequate, leading to respiratory failure. The other lipids listed are not the primary deficient components.

Question 8

A family that predominantly consumes polished rice presents with signs of a vitamin deficiency. Which vitamin deficiency is likely, and which investigation is used to detect it?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

Polished rice is rice that has had its outer bran and germ layers removed. These layers are the primary dietary source of Thiamine (Vitamin B1). A diet heavily reliant on polished rice is a classic cause of thiamine deficiency, which leads to the condition known as beriberi. The functional status of thiamine in the body is biochemically assessed by measuring the activity of the enzyme transketolase in red blood cells. Transketolase, an enzyme in the pentose phosphate pathway, requires thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP) as a cofactor. In a thiamine-deficient state, the enzymes baseline activity is low, but it increases significantly when TPP is added to the test tube, confirming the deficiency.

Question 9

When the insulin-to-glucagon ratio is low, which of the following enzymes is stimulated?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

A low insulin-to-glucagon ratio is the hormonal signal of the fasting state, indicating a need to mobilize stored energy. In adipose tissue, glucagon and low insulin levels lead to the activation of Hormone-Sensitive Lipase (HSL). HSL is the key intracellular enzyme that catalyzes the hydrolysis of stored triglycerides into free fatty acids and glycerol. These products are then released into the bloodstream to be used as fuel by other tissues like the liver and muscle, through processes like beta-oxidation and gluconeogenesis. In contrast, insulin (a high insulin:glucagon ratio) stimulates enzymes involved in storage, such as Lipoprotein lipase (A) for fat uptake, Phosphofructokinase I (C) for glycolysis, and Glycogen synthase (D) for glycogen synthesis.

Question 10

A 4-year-old child presents with easy fatigability. The mother reports that the child becomes very hungry between meals but recovers after eating. Liver examination shows no glycogen storage. What is the most likely diagnosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The clinical presentation of fasting hypoglycemia (fatigue and hunger between meals) that is relieved by feeding, combined with the definitive finding of an absence of glycogen in the liver, points to a defect in glycogen synthesis. The key enzyme responsible for creating glycogen from glucose is glycogen synthase. A deficiency in this enzyme (Glycogen Storage Disease Type 0) means the liver cannot store glucose as glycogen after a meal. As a result, during a fast, the liver has no glycogen stores to break down (glycogenolysis) to maintain blood glucose levels, leading to hypoglycemia. Deficiencies in branching (A) or debranching (B) enzymes lead to abnormal glycogen structures, not absence. Glucose-6-phosphatase deficiency (C) leads to a massive accumulation of normal glycogen.

Correct answer: B

This clinical presentation is highly characteristic of Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia (CLL), a malignancy of mature B-lymphocytes. Question 14

Which of the following statements are true regarding the relative risk associated with Mendelian patterns of inheritance?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

This question asks for the correct recurrence risk for an affected child in a typical Mendelian inheritance scenario. For an autosomal recessive disorder, an affected child (genotype aa) is most commonly born to two heterozygous carrier parents (genotype Aa). In such a cross (Aa x Aa), the probability for each pregnancy is as follows: a 1 in 4 chance of having an affected child (aa), a 2 in 4 (or 1/2) chance of having an unaffected carrier child (Aa), and a 1 in 4 chance of having an unaffected, non-carrier child (AA). Therefore, the risk of having another child affected with the same autosomal recessive condition is 1/4 or 25%. The other options are incorrect for standard Mendelian patterns.

Uric acid is the final product of purine metabolism. The enzyme xanthine oxidase catalyzes the last two steps of this pathway. Allopurinol is a xanthine oxidase inhibitor. Question 128

A patient diagnosed with schizophrenia did not respond to treatment with haloperidol and thioridazine. He was started on another medication, after which he developed side effects such as excessive salivation (sialorrhea), dyslipidemia, and hyperglycemia. Which drug was most likely initiated?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

This patient has treatment-resistant schizophrenia, defined by the failure to respond to adequate trials of at least two different antipsychotic medications (haloperidol and thioridazine). The drug of choice and the only medication approved for treatment-resistant schizophrenia is clozapine. Furthermore, the side effect profile described is highly characteristic of clozapine. It is known for causing significant metabolic side effects, including dyslipidemia and hyperglycemia (risk of new-onset diabetes). Uniquely among antipsychotics, it frequently causes sialorrhea (excessive salivation), whereas most others tend to cause dry mouth.
