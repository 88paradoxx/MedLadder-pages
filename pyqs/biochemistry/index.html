Question 5

A patient has elevated phenylalanine levels despite normal phenylalanine hydroxylase activity. Deficiency of which cofactor is most likely responsible for this finding?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) catalyzes the conversion of phenylalanine to tyrosine. This reaction requires a specific cofactor, tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4). In classic Phenylketonuria (PKU), the PAH enzyme itself is deficient. However, a less common cause of hyperphenylalaninemia is a defect in the synthesis or recycling of the BH4 cofactor. Question 8

A frameshift mutation caused by insertion of a nucleotide at the 4th position of a 900-base mRNA occurs. What is the most likely consequence of this mutation?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The genetic code is read in non-overlapping triplets of nucleotides called codons. A frameshift mutation occurs when the number of inserted or deleted nucleotides is not a multiple of three. In this case, a single nucleotide is inserted at position 4. This is highly disruptive because it shifts the reading frame for the entire downstream sequence of the mRNA. The insertion at position 4 will alter the second codon (bases 4, 5, 6) and every subsequent codon thereafter. This will lead to the incorporation of a completely different sequence of amino acids from that point onward, resulting in a complete change in the protein product. Furthermore, a frameshift mutation often introduces a premature stop codon, which would lead to the truncation of the protein. The final protein will be completely different and almost certainly non-functional.

Question 18

In a patient receiving phenobarbitone therapy, which complex of the electron transport chain is inhibited by this drug?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Barbiturates, such as phenobarbitone and amobarbital, are known inhibitors of the mitochondrial electron transport chain. Similar to rotenone, they act by blocking the electron transfer within Complex I (NADH dehydrogenase). They specifically inhibit the transfer of electrons from the iron-sulfur centers within Complex I to ubiquinone (coenzyme Q). This inhibition disrupts the flow of electrons originating from NADH, thereby reducing the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane and severely impairing ATP synthesis through oxidative phosphorylation. This action contributes to the central nervous system depression seen with barbiturate overdose. The other complexes (II, III, and IV) are not directly inhibited by phenobarbitone, although the overall function of the chain is compromised due to the block at Complex I.

Question 38

A patient with tuberculosis on isoniazid therapy for six months presents with tingling and paresthesia. Deficiency of which vitamin is the most likely cause?

A well-known side effect of the anti-tuberculosis drug isoniazid (INH) is a dose-dependent peripheral neuropathy, which presents with symptoms like tingling, numbness, and paresthesias in a glove and stocking distribution. The most common type of gallstones found in Western populations are cholesterol gallstones. These form when the bile in the gallbladder becomes supersaturated with cholesterol. Normally, cholesterol is kept soluble in bile through the formation of micelles with bile salts and phospholipids. Question 96

What do chaperones assist in:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Chaperones, also known as heat shock proteins (HSPs), are a functionally related group of proteins whose primary role is to assist in the correct folding of other proteins. As a newly synthesized polypeptide chain emerges from the ribosome, it must fold into a specific, stable three-dimensional conformation to become biologically active. This process is complex and prone to errors, such as misfolding and aggregation. Chaperones bind to unfolded or partially folded polypeptide chains, preventing them from aggregating and facilitating their correct folding into the proper functional structure. They do not form part of the final protein structure but act as catalysts for the folding process. They are not primarily involved in cleavage (A), degradation (C), or post-translational modification (D) of proteins.
