Question 1

A posthumous child is:

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

The term posthumous is derived from the Latin postumus, meaning last, and in this context, it refers to something that occurs or is published after someone's death. In legal and common language, a posthumous child is specifically defined as a child who is born after the death of their biological father. This situation has significant legal implications, particularly concerning matters of inheritance, succession, and legitimacy. Most legal systems have provisions to ensure that a posthumous child is granted the same rights as a child born during the father's lifetime. For example, the child is generally considered an heir to the deceased father's estate. The term does not apply to a child delivered after the mother's death (which would typically involve a postmortem cesarean section) or after both parents' deaths, although the word could be used more broadly in a non-legal sense. Abandonment by parents is a separate social and legal issue.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: D

The choice of surgical procedure to correct congenital ptosis (drooping of the upper eyelid) depends primarily on the function of the levator palpebrae superioris muscle, which is the main muscle responsible for elevating the eyelid. This allows the child to lift their eyelid by raising their eyebrow.

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

Brimonidine is a topical alpha-2 adrenergic agonist used to lower intraocular pressure in the treatment of glaucoma. While it is generally safe in adults, it is absolutely contraindicated in infants and young children (typically under the age of 2). Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: B

Chronic poisoning with the heavy metal cadmium has characteristic effects on the musculoskeletal and renal systems. Cadmium accumulates in the renal cortex and is highly nephrotoxic, primarily damaging the proximal renal tubules. While other metals can cause nephropathy (mercury , lead ) or neuropathy causing pain (arsenic ), the classic combination of nephropathy with severe muscle and bone pain is most strongly associated with chronic cadmium toxicity.

Question 14

The Australian antigen for hepatitis B is?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The Australia antigen is the original name given to the Hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg). It was discovered in 1965 by Dr. Baruch Blumberg in the serum of an Australian Aboriginal person, hence the name. The other markers, HBeAg (e antigen) and HBcAg (core antigen), are different components of the virus, and HBV DNA is the viral genetic material.

Question 18

Echinococcus granulosus is most commonly found in which of the following animals?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

Echinococcus granulosus is a cestode (tapeworm) that causes cystic echinococcosis (hydatid disease) in humans. The parasite has a complex two-host lifecycle. The definitive host, where the adult tapeworm lives in the intestine, is a canid, most commonly the dog. The adult worm in the dog's intestine produces eggs, which are passed in the feces. The intermediate hosts are typically herbivores, such as sheep, goats, cattle, and pigs, which become infected by ingesting the eggs. In the intermediate host, the eggs hatch, and the larvae migrate to organs like the liver and lungs, where they form the characteristic hydatid cysts. The lifecycle is completed when a dog ingests the cyst-containing organs of an infected intermediate host. Humans are accidental intermediate hosts, becoming infected by ingesting the eggs from soil or food contaminated with dog feces, or through direct contact with infected dogs. Cats are definitive hosts for other tapeworms like Taenia taeniaeformis.

Question 35

Which region of the scaphoid bone is most prone to developing avascular necrosis?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: C

The scaphoid bone of the wrist has a tenuous and unique blood supply. The majority of its blood supply (70-80%) enters through small foramina in the dorsal ridge, located in its distal half, and then flows in a retrograde fashion to supply the proximal part of the bone. The proximal pole (proximal 1/3rd) has a very limited or non-existent direct blood supply. When the scaphoid is fractured, particularly through its waist (the middle 1/3rd), this retrograde blood supply to the proximal fragment can be completely disrupted. This interruption of blood flow makes the proximal pole highly susceptible to developing avascular necrosis (AVN), a condition where the bone tissue dies due to a lack of blood supply. The risk of AVN increases significantly with more proximal fractures. This complication can lead to non-union of the fracture, collapse of the bone, and subsequent degenerative arthritis of the wrist.

A patient receives sevoflurane and succinylcholine for abdominal surgery and subsequently develops postoperative muscle rigidity. What is the drug of choice for management?

Show answer and explanation

Correct answer: A

The patient's presentation of muscle rigidity and likely hyperthermia after receiving a volatile anesthetic (sevoflurane) and a depolarizing muscle relaxant (succinylcholine) is the classic picture of malignant hyperthermia (MH). MH is a life-threatening pharmacogenetic disorder of skeletal muscle calcium regulation. In susceptible individuals, these triggering agents cause a massive, uncontrolled release of calcium from the sarcoplasmic reticulum, leading to sustained muscle contraction (rigidity), a hypermetabolic state, rhabdomyolysis, and a rapid rise in body temperature. The specific and life-saving antidote for malignant hyperthermia is dantrolene.
