About Acute Myeloid Leukemia (AML) MCQs

Sample MCQ #1

The anaemia associated with leukaemia is ?

AIron deficiency
BMegaloblastic type
CMyelophthisic type
DAll of above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Myelophthisic type

Ans. is 'c' i.e., Myelophthisis type Myelophthisic anemia Space - occupying lesions that destroy significant amount of bone marrow or disturb the marrow architecture, depress its productive capacity. This form of anemia is referred to as myelophthisic anemia. Characteristically, immature erythroid and myeloid progenitors appears in the peripheral blood —> Leukoerythroblastosis. Causes are Metastatic cancer (M.C. cause) Granulomatous disease infiltrating bone marrow. Myeloproliferative disorders (such as myeloid leukemia) Myelofibrosis

Sample MCQ #2

Child with chloromas. Next step in evaluation.

APlatelet count
BLeukocyte count
CHb
DPeripheral smear
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Peripheral smear

In chloroma, there is extramedullary proliferation of blasts so its necessary to do a bone marrow evaluation to rule out concomitant leukemia. since bone marrow is not in option, peripheral smear is the next best answer. Since if the blasts are present in peripheral smear, it is suggestive of leukemia Reference: Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases 10 th edition, chapter 13, pg 592

Sample MCQ #3

All Trans' retinoic acid is used in the treatment of tumor associated with:

ABCR-ABL
BPML-RARA
CcMYC
DCEBPA
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. PML-RARA

All trans retinoic acid is used in the treatment of Acute promyelocytic leukemia Acute promyelocytic leukemia (M3) with t(15;17) (q22;q12); PML/RAR-a Therapy with the retinoic acid analogue all trans retinoic acid can result in maturation of these leukemic cells and clinical remission Reference: Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases 10 th edition, chapter 13, pg 588-590

Sample MCQ #4

A patient presented with painless b/l proptosis. What is the next investigation to diagnose it as chloroma?

ABlood hemoglobin
BPeripheral smear
CPlatelets
DBone marrow (reticulin)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Peripheral smear

Myeloid (granulocytic) sarcoma, or myeloblastoma are extramedullary blast proliferation.These tumors are called chloromas because some appear green or turn green in dilute acid secondary to expression of MPO. The tumors are usually localized; they often involve bone, periosteum, soft tissues, lymph nodes, or skin. Common sites are the orbit and the paranasal sinuses. The diagnosis can be made, if Auer rods are detected on blasts in peripheral smear or if myeloid origin is confirmed by cytochemical or immunohistochemical methods. The diagnosis should be suspected, if eosinophilic myelocytes are present in hematoxylin and eosin–stained biopsy sections. Imprint preparations can be helpful. Reference: Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases 10 th edition, chapter 13, pg 601

Sample MCQ #5 · Image-based

A 35-year-old male patient presented to causality complaining easy tiredness and weakness since 1 year. CBC performed shows high TLC and automated differential counts suggested monocytosis. Peripheral smear examination was done and shown below. Based on characteristic features what is your probable diagnosis

AALL
BAML, M2
CAML, M3
DAML, M5
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. AML, M3

The nuclear size and shape in the abnormal promyelocytes of the hypergranular APML are of then bilobed and kidney shaped. The cytoplasm of these cells have dense granules obscuring the nuclear border. Characteristics cells have bundle of Auer rod in their cytoplasm called as faggot cells.

Reference: Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases 10th edition, Chapter 13, pg 588-589
