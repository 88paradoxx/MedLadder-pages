Sample Gall Bladder and Pancreas MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 45-year-old male patient who is chronic alcoholic drinker and also having uncontrolled diabetes since 5 years presented with sudden onset on pain abdomen and vomiting. Pain is intense and referred to back. Patient was sent for ultrasound, which revealed diagnosis of acute pancreatitis and conservative management was done. If biopsy was taken at this stage, what will be the histopathological findings you observe?

AParenchymal fibrosis
BAcinar atrophy
CVariable sized duct dilation
DNecrotizing pancreatitis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Necrotizing pancreatitis

(a, b, c) Chronic pancreatitis is characterized by parenchymal fibrosis, acinar atrophy and dropout, and variable ductal dilation. (d) In acute necrotizing pancreatitis, acini, ducts, and even islets undergo necrosis. In the most severe form, hemorrhagic pancreatitis, extensive parenchymal necrosis is accompanied by intraparenchymal hemorrhage due to vascular injury. Reference: Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Diseases 10 th edition Chapter 18, pg 882

Sample MCQ #2

A 40-year-old female have gall stones since 5 years. Now since 2 weeks she had intermittent pain abdomen. Now she presented in emergency with acute severe pain in the right upper quadrant associated with mild fever, nausea, vomiting and tachycardia. He underwent laparoscopic cholecystectomy and specimen sent for histopathological examination. What are the histological findings to be seen in chronic cholecystitis?

AMural hypertrophy
BMild to moderate lymphocytic infiltration
CSubserosal fibrosis
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

All of these are features of chronic cholecystitis.

Sample MCQ #3

Empyema of the gallbladder and Gangrenous cholecystitis are seen in-

AAcute Calculous Cholecystitis
BAcute Acalculous Cholecystitis
CChronic Cholecystitis
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Acute Calculous Cholecystitis

In acute cholecystitis, the gallbladder usually is enlarged and tense, and has a bright red or blotchy, violaceous colour, the latter imparted by subserosal hemorrhages. The serosa frequently is covered by a fibrinous or, in severe cases, a fibrinopurulent exudate. In 90% of cases, stones are present, often obstructing the neck of the gallbladder or the cystic duct. The gallbladder lumen is filled with cloudy or turbid bile that may contain fibrin, blood, and pus. When the contained exudate is mostly pus, the condition is referred to as empyema of the gallbladder. In mild cases, the gallbladder wall is thickened, edematous, and hyperemic. In more severe cases, the gallbladder wall is green-black and necrotic-a condition termed gangrenous cholecystitis. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th ed, Pg-674

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following is/are true for the pancreatic pseudocyst? i. Lack of epithelial lining ii. Contains necrotic and haemorrhagic material iii. Requires surgical removal if more than 6.0 cm iv. It arises following a bout of acute pancreatitis

AOnly i, ii, and iii are correct
BOnly i and iii are correct
COnly ii and iv are correct
DAll are correct
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All are correct

Pancreatic pseudocyst is a poorly defined cyst with a necrotic brownish wall. Histologically, the cyst lacks an epithelial lining and instead is lined by fibrin and granulation tissue, with typical changes of chronic inflammation. Signs and symptoms of pancreatic pseudocyst include abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting and bloating. Diagnosis of pancreatic pseudocyst can be based on cystic fluid analysis. Complications of pancreatic pseudocyst include infection, hemorrhage, obstruction and rupture. Pseudo cysts typically arise following a bout of acute pancreatitis, particularly one superimposed on chronic alcholic pancreatitis. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th edition, Pg-684

Sample MCQ #5

Persons with pancreatic divisum and other genetic abnormalities are prone to develop chronic pancreatitis. BECAUSE In pancreatic divisum the bulk of pancreas drains into duodenum through minor papillae.

AIf both statements are true and causally related
BIf both statements are true but not causally related
CIf the first statement is true and second is false
DIf the first statement is false and second is true
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. If both statements are true and causally related

Pancreas divisum is the most common congenital anomaly of the pancreas. More than 95% of individuals are asymptomatic. The remaining 5% develop acute or chronic pancreatitis, possibly related to inadequate drainage of pancreatic secretions through the minor papilla. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th edition, Pg-680

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following is/are TRUE for pancreatic carcinoma? i. 60-80 years of age ii. Associated with cigarette smoking iii. It is the infiltrating ductal carcinoma of pancreas iv. There is an activating mutation of CDKN2A

AOnly i, ii, and iii are correct
BOnly i and iii are correct
COnly ii and iv are correct
DOnly iv is correct
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Only i, ii, and iii are correct

The recent sequencing of the pancreatic cancer genome has confirmed that four genes are most commonly affected by somatic mutations in this neoplasm: KRAS, CDKN2A/ p16, SMAD4, and TP53: KRAS is the most frequently altered oncogene in pancreatic cancer; it is activated by a point mutation in greater than 90% of cases. Cdkn2a (chromosome 9p ) is inactivated in 30% of pancreatic cancers through point mutations or homozygous deletions Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th edition, Pg-687

Sample MCQ #7

Which of the following is/are the characteristic(s) of pancreatic carcinoma? i. High invasiveness ii. Trousseau sign iii. Desmoplastic response iv. Murphy's sign

AOnly i, ii, and iii are correct
BOnly i and iii are correct
COnly ii and iv are correct
DOnly iv is correct
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Only i, ii, and iii are correct

Migratory thrombophlebitis (Trousseau syndrome) occurs in about 10% of patients and is attributable to the elaboration of platelet-aggregating factors and pro-coagulants from the tumour or its necrotic products. Murphy's sign is elicited in patient with acute cholecystitis. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th edition, Pg-686-689

Sample MCQ #8

Patient presented with pain in abdomen. His symptoms are relieved by steroids and diagnosis is autoimmune pancreatitis. Which of the following finding(s) is/are TRUE for the autoimmune pancreatitis? i. Ductocentric mixed inflammatory infiltrate ii. Increased IgG 4 related plasma cells iii. Presence of venulitis iv. Clinical presentation can mimic pancreatic carcinoma.

AOnly i, ii, and iii are correct
BOnly i and iii are correct
COnly ii and iv are correct
DAll are correct
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All are correct

Autoimmune pancreatitis is a pathogenically distinct form of chronic pancreatitis that is associated with the presence of IgG4-secreting plasma cells in the pancreas. Autoimmune pancreatitis is one manifestation of IgG related disease, which may involve multiple tissues. It is important to recognize because it responds to steroid therapy. Reference: Robbins Basic Pathology, 10 th edition, Pg-683-84
