Oral glucose stimulates the release of certain hormones from GIT that acts on beta cells of pancreas to increase insulin secretion. Such GI hormones are termed as Incretins Incretin hormones are: Glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP1) - most potent incretin Gastric inhibitory peptide (GIP) or Glucose dependent insulinotropic peptide Reference: Ganong, 25th edition page no.434

Sample MCQ #7 · Image-based

A 45-year-old man schedules an appointment with his physician for a follow-up visit for diabetes mellitus. The postprandial abdominal fullness he complained of during his last visit has resolved with regular exercise and metformin. He also mentions that his urinary frequency has decreased. He remembers seeing a positive value for glucose in his urine lab report, and is now concerned that if he urinates less, "the sugar won't be expelled". His labs from the last and current visit are shown below. Last visit Urinalysis Urinalysis Current visit Serum glucose (fasting) - 88 mg/dL Serum electrolytes: The physician advises him that his urinary frequency and the positive urinary glucose result were both pathologic complications of diabetes mellitus. Which of the following best explains the initial urinalysis findings in this patient?

ALack of insulin control in reabsorption of glucose from the tubule
BImpaired utilization of glucose by the kidney
CSecretion of glucose in the proximal convoluted tubule
DSaturation of glucose reabsorption proximal convoluted tubule
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Saturation of glucose reabsorption proximal convoluted tubule

Glucose is freely filtered in the glomerulus and completely reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule, and does not appear in the urine until it exceeds the normal accepted upper range.

A (random) blood glucose level of or above 200 mg/dL is diagnostic of diabetes mellitus and it is also at this level that glucose appears in the urine.

At 375 mg/dL and above, glucose transporters in the proximal convoluted tubule are saturated and the giucose content of urine increases significantly.

The reason for this range (200-375 mg/dL) is that each nephron has its own threshold value of glucose absorption, after which the transporters are saturated (glucose in the tubule moves against a concentration gradient and requires active transport).

Furthermore, some studies have shown that diabetics might have higher thresholds for the appearance of glucose in the urine.

Reference: Guyton & Hall, 14th edition pg no. 521
