Sample MCQ #1

A child presented to the clinic with history of cough, coryza, conjunctivitis and fever. There is fever and rash which arises behind the ears and is spreading all over chest, back and abdomen. What is the diagnosis?

AMeasles
BRubella
CDengue
DChicken pox
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Measles

Measles, also known as rubeola, is a highly contagious viral infection caused by the measles virus. It typically presents with a characteristic set of symptoms, including:

  • Fever: Measles often begins with a high fever, which can reach up to 104°F (40°C).
  • Cough: Measles is associated with a dry, hacking cough.
  • Coryza: This refers to inflammation of the mucous membranes in the nose, leading to nasal congestion, sneezing, and a runny nose.
  • Conjunctivitis: Measles can cause redness, irritation, and discharge in the eyes, known as conjunctivitis or "pink eye."
  • Koplik's spots: These are small white spots with a bluish-white center on a red background, which appear inside the mouth on the mucous membrane opposite the molars, typically around two days before the rash.
  • Rash: The hallmark symptom of measles is a red, blotchy rash that typically starts behind the ears and then spreads downward to involve the face, neck, chest, back, and abdomen. The rash usually lasts for several days and may be accompanied by intense itching.

Explanation to why other options are not correct:

B. Rubella: Rubella, also known as German measles, shares some similarities with measles, such as fever and rash. However, rubella typically presents with a milder illness characterized by a shorter duration of fever and a rash that starts on the face and spreads downward. Additionally, rubella is less likely to cause cough, coryza, and conjunctivitis compared to measles.

C. Dengue: Dengue fever is a viral infection transmitted by mosquitoes. It typically presents with symptoms such as high fever, severe headache, joint and muscle pain, and a characteristic rash. However, the rash in dengue fever usually appears later in the course of the illness and tends to be a maculopapular rash (flat, red spots) rather than the blotchy, erythematous rash seen in measles.

Chickenpox: Chickenpox, caused by the varicella-zoster virus, presents with a rash characterized by vesicles (fluid-filled blisters) on a red base, often described as "dewdrops on a rose petal." The rash typically starts on the face, scalp, and trunk before spreading to other parts of the body. While chickenpox can cause fever and may be accompanied by cough and other symptoms, the distribution and appearance of the rash are different from those seen in measles.

In summary, based on the characteristic symptoms described in the scenario, including fever, cough, coryza, conjunctivitis, and a rash that starts behind the ears and spreads downward, the most likely diagnosis is measles (option A). Therefore, option A is the correct answer.

Reference - Park's Preventive and social medicine, 27th EDITION Pg 164

Sample MCQ #2

Immunization for elderly is recommended for Influenza vaccine. Which of the following is the Other vaccine preventable disease in elderly ?

ADiphtheria
BMeningococci
CRota virus infection
DZoster
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Zoster

Immunization for the elderly is crucial to protect them from vaccine-preventable diseases, as aging can weaken the immune system and make individuals more susceptible to infections. While influenza vaccination is widely recommended for the elderly due to the increased risk of severe complications from influenza viruses, there are other vaccine-preventable diseases that also pose a threat to this age group.

D. Zoster: Zoster, commonly known as shingles, is caused by the reactivation of the varicella-zoster virus, the same virus that causes chickenpox. The risk of developing shingles increases with age, particularly after 50 years old, as immunity to the varicella-zoster virus declines over time. Therefore, vaccination against shingles with the zoster vaccine is recommended for adults aged 50 years and older to reduce the risk of developing shingles and its complications, including postherpetic neuralgia.

Explanation to why other options are not correct:

A. Diphtheria: Diphtheria is a bacterial infection caused by Corynebacterium diphtheriae. While diphtheria vaccination is important in childhood, routine booster doses are not typically recommended for the elderly unless they have not been adequately vaccinated in the past. Diphtheria is more prevalent in younger age groups, and the risk of infection decreases with age, so routine diphtheria immunization is not a priority for the elderly.

B. Meningococci: Meningococcal disease is caused by the bacterium Neisseria meningitidis and can lead to severe infections such as meningitis and septicemia. While older adults are at a lower risk compared to infants and adolescents, they can still be susceptible to meningococcal disease, particularly those with certain medical conditions or those traveling to areas with high rates of disease. However, routine immunization against meningococcal disease is not generally recommended for all elderly individuals.

C. Rota virus infection: Rotavirus primarily affects infants and young children, causing gastroenteritis with symptoms such as diarrhea and vomiting. While rotavirus vaccination is important for infants to prevent severe illness, it is not recommended for the elderly population, as they are not at high risk for rotavirus infection.

In summary, while influenza vaccination is crucial for the elderly, vaccination against shingles (zoster) is also recommended due to the increased risk of shingles with age. Options A, B, and C are not commonly recommended for routine immunization in the elderly population. Therefore, option (D) Zoster is the correct answer.

Reference - Park's Preventive and social medicine, 27th EDITION Pg 163

Sample MCQ #3 · Image-based

Which virus causes antigen shift?

AA
BB
CC
DD
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. A

Antigenic shift is a process that occurs in influenza viruses, particularly influenza A viruses. It happens when there is a major change in the influenza virus's surface antigens, hemagglutinin (HA), and neuraminidase (NA), leading to the emergence of a new influenza virus subtype. This process typically occurs when two different influenza A viruses infect the same host cell and exchange genetic material. As a result, a novel influenza A virus with a new HA and/or NA subtype can emerge.

It is important to note that antigenic shift is specific to influenza viruses, particularly influenza A viruses, and is not associated with other viruses.

Therefore the right answer is option A.
