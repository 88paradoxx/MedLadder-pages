Arules out disease

✓ Correct Answer: B. rules in disease

Highly specific test, when positive, rules in a disease. Reference: Park&rsquo;s Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine, 25 th Edition, pg- 153

Sample MCQ #2

A researcher finds a sensitive test to be positive for a person. For diagnosis, which of the following will hold true?

AIt confirms that the person does not have the disease
Bit suggests that if the disease is rare, the person surely has the disease
Cit confirms that the person has the disease if the disease is common
Dit is an expected finding in case the disease is prevalent
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. it confirms that the person has the disease if the disease is common

Sensitivity of a screening test is the ability of the test to identify correctly those who have the disease. It confirms that the person has the disease if the disease is common. Reference: Park&rsquo;s Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine, 25 th Edition, pg- 152

Sample MCQ #3

A test for Hepatitis C is performed on 200 patients with biopsy-proven disease and 200 patients free of the disease. The test shows positive results on 180 of the patients with the disease and negative results on 150 of the patients without the disease. Which of the following is appropriate?

AHas a positive predictive value of 90%
BHas a negative predictive value of 75%
CHas a sensitivity of 90%
DHas a specificity of 82.5%
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Has a sensitivity of 90%

To determine the appropriate statement, let's define the terms: Positive Predictive Value (PPV): The probability that a person with a positive test result truly has the disease. Negative Predictive Value (NPV): The probability that a person with a negative test result truly does not have the disease. Sensitivity: The proportion of true positive cases that the test correctly identifies. Specificity: The proportion of true negative cases that the test correctly identifies. Given the information: True positives (TP): 180 True negatives (TN): 150 False positives (FP): 50 (200 - 150) False negatives (FN): 20 (200 - 180) Sensitivity=TP/(FN+TP)=180/(20+180)=180/200=0.9 = 90% Reference: Park&rsquo;s Textbook of Preventive and Social Medicine, 25th Edition, pg- 152
