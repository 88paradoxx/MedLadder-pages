Sample MCQ #1

Which one of the following is not a socio-pathological factor associated with mental illness?

AEmotional stress
BFrustration
CEndocrine diseases
DAnxiety
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Endocrine diseases
  • Endocrine diseases:
    • Definition: These are physiological conditions affecting hormone levels and bodily functions, such as diabetes or thyroid disorders.
    • Classification: While endocrine diseases can have secondary effects on mental health (e.g., mood swings due to hormonal changes), they are not classified as socio-pathological factors because they originate from biological or medical causes rather than social circumstances.
  • Socio-pathological factors:
    • Emotional stress: This arises from external pressures, relationships, or life changes, significantly impacting mental health.
    • Frustration: Often stemming from unmet needs or desires, leading to negative emotions and potential mental health issues.
    • Anxiety: Typically linked to social factors such as job loss, financial problems, or relationship issues, which can exacerbate mental health disorders.

Why Not Other Options:

  • (a) Emotional stress: A significant socio-pathological factor contributing to mental illness due to its origins in interpersonal and situational challenges.
  • (b) Frustration: Closely related to emotional stress, it is often a response to life events that can lead to mental health issues.
  • (d) Anxiety: Commonly tied to social contexts and experiences, making it a clear socio-pathological factor.

Key Takeaways:

  • Differentiation: It's crucial to differentiate between socio-pathological factors (rooted in social and emotional experiences) and organic or physiological conditions (like endocrine diseases) when assessing mental health.
  • Complex Interplay: While endocrine diseases can influence mental health, they operate independently of socio-pathological factors, which are often more directly tied to individual experiences and social environment.

Sample MCQ #2

Chronological age 10 years, mental age 4 yrs. What is that person called as

AIdiot
BImbecile
CNormal
DGenius
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Imbecile

b. Imbecile: Historically, the term "imbecile" was used to describe individuals with moderate intellectual disability, typically characterized by an IQ between 25 and 50. These individuals may have significant challenges in adaptive functioning and intellectual tasks but are not as severely impaired as those classified as "idiots."

Explanation for why not the other options:

a. Idiot: This term historically referred to individuals with severe intellectual disabilities, typically characterized by an IQ below 25 or 30. However, it is no longer used in clinical or psychological contexts due to its derogatory nature and lack of specificity. The term "idiot" is considered offensive and outdated.

c.Normal: Given the significant discrepancy between chronological age (10 years) and mental age (4 years), it would not be appropriate to classify this individual as "normal." The term "normal" typically refers to individuals whose cognitive abilities and developmental milestones are consistent with their chronological age.

d.Genius: A person with a mental age significantly above their chronological age would not be classified as a "genius." "Genius" typically refers to individuals with exceptional intellectual abilities, often characterized by an IQ well above average and significant achievements in their field of expertise.

Given the scenario described, where the individual's mental age is significantly lower than their chronological age, the most appropriate classification would be "imbecile." This term historically referred to individuals with moderate intellectual disability, aligning more closely with the described situation than the other options provided.

Sample MCQ #3

Mental retardation term is replaced by

AMental deficiency
BMental lack
CMentally challenged
DIntellectual disability
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Intellectual disability

Option D: "Intellectual disability" is the preferred and more respectful term used to describe significant limitations in both intellectual functioning and adaptive behaviour. It focuses on the person's abilities rather than their limitations and is widely accepted in medical and social contexts. This term emphasizes the importance of providing support and accommodations to enable individuals with intellectual disabilities to lead fulfilling lives.

Explanation for why not the other options:

Option A: "Mental deficiency" is an outdated term that was previously used to describe intellectual disabilities. However, it is considered stigmatizing and outdated. The preferred term now is "intellectual disability," which focuses on the person's limitations in intellectual functioning rather than implying a deficiency or lack in mental capacity.

Option B: "Mental lack" is not a commonly used term to describe intellectual disabilities. It does not accurately or sensitively reflect the condition and is not a preferred term in contemporary discourse.

Option C: "Mentally challenged" is another outdated and stigmatizing term that was previously used to describe intellectual disabilities. However, like "mental deficiency," it is considered disrespectful and not reflective of the individual's capabilities. The preferred term now is "intellectual disability."

In summary, while the other options may have been used in the past, "intellectual disability" is the current and preferred term for describing this condition. It is more respectful, accurate, and inclusive of individuals with varying levels of intellectual functioning.
