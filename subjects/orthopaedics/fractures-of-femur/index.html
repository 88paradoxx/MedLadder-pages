Sample Fractures of Femur MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

Which of the following describes grade 2 fracture neck femur?

AIncomplete fracture, medial trabeculae intact
BComplete fracture with undisplaced neck
CComplete fracture with ischemic head
DModerate displacement of neck, vascularity damaged
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Complete fracture with undisplaced neck

Answer- B. Complete fracture with undisplaced neck Garden stage I: undisplaced incomplete, including valgus impacted fractures medial group of femoral neck trabeculae may demonstrate a greenstick fracture Garden stage II: undisplaced complete no disturbance of the medial trabeculae

Sample MCQ #2

Most common complication of fracture neck of femur

AMalunion
BAVN
CNonunion
DArthritis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. AVN

Answer- B. AVN AVN is the most common complication of femoral neck fracture. Non-union is the second most common complication of femoral neck fracture.

Sample MCQ #3

Which is NOT true about fractured neck of femurs?

AThe leg is shortened and externally rotated in most fractured neck of femurs
BThe main source of blood to the femoral head is via the vessels running within cancellous bone from the diaphysis
CSubcapital fracture cuts off all the blood to the head of the femur resulting in avascular necrosis
DThe blood supply to the head is increased by vessels in the capsular retinacula and it is this which prevents avascular necrosis of the head after an intertrochanteric fracture the ligamentum teres.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Subcapital fracture cuts off all the blood to the head of the femur resulting in avascular necrosis

The leg is shortened and externally rotated in most fractured neck of femurs. The main source of blood to the femoral head is via the vessels running within cancellous bone from the diaphysis. The blood supply to the head is increased by vessels in the capsular retinacula and it is this which prevents avascular necrosis of the head after an intertrochanteric fracture the ligamentum teres. Reference: Textbook of Gray's Anatomy for students, 3rd Edition, Lower Limb, Page number - 554

Sample MCQ #4

An 80-year-old male, immobilised following an intertrochanteric fracture, develops pain and swelling in the right calf. What is the most likely diagnosis?

AChronic osteomyelitis
BDeep vein thrombosis
CCompartment syndrome
DArterial claudication
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Deep vein thrombosis

Explanation: Immobilisation and calf swelling with pain suggest DVT , caused by thrombus formation in the deep veins. Risk factors include stasis, endothelial injury, and hypercoagulability (Virchow's triad) . Features: calf pain, tenderness, swelling, erythema, and warmth. Explanation of other options: Chronic osteomyelitis (Option A): Involves chronic bone infection with sequestrum and sinus, not acute calf swelling. Compartment syndrome (Option C): Severe pain, numbness, tense compartment, usually within 48 hrs of trauma/surgery. Pulses preserved but foot cold. Arterial claudication (Option D): Intermittent exertional calf pain from PAD; not acute, not linked to immobilisation.

Sample MCQ #5

Vascular sign of Narath is not seen in

ADevelopmental dysplasia of hip
BTom Smith arthitis
CPosterior dislocation of hip
DSubtrochanteric fracture of hip
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Subtrochanteric fracture of hip

Vascular sign of Narath: inability to feel femoral pulse against femoral head in the inguinal region. It is positive in all conditions where the femoral head is not present in its normal position. Eg. Traumatic hip dislocation, DDH, Tom Smith arthritis and Girdlestone arthroplasty Useful signs in orthopedics VIC: Volkmann&rsquo;s sign Piano key sign: Distal radio ulnar joint instability (e.g., Madelung deformity, Malunited colles fracture etc.) De Quervain&rsquo;s syonvitis: Finkelstein test Flexor tenosynovitis: Kanavel&rsquo;s signs AVN hip: Sectoral sign Iliopsoas tendonitis: Ludloff sign Reference- Fundamentals of Orthopedics Mohindra and Jain 1 st edition, page no 100

Sample MCQ #6

What is the treatment of Anderson Gustillo classification grade 3B?

AAntibiotics coated Intramedullary nailing
BIntramedullary wiring
CClose reduction
DK wire
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Antibiotics coated Intramedullary nailing

Type 3B fractures are usually treated with external fixation. However if the wound is not very contaminated and patient presents early to the orthopedic center, then antibiotic coated nailing can be performed. Reference- Anderson JT, Gustilo RB. Orthopedics clinics of North America, 1980; 11:56

Sample MCQ #7

A 12-years-old boy presents with a symmetric, expansile cystic lesion in the proximal humerus. All of the following can be done for his treatment except:

ACurettage
BIntralesional steroids
CIntralesional sclerosing agents
DRadiotherapy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Radiotherapy

Solitary bone cyst is the diagnosis of a 12 years old boy, who presents with a symmetric, expansile cystic lesion in the proximal humerus. Solitary Bone Cyst: Common in first two decades, primarily between 5-15 years More common in males MC site: Proximal humerus(50-60%) >Femur(25-30%) Cyst appears as centrally radioluscent lesions, on the metaphyseal side of the growth plate of a long bone Treatment: Curettage Methylprednisolone acetate injection into lesion Radiographic contrast injection into lesion Intralesional injection of sclerosing agents Reference: Chapman's Orthopedic Surgery, 3rd edition, Pg: 3392-3394.
