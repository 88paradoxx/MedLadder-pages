Sample Nerve Injuries MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

A 25-year-old football player sustains a blow to the arm during a match. He develops temporary weakness and tingling, but recovers completely within 6 weeks without surgical intervention. Which of the following best describes neuropraxia?

AIntact axon, damaged nerve sheath
BDamaged axon, intact nerve sheath
CDamaged axon and nerve sheath
DNone of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Intact axon, damaged nerve sheath

Explanation: Neuropraxia is the mildest form of peripheral nerve injury , as described in Seddon's classification . In this condition, the axon remains intact, but there is focal demyelination or damage to the myelin sheath due to compression or ischemia. Conduction block occurs across the site of injury, leading to weakness or sensory loss. Since the axon and endoneurial tubes are preserved, full recovery occurs within weeks to months without Wallerian degeneration. Explanation of other options: Damaged axon, intact nerve sheath (Option B): Describes axonotmesis , where axons are damaged but connective tissue sheaths remain intact. Damaged axon and nerve sheath (Option C): Describes neurotmesis , the most severe form of injury, with a poor prognosis.

Indications of external fixator Open fracture (grade 3b-3c- absolute indication, grade-2-3a-relative indication) Vascular injury Severe soft tissue injury Damage control orthopedics Nerve injury is not an indication of external fixation Reference- AO manual of principle of fracture management

Sample MCQ #4

Carpal Tunnel syndrome is seen in all except

AHypothyroidism
BColles fracture
CSmith's fracture
DPregnancy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Smith's fracture

CTS is associated with Colles fracture and not Smith's fracture. Most of the CTS is idiopathic, however there are several risk factors such as: Hypothyroidism Diabetes Acromegaly Rheumatoid arthritis Pregnancy Gout Alcoholism Amyloidosis Sarcoidosis Malunited Colles fracture Reference: J. Maheshwari's 5th Edition of Essential Orthopaedics page no. 114

Sample MCQ #5

Carpal tunnel syndrome is caused by all EXCEPT

AHypothyroidism
BTuberculosis
CPregnancy
DAcromegaly
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Tuberculosis

Carpal tunnel syndrome is produced by entrapment of median nerve beneath the flexor retinaculum. Associated conditions that can leads to carpal tunnel syndrome are: Idiopathic(most common) Pregnancy Endocrine disorders - Hypothyroidism, Diabetes mellitus, Myxedema, Acromegaly, Hyperparathyroidism Deposition disorders - Rheumatoid disorder, Gout, Rheumatic disorder, Amyloidosis, Sarcoidosis, Leukemia, Chronic renal failure Reference: Apley's system of orthopedics & fracture, 8th edition, Page: 247-248.

Sample MCQ #6

All of the following are predictive findings for correctly diagnosing carpal tunnel syndrome EXCEPT:

AAbnormal hand diagram
BAbnormal Semmes-Weinstein testing in wrist-neutral position
CPositive median nerve compression test(Durkan's sign)
DLoss of small digit adduction(Wartenberg sign)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Loss of small digit adduction(Wartenberg sign)

Wartenberg sign is persistent abduction and extension of the small digit when a patient is asked to adduct the digits and is seen in cubital tunnel syndrome, but not carpal tunnel syndrome. Reference: Maheshwari Essential Orthopedics, 5 th edition, Page no. 303.

Sample MCQ #7

Most commonly injured ligament is?

AACL
BMCL
CLCL
DALL
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. MCL

Most commonly injured knee ligament is the MCL followed by the ACL. ACL is the most commonly operated knee ligament as most of the MCL injuries are managed conservatively. Reference - Manish Kumar Varshney 1 st edition page no 878

Sample MCQ #8

Investigation of choice for ligament injuries of the knee is?

AX-Ray
BUSG
CMRI
DArthroscopy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. MRI

Investigation of choice for knee ligament injury is MRI as it will clearly show injuries of both the cruciates (intra articular) and the collaterals (extra articular) However, the investigation of choice for intra articular knee structures such as, ACL, PCL, menisci and cartilage is diagnostic arthroscopy. This is because, when we put an arthroscope in the knee joint, we can visualize the intra articular structures in 5 to 10 times magnification. Reference - Maheshwari, Essential Orthopedics 5 th edition page no 151.
