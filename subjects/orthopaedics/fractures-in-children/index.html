Sample MCQ #1

What is the most common complication after successful treatment of a distal femoral physeal fracture?

ANonunion
BCompartment syndrome
CKnee stiffness
DPremature physeal closure
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Premature physeal closure

Following are some facts about fractures in children Fractures in children heal faster. Fractures close to the joint heal faster. Sideways displacement will remodel. Angulation in the plane of the adjacent joint is acceptable. Rotational malalignment will never remodel. Physis subjected to compressive forces inhibits growth Reference- Maheshwari 5th /ep. PG NO 59

Sample MCQ #2

Radial head excision is avoided in all except

AEssex Lopresti fracture
BDisplaced fracture in children
CUndisplaced fractures in adults
DComminuted fractures in adults
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Comminuted fractures in adults

The treatment of radial head fractures depend on the amount of comminution and the age of the patient. Comminuted fractures in adults is treated by excising the broken fragments. However the radial head excision is contraindicated in Essex Lopresti fracture - Combination of radial head fracture with interosseous membrane rupture and DRUJ subluxation. Displaced fracture in children - Excision of radial head in children will cause cubitus valgus Undisplaced fractures in adults - These can be treated with POP cast. Reference: J. Maheshwari's 5th Edition of Essential Orthopaedics page no. 107

Which tumor is responsible for the most incidence of metastasis in a child?

AWilm's tumor
BNeuroblastoma
CNephroblastoma
DAcute Lymphocytic Leukemia

✓ Correct Answer: B. Neuroblastoma

Neuroblastoma is responsible for the most incidence of metastasis in children. Important points: Most common malignancy of bone: Metastasis MC primaries for bone mets: Breast > Prostate> Lung MC primaries for mets in children: Neuroblastoma MC bone involved: Spine> proximal femur MC part of spine involved: Thoracic > Lumbar Reference: Tachdjian's Pediatric Orthopaedics 5th edition page no. 89
