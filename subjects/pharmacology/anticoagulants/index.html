Sample Anticoagulants MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

Which enzyme is involved in the management of thrombotic events by converting plasminogen to plasmin?

AUrokinase
BLipase
CCreatine kinase
DThrombin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Urokinase

Explanation: Urokinase is an enzyme that promotes the breakdown of blood clots by converting plasminogen into plasmin, which then degrades fibrin, the main component of clots. This fibrinolytic action helps dissolve clots in blood vessels. It is used in Pulmonary embolism, Deep vein thrombosis (DVT) etc to dissolve clots.

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following drugs acts as a competitive inhibitor of the enzyme dihydrofolate reductase?

AMethotrexate
BAspirin
CPenicillin
DStatins
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Methotrexate

Explanation: Methotrexate is a structural analogue of folic acid and acts as a competitive inhibitor of dihydrofolate reductase, preventing the conversion of dihydrofolate to tetrahydrofolate, which is essential for DNA synthesis and cell division. This particularly affects rapidly dividing cells, making it useful in cancer and autoimmune diseases.

Sample MCQ #3

Which one of the following compounds is function as antagonist of Vitamin K, and commonly used to reduce blood clotting in patients with high risk of thromboembolism?

AWarfarin
BMenadione
CMenaquinone
DTocopherol
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Warfarin

Option A: Antagonists of vitamin K are used to reduce blood coagulation in patients at risk of thromboembolism; the most widely used is warfarin. Option B: Menadione and menadiol diacetate, synthetic compounds that can be metabolized to phylloquinone. It has vitamin K activity. Option C: Menaquinones, synthesized by intestinal bacteria, with differing lengths of side chain. It has also vitamin K like activity. Option D: Tocopherol have vitamin E activity. Reference: Harper's illustrated biochemistry 32 nd edition, CH 44 PN 532

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following drugs may inhibit the hepatic microsomal P450 responsible for warfarin metabolism?

AAmiodarone
BEthanol
CPhenobarbital
DProcainamide
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Amiodarone

Amiodarone is an important anti-arrhythmic drug and has a well-documented ability to inhibit the hepatic metabolism of many drugs. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung Fourteenth Edition Pg no.60

Sample MCQ #5

Rifampicin increase elimination of all of the following drugs except?

Amethadone
Bcyclosporine
Canticoagulants
Dcaptopril
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. captopril

Rifampin strongly induces most cytochrome P450 isoforms (CYP1A2, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, and 3A4), which increases the elimination of numerous other drugs including methadone, anticoagulants, cyclosporine, some anticonvulsants, protease inhibitors, some nonnucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitors or integrase strand transfer inhibitors, contraceptives, and a host of others. Coadministration of rifampin results in significantly lower serum levels of these drugs. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, 14th Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Page no. 845-846

Sample MCQ #6

Patients with a history of mitral stenosis and stroke presents with irregularly irregular heart beats. There is no clot in the left atrium on TEE. Patient is already on ACE inhibitors and statins. Which of the following drugs should be added for stroke prevention?

AWarfarin
BDabigatran
CAspirin with clopidogrel
DAspirin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Warfarin

Anticoagulants are of little value in thrombotic stroke. Neurological sequelae are similar whether they are given or not. Moreover, in the initial stages it is difficult to rule out haemorrhagic stroke. However, they may be given in cerebral embolism because showers of emboli are often recurrent in case of mitral stenosis/atrial fibrillation, and can be prevented by anticoagulants. Antiplatelet drugs are superior to anticoagulants in transient ischaemic attacks (TIAs).

Sample MCQ #7

A 78-year-old woman does not want oral anti-coagulation because she does not want to come to clinic for frequent blood tests. You suggest an oral alternative and start her on apixaban and counsel her on the importance of taking the medication each day, as suddenly stopping can lead to

AAnaphylaxis
BExcess bleeding
CStroke
DThrombocytopenia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Stroke

Due to the shorter half-life of the oral factor X and thrombin inhibitors, the anticoagulant status of the patient changes rapidly. Sudden cessation of short-acting oral anticoagulants can lead to stroke . Excess bleeding is associated with taking any of the anticoagulants not with stopping them. An increase in INR reflects increased anticoagulation by warfarin. Thrombo- cytopenia is a risk associated with heparin. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 834-33

Sample MCQ #8

Unfortunately, because of a calculation error, the heparin drip is running at 100 times the rate it should be running at for a 67 year old patient with DVT. Protamine sulfate is immediately given intravenously. This agent works by which of the following mechanisms of action?

AChemical antagonist
BFunctional agonist
CPartial agonist
DPartial antagonist
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Chemical antagonist

Chemical antagonist. A chemical antagonist prevents the actions of an agonist by modifying or sequestering the agonist so that it is incapable of binding to and activating its receptor. Protamine sulfate is a chemical antagonist for heparin. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung Fourteenth Edition Pg no. 22
