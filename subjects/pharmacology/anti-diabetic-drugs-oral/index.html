Sample MCQ #2

A 67-year-old diabetic male presents with dizziness due to hypoglycemia. He is on treatment with metformin with acarbose. Hypoglycemia should be treated with ?

AOral glucose
BOral sucrose
CGlucagon
DNo need to treat
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Oral glucose

Patients taking an &alpha;-glucosidase inhibitor who experience hypoglycemia should be treated with oral glucose (dextrose) and not sucrose, because the absorption of sucrose will be delayed. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 749

Sample MCQ #3

In response to drug X, pancreatic islet cells begin producing increasing amounts of secretory granules. Identify X.

AMetformin
BPioglitazone
CGlimepiride
DInsulin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Glimepiride

The secretory granules produced by the pancreatic islet cells in response to X, contain insulin and C-peptide, suggesting that the administered drug works by increasing endogenous insulin production. Glimepiride, a sulfonylurea, stimulates insulin secretion from pancreatic &beta;-cells. Their principle target is the ATP-sensitive K+ channels in the pancreatic &beta;-cell plasma membrane. Inhibition of K+ channels by sulfonylureas causes depolarization of the &beta;-cell membrane, leading to an influx of Ca2+, which stimulates exocytosis of insulin-containing secretory granules. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 759

Sample MCQ #4

A 50-year-old man with type 2 diabetes mellitus has a HbA1C of 8.5%. Consultant prescribes a drug that reversibly inhibits a membrane-bound enzyme that hydrolyses carbohydrate bonds. Which of the following drugs was most likely added to this patient's medication regimen?

ALinagliptin
BMetformin
CRosiglitazone
DMiglitol
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Miglitol

The drug inhibits alpha-glucosidase, Miglitol, prevents the hydrolysis of polysaccharides to monosaccharides. Since polysaccharides are not absorbed as easily as monosaccharides, alpha-glucosidase therapy results in a slower rise in postprandial blood glucose concentrations. Adverse effects of alpha-glucosidase inhibitors are caused by impaired absorption and include flatulence, diarrhoea, and early satiety. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung, 14th Edition, Page Number-761.

Sample MCQ #7

A 12-year-old boy with type 1 diabetes has severe hyperglycaemia, ketoacidosis, and a blood pH of 7.20. Which of the following agents should be administered to achieve rapid control of the severe ketoacidosis ?

AGlyburide
BInsulin glargine
CNPH insulin
DRegular insulin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Regular insulin

Oral antidiabetic agents are inappropriate in this patient because he has insulin-dependent diabetes. He needs a rapid-acting insulin preparation that can be given intravenously. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 754

Sample MCQ #8

A 25-year-old woman with type 1 diabetes wishes to control of her diabetes to improve her long-term prognosis. Which of the following regimens is most appropriate?

AMorning injections of mixed insulin lispro and insulin aspart
BEvening injections of mixed regular insulin and insulin glargine
CMorning and evening injections of regular insulin, supplemented by small amounts of NPH insulin at mealtimes
DMorning injections of insulin glargine, supplemented by small amounts of insulin lispro at mealtimes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Morning injections of insulin glargine, supplemented by small amounts of insulin lispro at mealtimes

Insulin regimens for tight control usually take the form of establishing a basal level of insulin with a small amount of a long-acting preparation (eg, insulin glargine) and supplementing the insulin levels, when called for by food intake, with short-acting insulin lispro. Less tight control may be achieved with 2 injections of intermediate-acting insulin per day. Because intake of glucose is mainly during the day, long- acting insulins are usually given in the morning, not at night. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no.754
