Sample MCQ #2

A 42-year-old woman with RA comes with 10-month history of joint pain and stiffness in her wrists and fingers. The symptoms are worse in the morning and improve with activity. She is started on first line DMARDs. All of the following are true about treatment except?

ATo minimize adverse effects, administer folic acid.
BPregnancy is contraindication with the treatment.
CAvoid administering NSAIDs on the same day as DMARDs.
DNeed yearly fundoscopy to rule out bull's eye Retinopathy.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Need yearly fundoscopy to rule out bull's eye Retinopathy.

This patient of RA is initiated with methotrexate PO 10-25 mg weekly. Option A- True. Folic acid 7&ndash;10 mg PO once weekly 24&ndash;48 hours after MTX administration Option B- True. Couple should plan for contraception while she's been on methotrexate treatment. If she plans to conceive, Sulfasalazine should be considered in patients with low disease activity. Option C- True. NSAIDs can inhibit the renal excretion of MTX, which may increase the risk for adverse effects. Option D- False. Given statement is true about CHQ therapy, should be consider in patients with low disease activity after remission. Not a first line of treatment. Reference- Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg no. 995-996

Sample MCQ #3

A 59-year-old woman with rheumatoid arthritis is planned for DEXA scan and result shows a T-score of -1.80, Which of the following drugs is most likely involved in the pathogenesis of this finding?

ASulfasalazine
BMedroxyprogesterone acetate
CPrednisone
DAdalimumab
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Prednisone

Patients with refractory rheumatoid arthritis are frequently prescribed glucocorticoids (e.g., prednisone), which induce bone resorption and increase the risk of osteopenia and osteoporosis. Glucocorticoid-induced bone resorption is primarily mediated via the RANK ligand signaling pathway, a cascade that upregulates osteoclast activity and induces apoptosis of osteoblasts. Chronic glucocorticoid use can also induce mild hypocalcemia, which leads to PTH-mediated bone resorption. In addition, decreased estrogen levels following menopause increase this patient's risk for osteoporosis and osteopenia. Bisphosphonates and denosumab (anti-RANK ligand antibody) are used to treat glucocorticoid-induced osteoporosis Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung, Pg no. 713

Sample MCQ #5

An 18-year-old was brought to the emergency department by her friends as she had turned blue. She was well otherwise. On examination she has central cyanosis; all other parameters are normal. What is the most likely cause?

ACarbon Monoxide Poisoning
BLead Poisoning
CDrinking water contaminated with nitrates
DAnorexia Nervosa
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Drinking water contaminated with nitrates

The question deals with cyanosis in an otherwise well patient. Cyanosis is seen in hypoxia. But the patient with hypoxia will be dyspnoeic. Here the patient is otherwise normal. Rare cause of cyanosis is due to abnormal hemoglobins like methemoglobin or Sulfhemoglobin. The causes of methemoglobinemia includes: Nitrates, nitric oxide or nitrobenzene Aniline dyes Propanil(a herbicide) Dapsone Pyridium So the accurate among the options are drinking water contaminated with nitrates. Lead poisoning will not lead to cyanosis but bluish lines in the gums. Carbon monoxide poisoning will cause cherry red discolouration. Anorexia nervosa is a disease of distorted perception of body image, where the patient restricts food intake and will be malnourished. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal medicine, 21st edition Ch:459 Pg 3593

Sample MCQ #6

A 17-year-old female presents after taking an overdose of her grandmother's medication. Investigations revealed: Serum potassium 6 mmol/L (3.5-4.9) Which one of the following drugs is the most likely cause of this abnormality?

AAspirin
BDigoxin
CSalbutamol
DPropranolol
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Propranolol

Beta blockers , particularly propranolol, in overdose can cause hyperkalaemia by transmembrane shifts. It is useful to think of the opposite - beta-agonists such as salbutamol are far more widely known to cause hypokalaemia. Overdose of digoxin therapy most commonly manifests as rhythm disturbance, and may be exacerbated by hyper- or hypokalaemia. Salicylate overdose causes metabolic acidosis and respiratory alkalosis. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Principles, 20 th edition, pg:423

Sample MCQ #7

A 20 year old male has taken a drug overdose of multiple drugs. Which one of the following can be effectively removed by haemodialysis?

AAmiodarone
BDigoxin
CPhenytoin
DLithium
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Lithium

Drugs with a large volume of distribution are poorly dialysed. Haemodialysis is effective in lithium poisoning. LIST OF DIALYZABLE TOXINS Dialysis should be considered in cases of severe poisoning due to: Carbamazepine Ethylene glycol Isopropyl alcohol Lithium Methanol Theophylline Salicylates Valproate. Hemodialysis corrects the acid base balance as well as removal of toxins compared to hemoperfusion. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21st Ed, Ch: 459, Pg: 3589.

Sample MCQ #8

A 38 year old man with a history of depression is brought to the accident and emergency department after being found unconscious in a garage. A friend said that he complained of a headache, then had nausea and vomiting. He then became unarousable. Oxygen saturations are 95% on air and breath sounds are clear on auscultation. What is the likely diagnosis?

APulmonary eosinophilia
BPulmonary embolism
CPneumocystis pneumonia
DCarbon monoxide poisoning
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Carbon monoxide poisoning

The patient has a clear lung field with normal oxygen saturation which rules out primary lung of vascular pathologies like pneumonitis, pulmonary embolism and eosinophilia. Carbon monoxide poisoning is produced by the incomplete combustion of carbon containing fuels such as gas, coal, oil, wood and coke. CO increases the affinity of O 2 to Haemoglobin which results in cellular hypoxia in spite of a normal saturation. The O2 Hb dissociation curve shifts to the left. Headache is the most common symptom (90%) followed by nausea & vomiting, vertigo, alteration in consciousness and weakness. The cherry red skin colour occurs when COHb concentration exceeds 20% but it is rarely seen in life. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal medicine, 21st edition Ch:459 Pg 3593
