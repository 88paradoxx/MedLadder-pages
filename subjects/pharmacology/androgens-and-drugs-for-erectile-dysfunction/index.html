Sample MCQ #1

A 50-year-old man is presents with dizziness and confusion. He had an episode of chest pain and took several tablets of nitroglycerin. His current medications include tadalafil for erectile dysfunction. His blood pressure is 50/20 mmHg and his heart rate is 120 beats/min. Which of the following cellular changes is most likely responsible for this patient's symptoms?

AGs protein phosphorylation
BCyclic GMP accumulation
CTyrosine kinase overactivity
DEnhanced phospholipid metabolism
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Cyclic GMP accumulation

The interaction between nitrates and phosphodiesterase inhibitors used in erectile dysfunction (tadalafil, sildenafil, vardenafil) is well known and very important to understand. Nitrates are converted to nitric oxide by vascular smooth muscle cells, and nitric oxide causes increased intracellular CGMP as a second-messenger. Increased GMP concentration leads to vascular smooth muscle relaxation. Additionally, GMP is metabolized within the cells by phosphodiesterase, and phosphodiesterase inhibitors will lead to increased intracellular GMP. GMP accumulation in vascular smooth muscle cells due to both enhanced synthesis (nitrates) and inhibited degradation (PDE inhibitors) is responsible for profound hypotension due to extreme vasodilatation when these drugs are used together. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology, Fourteenth Edition By Bertram G. Katzung Pg. no. 194

Sample MCQ #2

A 78-year-old man presents with the complaint of difficulty urinating. He has difficulty starting his stream and feels his bladder is still full after he urinates. He has to get up about four times a night to urinate. The physician starts him on finasteride, which non-competitively inhibits 5 alpha-reductase. What effect does a noncompetitive inhibitor have on enzyme kinetics?

ADecreases Km
BDecreases Vmax
CIncreases Km
DIncreases Vmax
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Decreases Vmax

Decreases Vmax. Noncompetitive inhibitor decrease the Vmax. Noncompetitive inhibitors block enzyme activity with or without substrates bound, therefore the affinity is unaffected. The Vmax decreases because when the inhibitor is bound, it decreases the amount of active sites available for the substrate. Reference: Basic & Clinical Pharmacology by Bertram G. Katzung Fourteenth Edition Pg no. 22

Sample MCQ #3

A patient with pre-existing liver disease consumed a drug and developed worsening of liver dysfunction. The metabolite of the drug responsible is N-acetyl-para-amino-benzo-quinone-imine(NAPQI). The likely implicated drug in this case is

AParacetamol
BValproate
CAmiodarone
DLorazepam
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Paracetamol

This is a case of Paracetamol toxicity. Mechanism of toxicity of Paracetamol N-acetyl-p-benzoquinoneimine (NABQI) is a highly reactive arylating minor metabolite of paracetamol which is detoxified by conjugation with glutathione. When a very large dose of paracetamol is taken, glucuronidation capacity is saturated, more of the minor metabolite is formed-hepatic glutathione is depleted and this metabolite binds covalently to proteins in liver cells (and renal tubules) causing necrosis. Antidote: N-acetylcysteine
