Sample MCQ #1

In multiple sclerosis slow conduction of sensory and motor pathway is due to

ADefects in node of ranvier
BLoss of myelin sheath
CLeaking of sodium channel
DLeaking potassium channel
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Loss of myelin sheath

Autoimmune inflammation and demyelination of CNS (brain and spinal cord) with subsequent axonal damage. Most often affects females aged 20-40 Symptoms may exacerbate with increased body temperature (eg, hot bath, exercise). Relapsing and remitting is most common clinical course. &uarr;IgG level and myelin basic protein in CSF. Oligoclonal bands aid in diagnosis. MRI is gold standard. Periventricular plaques A (areas of oligodendrocyte loss and reactive gliosis). Multiple white matter lesions disseminated in space and time.

patient underwent adrenalectomy and developed pigmentation and fatigue diagnosis?

AConn's syndrome
BSheehan syndrome
CAddison's disease
DMetabolic alkalosis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Addison's disease

Removal of adrenal gland leads to Addison's disease. Deficiency of mainly 3 hormones Due to mineralocorticoid deficiency: Hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypotension, small heart, nausea, vomiting Due to glucocorticoids deficiency: Hypoglycemia, fatigue, anorexia, skin pigmentation, weight loss, myalgia Due to adrenal androgen deficiency: Lack of energy, dry & itchy skin, loss of libido in women & loss of axillary & pubic hairs in women.

Sample MCQ #3

With regard to a patient with a neuromuscular disorder on the critical care unit which is true:

APotassium-sparing diuretics should be avoided in patients with hypokalemic periodic paralysis.
BRocuronium use should be avoided in patients with myasthenia gravis
CPatients with motor neurone disease typically require double the standard dose of suxamethonium to provide optimum intubating conditions.
DLocal anesthesia can exacerbate symptoms of multiple sclerosis
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Local anesthesia can exacerbate symptoms of multiple sclerosis

The use of suxamethonium is challenging in patients with neuromuscular disorders. Key points to remember include relative resistance to depolarisation in myasthenia gravis, often requiring an increased dosage, and the development of extrajunctional receptors in many of the myotonias, leading to extensive depolarisation and potentially fatal hyperkalemia with the use of depolarising neuromuscular blockade. In addition, there are disease-specific aspects of nondepolarizing neuromuscular blockade that should be noted,including the relative sensitivity in conditions such as Guillain-Barr&eacute; syndrome. Hence dose reduction to 10-20% should be considered in addition to ongoing monitoring of blockade. Reference: Harrison 20 th 3188-3200

Sample MCQ #4

Chronic subdural haematoma in a 77-year-old man is not associated with the presence of which of the following?

ABilateral papilloedema
BHemiparesis
CFluctuating level of consciousness
DInternuclear ophthalmoplegia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Internuclear ophthalmoplegia

Chronic subdural haematoma is classically associated with fluctuating conscious level and cognitive function. Bilateral papilloedema may occur with raised intracranial pressure. Bilateral internuclear ophthalmoplegia is associated with multiple sclerosis and unilateral lesions of medial longitudinal fasciculus may occur with small brain stem infarcts. Unequal pupils are associated with rapid transtentorial coning in extradural haemorrhage leading to ipsilateral dilated pupil followed by bilateral fixed dilated pupils. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 443, Pg: 3457

Sample MCQ #5

A 47 year old man was referred for assessment of unsteady gait, which has been present for 6 months. He has lost a stone in weight over the several months. An MRI of the brain shows multiple high signal areas. A biopsy was taken of one of the lesions. The report shows perivascular infiltrates of lymphocytes affecting white and gray matter. There was minimal myelin loss. CSF examination shows raised white cell count, and oligoclonal bands were not raised. What is the most likely diagnosis?

ACreutzfeldt Jakob disease
BProgressive multifocal leucoencephalopathy
CCNS lymphoma
DGlioma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. CNS lymphoma

The white matter lesions suggest either multiple sclerosis or lymphoma. Infiltration with lymphocytes can be seen in Multiple Sclerosis too. In around 10% of patients with MS, OCB can be absent. However, the presence of constitutional symptoms makes the possibility of CNS lymphoma more likely. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine. 21 st Ed, Ch:90 Pg: 705

Sample MCQ #7

A 59-year-old woman presents with poly-symptomatology. On examination she has prominent fasciculations. From which of the following conditions is she unlikely to suffer?

ACervical spondylosis
BMotor neurone disease
CMultiple sclerosis
DSpinal muscular atrophy
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Multiple sclerosis

Fasciculation suggest lower motor neurone disease . It is seen usually in diseases affecting the anterior horn cells of spinal cord. It can also be seen in diseases of the nerve roots and peripheral nerves. However, it is absent in diseases of the muscles or neuromuscular junction. Cervical spondylosis is a disease which affects the spinal cord as well as the nerve roots. So it can cause fasciculations. Motor neurone disease is the prototype illness that presents with fasciculations. Spinal muscular atrophy is an LMN type of MND and hence can cause fascicuations. Multiple Sclerosis is a disease causing demyelination of the white matter of the central nervous system and hence presents with predominant UMN symptoms and can have sensory symptoms as well. Hence MS is the less likely cause. Reference: Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 447, Pg: 3411-3415

Sample MCQ #8

A 35 year old female comes to you with paraplegia of one day duration, with a sensory level at T10, she has bladder involvement and is catheterized. She gives a history of loss of vision in her left eye, one year back, with residual defective vision. She was not on any follow up. Her MRI brain and spinal cord shows multiple T2 hyperintense lesions. What will be the treatment of this patient?

AIntravenous Immunoglobulins
BIV Methyl Prednisolone 1g/day for 3-5 days
CPlasma exchange
DRituximab
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. IV Methyl Prednisolone 1g/day for 3-5 days

This patient who has a background history of an defective vision, now presents with symptoms suggestive of a spinal cord transection and MRI showing T2 hyperintense lesions. This is typical of Multiple Sclerosis. The acute episodes of Multiple Sclerosis is treated with steroids. Methylprednisolone 500-1000mg for 3-5 days, followed by a taper with oral steroids 60-80mg/day for two weeks. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 444, Pg: 3468
