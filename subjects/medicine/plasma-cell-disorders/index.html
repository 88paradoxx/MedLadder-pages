Sample MCQ #3

A 71 year old patient has a set of investigations due to lethargy. She complains of polyuria and generalised back pains. Upon investigation, she has the following results: Hb 8 g/dl MCV 100 fl Platelets 190 x 10^9/L total protein 9 g/dl Albumin 3.4 (3.7-4.9) g/dl calcium 12 mg/dl phosphate 1.8 mg/dl What is the appropriate next investigation?

AComplete liver function tests
BUrinary albumin
CSerum Protein electrophoresis
D24 hour urine protein
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Serum Protein electrophoresis

The patient in this question has bone pains, hypercalcemia with impaired renal function and AG reversal, all pointing to the diagnosis of Myeloma. Hence the next best step would be a serum protein electrophoresis. 24 hour urine albumin or protein and LFT is of little use in this clinical scenario. Reference : Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 111 Pg : 865-867

Sample MCQ #4

Which of the following diseases presents with Thrombotic microangiopathy without Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia?

AMalignant Hypertension.
BPOEMS Syndrome
CHemolytic Uremic syndrome
DHELLP syndrome.
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. POEMS Syndrome

Thrombotic microangiopathy(TMA) is a pathological lesion characterized by the presence of endothelial injury in the terminal arteriole and capillaries of organs, causing hyaline platelet thrombi, resulting in partial or complete occlusion of vessels. In kidneys it leads to a membranoproliferative pattern in glomerulus. Usually TMA is associated with Micro angiopathic hemolytic anemia(MAHA) characterized by schistocytes and thrombocytopenia. Typical examples of TMA include: Hemolytic uremic syndrome Thrombotic Thrombocytopenic Purpura Malignant hypertension Scleroderma renal crisis APLa syndrome HELLP syndrome HIV infection Radiation nephropathy TMA without MAHA is seen in POEMS Syndrome TMA associated with Myeloproliferative neoplasias Reference: Harrison's Principles of internal medicine, 21 st ed, Ch: 217, Pg: 2364.

✓ Correct Answer: A. Amyloidosis

Macroglossia, Raccoon eyes, hepatomegaly, anemia are features of amyloidosis Crohn's disease can affect any part of the gastrointestinal tract from the mouth to the anus. Aphthous ulceration is common in active disease. Aphthous ulcers and glossitis are both features of celiac disease. Sample MCQ #9 · Image-based

A 56-year-old patient presents with fatigue, tingling sensation in the arms, weight loss, and a history of polyuria. A bone marrow biopsy was performed, and the findings are shown below. What is the most probable diagnosis?

ASubacute combined degeneration due to cobalamin deficiency
BMultiple myeloma
CSubacute degeneration due to diabetes
DMegaloblastic anemia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Multiple myeloma

Explanation of answer:

The presence of plasmacytosis in the bone marrow, along with symptoms like polyuria (from hypercalcemia), neuropathy, fatigue, and weight loss, strongly indicates multiple myeloma.

Bone marrow biopsy findings: Numerous plasma cells with eccentric nuclei, perinuclear clearing (hof), and basophilic cytoplasm consistent with plasmacytosis.

  • Multiple myeloma (MM) is a plasma cell malignancy characterized by monoclonal proliferation of abnormal plasma cells in the bone marrow, leading to overproduction of abnormal immunoglobulin or light chains (M protein).
  • Key Clinical Clues in This Case:
    • Fatigue and weight loss → Constitutional symptoms
    • Tingling in arms → Neuropathy (common due to paraproteinemia or amyloidosis)
    • Polyuria → Due to hypercalcemia (part of CRAB criteria)
    • Bone marrow biopsy → Shows >10% abnormal plasma cells (as seen in the image)
  • Diagnostic Features of MM:
    • Bone marrow plasmacytosis >10% (as seen on histology)
    • CRAB Criteria:
      • C: Hypercalcemia
      • R: Renal dysfunction
      • A: Anemia
      • B: Bone lesions (lytic lesions on imaging)
    • Serum protein electrophoresis (SPEP): M-spike
    • Urine: Bence Jones protein
    • Peripheral smear: Rouleaux formation
    • Flow cytometry: CD138+ plasma cells
    • FISH: High-risk cytogenetics (e.g., 17p deletion)
    • Imaging:
      • Chest and bone radiographs: Lytic lesions/diffuse osteopenia
      • MRI: Extent of bone marrow infiltration and cord or root compression in patients with pain syndrome
      • PET/CT: Assess bone damage, extramedullary sites of the disease

Explanation of other options:

Subacute combined degeneration due to cobalamin deficiency (Option A): Presents with neurological symptoms like paresthesia and loss of proprioception but does not cause hypercalcemia or plasma cell proliferation in bone marrow.

Subacute degeneration due to diabetes (Option C): Refers to diabetic neuropathy, which causes tingling and numbness but no marrow abnormalities or CRAB features.

Megaloblastic anemia (Option D): Results from B12 or folate deficiency, causes macrocytic anemia and neurological symptoms, but not hypercalcemia or increased plasma cells on biopsy.
