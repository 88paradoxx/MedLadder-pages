Sample MCQ #1

Most common cause of cancer death?

ALung cancer
BProstate cancer
CHCC
DGallbladder cancer
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Lung cancer

Lung cancer is one of the most common cancers throughout the world. In the UK, there are approximately 45 000 new cases a year. From the time of diagnosis, 60% of patients are dead within 1 year and only 15% survive 5 years, making lung cancer the most common cause of cancer death. Survival is dependent on the stage that the patient presents with lung cancer. Reference: Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery 28th Edition, Chapter 59, Cardiac Surgery, Pg: 984.

Sample MCQ #2

Which of the following does not cause bladder outlet obstruction?

AVesico-ureteral reflux
BUrethral strictures
CBladder neck stenosis
DBPH
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Vesico-ureteral reflux

Vesico-ureteral reflux (VUR) does not cause bladder outlet obstruction (BOO). VUR is the retrograde flow of urine from the bladder to the ureter. Urodynamically proven BOO may result from- BPH - Benign prostatic hyperplasia Bladder neck stenosis Bladder neck hypertrophy Prostate cancer Urethral strictures Functional obstruction due to neuropathic conditions Urodynamically proven BOO may result from: BPH; bladder neck stenosis; bladder neck dyssynergia or functional bladder neck obstruction; bladder neck hypertrophy; prostate cancer; urethral stricture; functional obstruction due to neuropathic conditions. Reference : Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery 28 th Edition, Chapter 84, The prostate and seminal vesicles, Pg: 1524.

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following iodine isotopes is used in the management of prostate cancer?

AI-131
BI-125
CI-123
DI-121
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. I-125

brachytherapy for prostate cancer is passed via trans- perineal needles Radio-isotopes commonly used are- iodine-125 and palladium-103 Theses isotopes deliver an intense, confined radiation dose. Iodine-125 ( 125 I) is a radioisotope of iodine which has uses in biological assays, nuclear medicine imaging and in radiation therapy as brachytherapy to treat a number of conditions, including prostate cancer, uveal melanomas, and brain tumors. It is the second longest-lived radioisotope of iodine, after iodine-129. Permanent seed implants are used as monotherapy in cases of prostate tumors with a high probability of being limited to the prostate, without any need to combine it with external radiotherapy or hormone therapy https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11103823/

Sample MCQ #4

In a patient with a carcinoma prostate with high Gleason score, bone scan was done. Bone scan shows increased uptake with bones and reduced in kidney and soft tissues. This is reported as super scan because of:

AIncreased bone uptake
BIncreased uptake because of osteoblastic lesions
CDecreased uptake in kidney
DIncreased because carcinoma prostate
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Increased uptake because of osteoblastic lesions

Once the diagnosis has been established, if metastatic spread is suspected (on the basis of a high PSA [>10 ng/mL], locally advanced disease or presence of Gleason 7 or higher) a bone scan should be carried out. This is because prostate cancer is predominantly osteoblastic lesions which shows increased uptake in bone scans.

Sample MCQ #5

Which of the following is not true regarding radical prostatectomy for prostate cancer?

ASuitable for localised disease
BShould be carried out only in men with a life expectancy of < 10 yrs
CExclusion of metastases would be required before surgery
DResults in a high incidence of impotence
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Should be carried out only in men with a life expectancy of < 10 yrs

Radical prostatectomy is suitable for- Localised disease Carried out only in men with a life expectancy of >10 years . Exclusion of metastases would require a negative bone scan and MRI of the pelvis. Procedure - Removal of the prostate down to the distal urethral sphincter , in addition to the seminal vesicles . The bladder neck is reconstituted and anastomosed to the urethra. It results in a high incidence of impotence , but a low incidence of severe stress incontinence ( Reference : Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery 28 th Edition, Chapter 84, The prostate and seminal vesicles, Pg: 1536.

Sample MCQ #6 · Image-based

A 77 years old man with biopsy proven prostate cancer and serum PSA of 67 presents with following bone scan. What is the likely stage?

ALimited to prostate
BLocally advanced
CMetastatic
DCannot be commented upon
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Metastatic

Bone scan shows multiple hot-spots suggestive of metastatic disease in a man with prostate cancer.

Once the diagnosis has been established, if metastatic spread is suspected (on the basis of a high PSA [>10 ng/mL], locally advanced disease or presence of Gleason 7 or higher) a bone scan should be carried out. If, however, the PSA is <10 ng/mL, then a bone scan would be performed only on clinical indications. The bone scan is performed by the injection of technetium-99m, which is then monitored using a gamma camera. It is more sensitive in the diagnosis of metastases (Figure 84.20) than a skeletal survey, but false positives occur in areas of arthritis, osteomyelitis or a healing fracture.

Reference: Bailey & Love's short practice of surgery 28th Edition, Chapter 84, The prostate and seminal vesicles, Pg: 1535.
