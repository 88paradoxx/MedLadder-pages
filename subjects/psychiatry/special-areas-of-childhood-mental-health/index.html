Sample MCQ #1

A 25 year old female is presented to OPD with complaints of agitation, muttering to herself and sleep disturbances. She claims that her husband has stolen the baby from her womb. She has delivered a healthy child three weeks back, however has not taking care of the baby. What is the most probable diagnosis?

APostpartum Blues
BPostpartum Depression
CPostpartum Psychosis
DMania
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Postpartum Psychosis

Nihilistic delusion is associated postpartum and duration is around 3 weeks. Diagnosis is Postpartum psychosis. Reference: Kaplan and Saddocks Synopsis of Psychiaitry, 11th Edition, Chapter 8, Page No.: 357

Sample MCQ #2

Which one of the following is NOT a poor prognostic factor in OCD?

AMale gender
BPoor insight
CEarly onset
DFamily history of OCD
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Family history of OCD

It is observed that about 20&ndash;30% of those with OCD show a significant improvement, 40&ndash;50% have a moderate improvement, while the rest have a chronic or worsening course. Poor prognostic factors include yielding to compulsions, a childhood onset , male , bizarre content of compulsions, need for hospitalization , a coexisting major depressive disorder , the presence of overvalued ideas , and the presence of personality disorder (especially schizotypal). (Option A, B & C) A good prognosis is indicated by good premorbid adjustment, the presence of a precipitating event, and episodic nature. The content or theme of obsessions and family history do not relate to the prognosis . Reference: Synopsis of Psychiatry, Eleventh edition, Chapter 10, page no. 420

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following is/are leading cause of death among 15-24 years old?

AUnintentional injury
BHomicide
CCancer
DAll of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. All of the above

Unintentional injury and homicide were the leading causes of death among residents ages 15-24, each accounting for 34.2% and 32.9%, respectively, of all deaths. These were followed by suicide (9.9%) and cancer (5.8%). Accidents (unintentional injuries), homicide, suicide, cancer, and heart disease make up the five leading causes of death for teenagers Reference : https://cchealth.org/health-data/hospital-council/2010/pdf/11_lcd_by_age.pdf

Sample MCQ #4

The Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP) does not include which of the following disorders?

AOCD
BDepression
CSchizophrenia
DChildhood mental disorder
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. OCD

Explanation: The Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP) by the World Health Organization (WHO) aims to improve access to mental health care in low and middle-income countries. It focuses on priority conditions including: Depression (Option B) Schizophrenia (Option C) Bipolar disorder Epilepsy Dementia Self-harm Alcohol and substance use disorders Child and adolescent mental disorders (Option D) OCD is not listed as a core mhGAP target condition. References: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK310854/

Sample MCQ #5

Who has the right to make complaints about deficiencies in the provision of mental health services?

AOnly healthcare professionals can file complaints
BThe person with a mental illness or their nominated representative has the right to make complaints
CThe concerned Board can file complaints on behalf of the person with a mental illness
DOnly legal guardians can make complaints
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. The person with a mental illness or their nominated representative has the right to make complaints

Explanation: Under the Mental Healthcare Act (MHCA) 2017, a person with a mental illness (PMI) has the right to complain about any deficiencies in the provision of mental health services. If the person is unable to do so, their nominated representative (NR) can file a complaint on their behalf. Reference: The Mental Healthcare Act, 2017

Sample MCQ #6

What is the maximum duration of emergency treatment a person with mental illness can receive under the Mental Health Care Act, 2017?

A24 hours
B48 hours
C72 hours
D7 days
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 72 hours

Explanation: As per section 94 of the Mental Healthcare Act (MHCA) 2017, emergency treatment can be provided for up to 72 hours, with the consent of the nominated representative (if available). Any medical practitioner can provide emergency treatment as per the provisions of this section of the act. Reference: The Mental Healthcare Act, 2017

Sample MCQ #8

Which is the last stage of psychosocial development as described by Erikson?

AAutonomy versus shame and doubt
BGenerativity versus stagnation
CIndustry versus inferiority
DIntegrity versus despair
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Integrity versus despair

Erikson, a student of Freud, accepted the basic theory of early psychosocial development but proposed that development continues across the lifespan, particularly in terms of the developing relationship between the individual and the social system within which they live. He underlined the social context for this final stage of growth. In Childhood and Society, he wrote, 'the style of integrity developed by his culture or civilisation thus becomes the "patrimony" of his soul In such final consolidation, death loses its sting When the attempt to attain integrity has failed, the individual may become deeply disgusted with external world and contemptuous of persons as well institutions Reference: Synopsis of Psychiatry, eleventh edition, chapter 4, page no. 169

Sample MCQ #9 · Image-based

Conservation of substance comes in which cognitive developmental stage?

ASensorimotor stage
BPreoperational thought stage
CConcrete operations
DFormal operations
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Concrete operations

Explanation:

Conservation is the ability to understand that objects maintain certain characteristics despite changes in shape. For instance, when a ball of clay is rolled into a long sausage shape, children recognize that both forms contain the same amount of clay. Inability to conserve, typical of the preoperational stage, is seen when a child thinks the sausage has more clay because it is longer.

Reversibility refers to the understanding that one thing can change into another and back again, such as ice transforming into water.

Explanation for the other options:

A. Sensorimotor stage:

  • Piaget's sensorimotor stage describes how infants learn through sensory observation and motor control, divided into six substages. Infants blend biology and experience to form learned behaviours, such as adapting their sucking reflex to locate a nipple. They develop schemas-basic concepts formed through interaction.
  • A critical achievement is object permanence, the understanding that objects exist independently of perception, allowing infants to search for dropped items. Around 18 months, they begin to develop symbolization, using mental images and words to represent objects. This transition marks the shift from the sensorimotor stage to the preoperational stage of development.

B. Preoperational thought stage:

  • During the Preoperational stage, children use symbols and language but think intuitively, lacking logical reasoning. They name objects but struggle with categories and cause and effect; for instance, they might believe a broken glass was meant to break. They perceive the same object in different contexts as separate items and define things by their functions, like calling a bike "to ride" and a hole "to dig."
  • In the preoperational stage, children struggle with moral dilemmas and often judge guilt based on quantity rather than intent, believing that more broken dishes mean more guilt. They hold a sense of immanent justice, thinking punishment for bad deeds is inevitable.
  • Egocentric in their thinking, children cannot understand others' perspectives and struggle to modify their behaviour for others. They also engage in magical thinking, believing events that occur together cause one another (e.g., thunder causes lightning) and exhibit animistic thinking, attributing life-like qualities to objects and events.

D. Formal Operations:

  • The formal operations stage involves highly logical, systematic, and abstract thinking. Adolescents can reason deductively, grasp probabilities, and explore complex issues like philosophy and ethics. They develop hypothetico-deductive thinking, allowing them to form and test hypotheses.
  • While they reflect on their own and others' thoughts, adolescents may show self-consciousness and occasionally revert to egocentric thinking. Not all adolescents enter this stage simultaneously or to the same degree; some may remain in the concrete operational stage throughout their lives.

Key Takeaways:

Conservation is the ability to understand that objects maintain certain characteristics despite changes in shape. This ability, along with reversibility and systematic reasoning, develops in the Concrete Operations stage (age 7-11).
