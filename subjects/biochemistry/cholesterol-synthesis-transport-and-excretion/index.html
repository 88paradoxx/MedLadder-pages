Sample MCQ #3

All of the following nutrients are considered lipotropic factors that help prevent fatty liver EXCEPT:

ACholine
BLecithine
CArginine
DMethionine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Arginine

Explanation: Lipotropic factors are substances that promote the export of fat from the liver, preventing hepatic steatosis (fatty liver). Choline , lecithin (which contains choline), and methionine (which can be converted to cysteine and is a methyl donor) are classic lipotropic agents, arginine is not.( Option A,B and D ruled out) Arginine is primarily involved in the urea cycle, nitric oxide synthesis, and other metabolic pathways unrelated to hepatic lipid transport. Key Takeaways: Lipotropic factors prevent fatty liver (e.g., choline, methionine, betaine, inositol). Arginine is not a lipotropic factor.

Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Arginine metabolism

Sample MCQ #8

A diabetic patient presents with elevated VLDL levels due to which combination of the following enzyme activities?

AHigh HSL, low LPL
BHigh LPL, low HSL
CHigh HSL, High LPL
DLow LPL, Low HSL
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. High HSL, low LPL

Explanation: Hormone-Sensitive Lipase (HSL): HSL breaks down stored triglycerides in adipose tissue into free fatty acids and glycerol. In diabetes, HSL activity is increased , releasing more free fatty acids into circulation, which the liver uses to synthesize VLDL (Very Low-Density Lipoprotein). Lipoprotein Lipase (LPL): LPL hydrolyzes triglycerides in VLDL and chylomicrons into free fatty acids for tissue uptake. In diabetes, LPL activity is reduced , impairing the clearance of VLDL from the bloodstream. Combined Effect: High HSL &rarr; more substrate for hepatic VLDL production. Low LPL &rarr; reduced VLDL clearance. Result: elevated VLDL levels in diabetic patients, contributing to hypertriglyceridemia. Hence options B,C, and D are incorrect
