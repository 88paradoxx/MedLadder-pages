Sample Breathing Systems MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

During spontaneous breathing, volatile anesthetics

AIncrease tidal volume (Vt) and decrease respiratory rate
BIncrease Vt and increase respiratory rate
CDecrease Vt and decrease respiratory rate
DDecrease Vt and increase respiratory rate
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Decrease Vt and increase respiratory rate

At concentrations of 1 MAC or less, volatile anesthetics, as well as the inhaled anesthetic N 2 O, will produce dose-dependent increases in the respiratory rate in spontaneously breathing patients. This trend continues at concentrations greater than 1 MAC for all of the inhaled anesthetics except isoflurane. With the exception of N 2 O, the evidence suggests that this effect is caused by direct activation of the respiratory center in the CNS rather than by stimulation of pulmonary stretch receptors. Additionally, volatile anesthetics decrease Vt and significantly alter the breathing pattern from the normal awake pattern of intermittent deep breaths separated by varying time intervals to one of rapid, shallow, regular, and rhythmic breathing. Reference: Miller&rsquo;s Anesthesia, ed 8, pp 691-692.

Sample MCQ #2

Anaesthetic breathing systems can be open or closed. To be a closed circuit, which of the following is a pre requisite

ASoda lime
BPressure limiting valve
CSpontaneous patient
DControlled ventilation
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Soda lime

The components of a circle system include: a CO 2 absorber containing CO 2 absorbent; a fresh gas inlet; an inspiratory unidirectional valve and inspiratory breathing tube; a Y-connector; an expiratory unidirectional valve and expiratory breathing tube; an APL valve; and a reservoir. Reference: Morgan, edition 4, page no 40

Sample MCQ #3

Compared to the Mapleson A system, the circle system

AIs less bulky
BHas a decreased risk of disconnection
CHas decreased resistance to patient breathing
DBetter conserves humidity
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Better conserves humidity

Advantages of the circle system include economy (low fresh gas flow rates, decreased use of volatile agents), conservation of heat and humidity, and decreased operating-room pollution. Disadvantages of circle system include greater size, decreased portability, increased risk of disconnection, and increased resistance to patient breathing. Reference: Lippincott's ANESTHESIA REVIEW: 1,001 QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS Paul Sikka, MD, PhD

Sample MCQ #4

What is the typical pressure setting for the valve in a Mapleson circuit?

A10-20 mmHg
B30-40 mmHg
C10-20 cm H 2 O
D30-40 cm H 2 O
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. 30-40 cm H 2 O

Explanation: The adjustable pressure-limiting (APL) valve in a Mapleson breathing circuit is typically set at 30-40 cm H 2 O to allow appropriate control of airway pressure during manual ventilation. The Mapleson circuit is a semi-open anesthetic breathing system used in controlled and spontaneous ventilation. It consists of: Fresh gas flow inlet Corrugated tubing Reservoir bag Adjustable Pressure-Limiting (APL) valve The APL valve is crucial for: Controlling the maximum pressure delivered during manual ventilation Preventing barotrauma by allowing excess gas to escape once a set pressure is reached The typical pressure setting for the APL valve is 30-40 cm H₂O, which ensures: Adequate positive pressure during assisted breaths Protection against excessive airway pressure

Sample MCQ #5

Since fresh gas flow equal to minute ventilation is sufficient to prevent rebreathing, which of the following Mapleson circuit breathing/ventilation systems is the most efficient for spontaneous ventilation of the patient?

AMapleson A
BMapleson B
CMapleson C
DMapleson D
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Mapleson A

During spontaneous ventilation/breathing of the patient, the Mapleson circuit providing for the most efficacy ranges from A > D > C > B (in the order of decreasing efficiency). Reference: Lippincott's ANESTHESIA REVIEW: 1,001 QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS Paul Sikka, MD, PhD

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following statements regarding the Mapleson D breathing circuit is FALSE?

AIt has a proximal fresh gas inflow and a distal overflow valve
BWith an inspiratory-to-expiratory (I:E) breathing ratio of 1:2, rebreathing is eliminated with sponta neous ventilation when the fresh gas flow is three times the minute ventilation
CTo eliminate rebreathing, higher fresh gas flows are needed with spontaneous ventilation than with controlled ventilation
DThe Mapleson D circuit is the most widely used of the Mapleson circuits for pediatric anesthesia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. The Mapleson D circuit is the most widely used of the Mapleson circuits for pediatric anesthesia

All of the statements are correct except for C. The Mapleson D system is a semiopen anesthetic system with a proximal fresh gas inflow and a distal overflow valve. To eliminate rebreathing, higher fresh gas flows are needed with spontaneous ventilation than with controlled ventilation. Note the Mapleson A system is a semiopen anesthetic system with a distal fresh gas flow and a proximal overflow valve. The Mapleson F circuit is most widely used for pediatric anesthesia. Reference: Davis: Smith's Anesthesia for Infants and Children, ed 8, pp 294-297.

Sample MCQ #7

A 4-year-old child weighing 20 kg has a minute volume of 3 L/min. If using the Jackson Rees circuit for spontaneous ventilation, how much fresh gas flow should be used for such a patient to prevent rebreathe growth?

A2L/min
B4L/min
C8L/min
D12L/min
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. 8L/min

While using Jackson Rees circuit, Fresh gas flows of 2-3 times minute volume should be used to prevent rebreathing during spontaneous ventilation, (But plz note, whatever be the baby's weight, a minimum flow of 3 L/min should be kept) During controlled ventilation in children, normocapnia can be maintained with a fresh gas flow of 1L + 100 mL/kg/min E.g. a 4-year-old weighing 20 kg would need a total FGF of around 3 L/min. Reference: Objective anesthesia review (Atul Kulkarni) 3rd Ed Pg. 403
