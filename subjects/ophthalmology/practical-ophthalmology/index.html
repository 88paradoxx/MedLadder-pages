Sample MCQ #1

Which optical instrument provides an inverted and reversed image of the retina?

ADirect ophthalmoscope
BIndirect ophthalmoscope with 20 D lens
CHruby lens and slit lamp (10 × objective magnification)
DGoldmann contact lens and slit lamp (10 × objective magnification)
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Indirect ophthalmoscope with 20 D lens
  • Ophthalmic instruments use a combination of external lenses and the cornea/lens system to create an image of the retina.
  • Less magnification permits a larger field of view whereas greater magnification results in less depth of focus but higher resolution of details.
  • The appropriate ophthalmic instrument permits the viewing of different aspects of the retina.

Reference: Khurana Optics 2/e, P. 59

Sample MCQ #2

Identify the instrument used for handling delicate tissues during eye surgery.

AMcPherson forceps
BLim's forceps
CArtery forceps
DSR holding forceps
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Lim's forceps

Explanation:

The instrument shown is Lim's forceps, which is specifically designed to grasp the edges of the cornea or sclera during suturing in ocular surgery. It allows precise handling of delicate tissues without causing trauma.

Explanation for Other Options:

McPherson forceps (Option A): Used to atraumatically hold, retract, or stitch tissues during cataract or glaucoma procedures.

Artery forceps (Option C): Primarily used to clamp blood vessels and control bleeding.

SR holding forceps (Option D): Helps to stabilise the eyeball during cataract surgery.

Sample MCQ #3

Identify the instrument.

AEvisceration spatula
BChalazion clamp
CEnucleation spoon
DBone punch
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Chalazion clamp

Explanation:

The instrument shown is a chalazion clamp, identified by its solid blade designed to be placed externally on the skin. It also has a ring-shaped blade intended to be positioned on the inner conjunctival surface. The handle features a thumbscrew that can be tightened or loosened to apply the necessary pressure. With its forceps-like handle, once the chalazion is properly positioned within the ring, the clamp is tightened and secured in place.

Explanation for other options:

Evisceration spatula (Option A): Used to remove only the intraocular contents, while preserving the sclera and eye muscles intact.

Enucleation spoon (Option C): Used to remove the entire intraocular contents from the eye socket, leaving the eye's lining and muscles intact.

Bone punch (Option D): This instrument is used in bone surgery to remove small pieces of bone, such as in orbital surgeries, but it does not have the ring and blade structure characteristic of a chalazion clamp. It is unrelated to chalazion or conjunctival procedures

Sample MCQ #4

Identify the block:

ANasociliary
BAnterior ethmoidal
CSphenopalatine
DGreater palatine
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Nasociliary

The nasociliary nerve is a branch of the ophthalmic nerve (CN V1) (which is in turn a branch of the trigeminal nerve (CN V)). It is intermediate in size between the other two branches of the ophthalmic nerve, the frontal nerve and lacrimal nerve.

Structure

Course

The nasociliary nerve enters the orbit via the superior orbital fissure,[citation needed] through the common tendinous ring, and between the two heads of the lateral rectus muscle and between the superior and inferior rami of the oculomotor nerve.[citation needed] It passes across the optic nerve (CN II) along with the ophthalmic artery. It then runs obliquely beneath (inferior to) the superior rectus muscle and superior oblique muscle to the medial wall of the orbital cavity whereupon it emits the posterior ethmoidal nerve, and the anterior ethmoidal nerve.

Branches

Branches of the nasociliary nerve include:

posterior ethmoidal nerve

anterior ethmoidal nerve

long ciliary nerves

infratrochlear nerve

communicating branch to ciliary ganglion

Function

The branches of the nasociliary nerve provide sensory innervation to structures surrounding the eye such as the cornea, eyelids, conjunctiva, ethmoid air cells and mucosa of the nasal cavity

Incorrect Options:

Option B. Anterior Ethmoidal
The anterior ethmoidal nerve is a branch of the nasociliary nerve and provides sensory innervation to the anterior part of the nasal cavity and the skin of the tip of the nose (external nasal nerve). It does not supply the entire region anesthetized in a nasociliary nerve block.

Option C. Sphenopalatine
The sphenopalatine ganglion contains parasympathetic fibers and is primarily involved in lacrimation and nasal secretion. A block at this site is used for treating conditions like cluster headaches and trigeminal neuralgia, not for ocular anesthesia.

Option D. Greater Palatine
The greater palatine nerve is a branch of the maxillary nerve (V2) and provides sensory innervation to the hard palate and posterior part of the nasal cavity. It is not involved in orbital or anterior nasal cavity innervation.

Key Takeaway:

The nasociliary nerve (CN V1) supplies sensory innervation to the eye, nasal mucosa, and ethmoid air cells.

Nasociliary nerve block is used for ocular procedures and anesthesia.

Other nerves like anterior ethmoidal, sphenopalatine, and greater palatine have distinct sensory distributions and are not targeted in this block.
