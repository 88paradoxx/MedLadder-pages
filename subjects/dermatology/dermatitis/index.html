Sample Dermatitis MCQs with answers

Sample MCQ #1

Profilaggrin is found in the ______.

AStratum corneum
BStratum granulosum
CStratum spinosum
DStratum basale
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Stratum granulosum

Profilaggrin is found in the keratohyalin granules present in the stratum granulosum. Profilaggrin breaks down to yield individual filaggrin monomers at the transition between the stratum granulosum and stratum corneum. Deficiency of filaggrin: Atopic dermatitis & Ichthyosis vulgaris Reference : Rook's Textbook of Dermatology, 9th Edition, Page Number-7.

Sample MCQ #2

In which of the following conditions Parakeratosis most frequently occurs -

AActinic keratoses
BSeborrheic keratoses
CMolluscum contagiosum
DBasal cell carcinoma
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Actinic keratoses

Normally, nuclei are absent in stratum corneum. Retention of nuclei within the cells of stratum corneum - Parakeratosis. Parakeratosis is seen in - Normally &rarr; Mucous membrane of mouth and vagina. Disorders &rarr; Benign (Psoriasis, Seborrheic dermatitis) Malignant (Actinic keratosis, Squamous cell carcinoma) Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, chapter 3, page 34

Sample MCQ #4

A patient presents with an eczematous rash with yellow greasy scaling. The microorganism most intimately associated with causation is:

AMicrosporum
BStreptococcus
CPropionobacterium
DMalassezia
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Malassezia

Seborrheic Dermatitis Site: areas rich in sebaceous glands, particularly the scalp, face, and body folds. Linked to the interplay of normal skin flora - Malassezia spp, the composition of lipids on the skin surface and individual susceptibility. C/F - folliculocentric erythematous papules and plaques with a fine white scale, and a yellowish crust often described as a greasy scale-crust. Treatment: Topical antifungals: Ketoconazole, Ciclopirox, Selenium sulphide Topical steroids Topical immunomodulators: Tacrolimus, Pimecrolimus Reference: Rooks textbook of Dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 40, page 2

Sample MCQ #5

Which of the following statements about lichenification is NOT true?

AHyperpigmentation
BAtrophy of skin
CHyperlinearity of skin
DThick skin
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Atrophy of skin

Explanation: Lichenification is characterized by thickened, leathery skin, not atrophy. Atrophy refers to thinning of the epidermis or dermis, which is the opposite of what occurs in lichenification. Lichenification occurs due to chronic irritation or scratching, typically seen in: Chronic eczema Lichen simplex chronicus Atopic dermatitis Key features: Thickened skin Hyperpigmentation due to chronic inflammation Hyperlinearity, where skin markings become prominent Dryness and scaling may accompany it

Sample MCQ #7

Nasolabial dyssebacea and neovascularisation of cornea are features seen in deficiency of ?

AVitamin C
BVitamin B1
CVitamin B2
DVitamin B3
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Vitamin B2

Riboflavin, vitamin B2, is a water-soluble and heat-stable vitamin that the body uses to metabolize fats, protein, and carbohydrates into glucose for energy. Clinical deficiency of riboflavin - sore throat, hyperemia and edema of the pharyngeal and oral mucous membranes, cheilosis, angular stomatitis, glossitis, seborrheic dermatitis (dyssebacea), and normochromic, normocytic anemia associated with pure red cell cytoplasia of the bone marrow. Severe riboflavin deficiency can also affect the conversion of vitamin B 6 to its coenzyme and even curtail conversion of tryptophan to niacin. Neovascularisation of cornea can also be seen. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology 9 th edition, chapter 63, page 14

Sample MCQ #8

All of the following statements are true regarding Asteatotic eczema except

AIt usually affects legs, arms and hands with dry skin
BIt is also known as Eczema craquele
CChildren are most commonly affected
DIt is more common in winters
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Children are most commonly affected

Asteatotic eczema, also known as eczema craquel&eacute;, is a common type of pruritic dermatitis. C/F - dry, cracked, and scaling skin that is typically inflamed. Can lead to fissures are a result of epidermal water loss. The fissured skin occurs in a polygonal or curvilinear pattern (crazing pavement sign). typically presents on the anterolateral aspects of the lower legs Asteatotic eczema occurs especially in winter months when the skin is more at risk for becoming dry. Asteatotic eczema is also more common in the elderly. Reference: Rooks textbook of Dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 39, page 10
