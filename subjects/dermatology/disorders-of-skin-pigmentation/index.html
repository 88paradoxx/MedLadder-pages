Sample MCQ #2

Inverted Champagne bottle appearance is seen in

AVaricose veins
BDVT
CLipodermatosclerosis
DVenous ulceration
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Lipodermatosclerosis

Answer- C. Lipodermatosclerosis The most commonly recognized form of lipodermatosclerosis (LDS), chronic LDS presence with induration and hyperpigmentation of the skin involving the one or both of the lower legs in a characteristic "inverted champagne bottle" appearance. Associated with venous insufficiency, LDS is most common in middle-aged women.

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following histological type is most sensitive in cold?

AMeissner's corpuscle
BMerkel's disc
CLangerhans cell
DMelanocyte
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Melanocyte

Melanocytes are very susceptible to thermal injury and may die at temperatures below -5 C. As compared to other cells in the skin, including epidermal keratinocytes, merkel cells, langerhans cells, mesissners corpuscles, contact cooling of the skin surface causes selective loss of epidermal melanocytes when the tissue temperature reaches &minus;5 &deg;C or cooler with an exposure time of 10 minutes or longer, leading to partial skin depigmentation Longer exposures combined with colder temperature exposure led to more complete depigmentation in the treated skin surface. This is a reason why cryosurgery procedures on skin - using liquid nitrogen at -196 degrees Celsius - causes depigmentation as a common side effect Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology 9th edition, chapter 2, page 17

Sample MCQ #6

Which of the following is not used in treatment of melasma?

AKojic acid
BHydroquinone
CAzelaic acid
DTacrolimus
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Tacrolimus

Hydroquinone is the gold standard of topical treatment. It exerts its effect by inhibiting tyrosinase, the rate-limiting enzyme in melanin synthesis. HQ 2%-4% is prominently used as mono-therapy A triple combination of hydroquinone, retinoic acid, and corticosteroids (HQ 4%, tretinoin 0.05%, and fluocinolone acetonide 0.01%) Kligman regimen is commonly used Other depigmenting drugs - azelaic acid, kojic acid, glycolic acid Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 88, page 13
