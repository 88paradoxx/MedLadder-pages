Sample MCQ #1

Correct about stages of LGV is :

AStage 1 : painful genital ulcer
BStage 2 : painless genital ulcer
CStage 3 : inguinal bubo
DStage 2: inguinal bubo
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Stage 2: inguinal bubo

Primary Stage: The patient characteristically develops a painless genital ulcer or papules which are about 1 to 6 mm in size. secondary stage presents with the development of unilateral or bilateral tender inguinal and/or femoral lymphadenopathy (also called buboes), which occurs two to six weeks after the primary stage; this is called the inguinal syndrome. Late stage : necrosis of lymph node when left untreated. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 30, page 17

Sample MCQ #2

You are posted as MO on a rural PHC. A patient comes with painful genital ulcer. You issue a STI kit to him. Which of the following will be provided?

AKit 1
BKit 2
CKit 5
DKit 6
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: C. Kit 5

As a PHC MO, follow the guidelines of syndromic management NACO guidelines. According to it, a painful genital ulcer patient should be given red kit- kit no. 5 It contains tab acyclovir 400mg. Dose : Three tablets a day for 7 days (21 tablets total) Reference: NACO guidelines for Syndromic Management of STI

Sample MCQ #3

Which of the following statements is false about disseminated gonococcal infection?

AIt occurs as a result of direct spread by ascending infections
BClassic presentations is with a dermatitis - arthritis syndrome
CSkin lesions are small, tender and maculopapular
DIt is a common complication
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. It is a common complication

DGI occurs when the sexually transmitted pathogen Neisseria gonorrhoeae invades the bloodstream and spreads to distant sites in the body, leading to clinical findings such as septic arthritis, polyarthralgia, tenosynovitis, petechial/pustular skin lesions, bacteremia, or, on rare occasions, endocarditis or meningitis. The classic triad of features consists of dermatitis, tenosynovitis and migratory polyarthritis DGI is uncommon and thought to occur in 0.5-3% of untreated gonorrhea cases. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, chapter 30, page 5

Sample MCQ #6

Pseudo bubo formation is seen in :

AGranuloma inguinale
BLGV
CChancroid
DHerpes
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: A. Granuloma inguinale

Granuloma Inguinale Genital ulcerative disease caused by the intracellular gram-negative bacterium Klebsiella granulomatis (formerly known' as Calymmatobacterium granulomatis) Painless, slowly progressive ulcerative lesions on the genitals or perineum without regional lymphadenopathy, subcutaneous granulomas (Pseudo buboes) Lesions are highly vascular (beefy red appearance) and bleed on touch Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 30, page 25

Sample MCQ #7

Secondary stage of LGV is characterised by :

AInguinal syndrome
BOral syndrome
CAnorectal syndrome
DAny of the above
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: D. Any of the above

The secondary stage presents with the development of unilateral or bilateral tender inguinal and/or femoral lymphadenopathy (also called buboes), which occurs two to six weeks after the primary stage; this is called the inguinal syndrome. An anorectal syndrome also presents which is characterized by proctitis or proctocolitis-like symptoms. Pain during urination, rectal bleeding, pain during passing stools, abdominal pain, anal pain, tenesmus. Generalized symptoms like body aches, headache, and fever can occur during this stage. This syndrome usually occurs when the transmission is via the anal route. An oral syndrome can occur in people get LGV through the oral route. Cervical lymphadenopathy can occur. There are also reports of systemic complications like pneumonia and hepatitis. Reference: Rooks textbook of dermatology, 9 th edition, chapter 30, page 18

Sample MCQ #8

Which of the following is not considered as a sexually transmitted infection?

ASyphilis
BBacterial vaginosis
CGonorrhoea
DChancroid
Show Answer & Explanation ▾
✓ Correct Answer: B. Bacterial vaginosis

Bacterial vaginosis is not considered as an STI. It is usually caused by imbalance between aerobic and anaerobic flora of the vagina. However it is mostly seen in sexually active women, with new partner or multiple partners. It is rare in virgins. REFERENCE: Rooks textbook of dermatology, chapter 31
